\section{NP-hardness of approximation}
\label{sec:np-hardness}

We prove NP-hardness below $18/17$ for treedepth and below $12/11$
for treewidth, using a shared tripartite source construction. A
separate reduction from cubic Independent Set gives a constant
approximation gap for total elimination depth.

\subsection{Treedepth}
\label{subsec:np-treedepth}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:np-hardness}
For every fixed real $1\le r<18/17$, approximating treedepth within
factor $r$ on simple unweighted graphs is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}

We prove the theorem by encoding parity equations in a tripartite
graph, then converting its vertex-cover number into treedepth.

The truth blocks and assignment witnesses follow Bonnet, Neuen, and
Soko\l owski~\cite[Section~4 and Lemma~10]{BNS}, building on Bonnet's
treewidth construction~\cite[Section~3.1]{BonnetTreewidth}. The anchor graph
is the one in~\cite[Lemma~6]{BNS}. We modify the witness gadgets
for parity equations and use the anchor construction through the
corollary below. Appendix~\ref{subsec:np-provenance} records which
ingredients are inherited, and Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-proof} gives
the corollary's derivation.

\subsubsection{The source gap and normalization}

Let $F$ be an unweighted system of $m\ge1$ equations of the form
$x+y+z=a\pmod2$, where $a\in\{0,1\}$ and $x,y,z$ are three distinct
Boolean variables. Equations may be
repeated, and $\OPT(F)$ denotes the maximum number simultaneously
satisfied, counting repetitions. Write $a_j$ for the right-hand side
of equation $j$ and $d_x$ for the number of
equations containing $x$. We use H{\aa}stad's Max-3-LIN-$2$
gap~\cite{Hastad1997}, in the distinct-variable,
bounded-occurrence form stated by Hazan, Safra, and Schwartz.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-occurrence Max-3-LIN-2 gap {\cite[Theorem~10]{HazanSafraSchwartz2003}}]\label{thm:xor-source}
For every fixed $0<\epsilon<1/4$, it is NP-hard to distinguish unweighted systems
$F$ with $\OPT(F)\ge(1-\epsilon)m$ (YES) from those with
$\OPT(F)\le(1/2+\epsilon)m$ (NO).
The hardness holds when every equation contains three distinct
variables and each variable occurs a constant number of times, with
at most two possible occurrence counts. It also holds under the
following normalizations:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xor-normalization}
 d_x\in6\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
 \sum_xd_x=3m,\qquad
 |\{j:a_j=0\}|=|\{j:a_j=1\}|=m/2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The additional conditions are obtained by pairing the source with a
complemented copy and repeating each equation six times. Appendix~\ref{app:xor-normalization}
derives these conditions. We henceforth
assume~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}. Divisibility makes the truth-block
sizes integral, and the balance between right-hand sides will make
the three graph parts equal.

\subsubsection{A tripartite graph with equal parts}

We construct a tripartite graph $G_F$ with independent parts
$A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\mathbin{\dot\cup}C$. Its vertices have two
roles: vertices in \emph{truth blocks} encode values of individual
variables, and \emph{assignment witnesses} represent satisfying
assignments to the three variables of an equation. All blocks are
pairwise disjoint, every witness is a new vertex, and the parts
contain the vertices assigned below.
For every variable $x$, create independent truth blocks
$T_x^0\subseteq A$ and $T_x^1\subseteq B$, each of size $5d_x/6$,
a positive integer by~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}. Put all edges between these two blocks. Selecting
$T_x^a$ in an independent set will encode the assignment $x=a$. The
total size of one block per variable is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xor-truth-budget}
 \frac56\sum_xd_x=\frac{5m}{2}.
\end{equation}

For each equation $j$, create four witnesses, one for each of its
satisfying assignments. If its right-hand side is zero, call the
witness for assignment $000$ special and place it in $A$. If its
right-hand side is one, call the witness for assignment $111$ special
and place it in $B$. Place the other three witnesses in $C$. Join the
special witness to the other three witnesses of its equation, forming
a star. We call these four vertices and their star edges the
\emph{equation gadget}. These edges limit an independent set to at most three witnesses
of an equation, even if it selects no truth block for any of that
equation's variables.

For every witness representing a satisfying assignment $\sigma$ and
every variable $x$ of its equation, join it to the entire truth block
$T_x^{1-\sigma(x)}$. Add no other edges. In particular, witnesses
from distinct equations have no edges between them.

Every listed edge respects the tripartition. A special $000$ witness
lies in $A$ and its truth-block neighbors all lie in $B$. A special
$111$ witness lies in $B$ and its truth-block neighbors all lie in
$A$. The three remaining witnesses lie in $C$, and their truth-block
neighbors lie in $A\cup B$. Star edges join the special witness to
$C$. Thus $G_F$ is simple, unweighted, and tripartite.

The truth vertices number $5m$, and there are $4m$ witnesses, so
$G_F$ has $9m$ vertices. Equation~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}
gives $m/2$ special witnesses in each of $A$ and $B$, and three
witnesses per equation in $C$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xor-equal-parts}
 |A|=|B|=|C|=3m.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{The independent-set optimum}

An optimal assignment gives an independent set of size $\frac{5m}{2}+\OPT(F)$.
We show that omitting truth blocks cannot improve this value.

For an independent set $I$ containing every truth block that it
meets, the selected block $T_x^a$ fixes $x=a$. Variables with neither
block selected remain unassigned. Let $U$ be the unassigned variables
and put $D_U=\sum_{x\in U}d_x$. Complete this partial assignment by
choosing each unassigned variable independently and uniformly from
$\{0,1\}$. For equation $j$, let $r_j$ count its unassigned variables,
let $w_j$ be the number of the four witness vertices in equation $j$'s
gadget that belong to $I$, and let $p_j$ be the probability that
equation $j$ is satisfied by this completion.

\begin{lemma}[Witnesses under a partial assignment]
\label{lem:xor-witness-excess}
With this notation, $w_j-p_j\le5r_j/6$ for every equation $j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every selected witness agrees with the fixed variables, since
otherwise it has a neighbor in a selected truth block.
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
 \item If $r_j=0$, all three variables are fixed. When the equation
 is satisfied, $p_j=1$ and at most its unique consistent witness
 can be selected. Otherwise $p_j=0$ and none can be selected.
 Thus $w_j-p_j\leq0=5r_j/6$.
 \item If $1\leq r_j\leq3$, half of the $2^{r_j}$ completions
 satisfy the equation. Hence $p_j=1/2$ and there are
 $2^{r_j-1}$ consistent satisfying witnesses. The witness star
 has independence number three, so
 $w_j-p_j\leq\min\{2^{r_j-1},3\}-1/2$.
 For $r_j=1,2,3$ this bound is $1/2,3/2,5/2$, respectively.
 These values are at most $5r_j/6$, since
 $1/2\leq5/6$, $3/2\leq10/6$, and $5/2=15/6$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:xor-cover}
The graph $G_F$ satisfies $\alphaI(G_F)=5m/2+\OPT(F)$ and
$\vc(G_F)=13m/2-\OPT(F)$.
\end{lemma}


\begin{proof}
Start with any independent set $I$, and fill every truth block that
it meets. This preserves independence because a block has no internal
edges and its vertices have identical neighborhoods. The filled set
is no smaller than the original. From now on, write $I$ for the filled set. The
two nonempty blocks of a variable are joined completely, so at most one is selected.
A selected block $T_x^a$ fixes $x=a$. A variable with neither block
selected remains unassigned.
The selected truth vertices number $\frac{5m}{2}-5D_U/6$. Use the random
completion and the quantities $r_j,w_j,p_j$ defined above.
Lemma~\ref{lem:xor-witness-excess} gives $w_j-p_j\le5r_j/6$. Also, $\sum_jr_j=D_U$,
as both sides count occurrences of unassigned variables. The random
completion satisfies an expected $\sum_jp_j$ equations by linearity
of expectation. Every completion satisfies at most $\OPT(F)$ equations,
so $\sum_jp_j\le\OPT(F)$. It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |I|&=\frac{5m}{2}-\frac56D_U+\sum_jw_j
 \le\frac{5m}{2}-\frac56D_U+\sum_jp_j+\frac56\sum_jr_j\\
 &=\frac{5m}{2}-\frac56D_U+\sum_jp_j+\frac56D_U
 =\frac{5m}{2}+\sum_jp_j\le\frac{5m}{2}+\OPT(F).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since filling does not decrease the size, this bound also holds for
the independent set before its truth blocks were filled.

For the reverse bound, take an assignment satisfying $\OPT(F)$
equations. Select its truth blocks, of total size $\frac{5m}{2}$, and, for each
satisfied equation, its unique witness with that assignment. Such a
witness has no neighbor in the selected truth blocks. Only one witness
is selected per equation, so no star edge has both endpoints selected.
There are no edges between distinct equation gadgets. The resulting
independent set has size $\frac{5m}{2}+\OPT(F)$.

Using~\eqref{eq:xor-truth-budget} proves the independent-set identity.
The graph has $9m$ vertices, so complementation gives the stated
vertex-cover identity.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The anchor graph}
\label{subsubsec:anchor-graph}

Let $G$ be tripartite with specified independent parts $A,B,C$, and
let $\ell\ge1$ be an integer. Add pairwise disjoint sets
$K_A,K_B,K_C$ of size $\ell$ and distinct vertices $z_A,z_B,z_C$,
with all added vertices distinct and outside $V(G)$.
The graph $H_\ell(G)$ has vertex set
$V(G)\cup K_A\cup K_B\cup K_C\cup\{z_A,z_B,z_C\}$ and edge set
\[
 E(H_\ell(G))=E(G)\cup
 \bigcup_{X\in\{A,B,C\}}
 \left(\binom{K_X}{2}\cup
       \{\{u,v\}:u\in K_X,\ v\in X\cup\{z_X\}\}\right).
\]
Thus $K_X$ is a clique joined completely to $X\cup\{z_X\}$, and
there are no other new edges. We call $K_X$ the \emph{anchor clique}
of part $X$. The vertex $z_X$ is associated with that part. This is
the anchor graph of~\cite[Lemma~6]{BNS} and has $|V(G)|+3\ell+3$ vertices.

\begin{corollary}[Anchors as long as the largest part]\label{cor:max-part-anchor}
If $G$ is tripartite with nonempty specified independent parts $A,B,C$ and
$\ell\ge\max\{|A|,|B|,|C|\}$, then
$\td(H_\ell(G))=\ell+\vc(G)+1$.
\end{corollary}

We derive this from the anchor construction in Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-proof},
including the short-anchor analysis.
Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski require $\ell\geq\vc(G)$ for their
identity~\cite[Lemma~6]{BNS}. Our corollary only requires $\ell$ to be at
least the largest part size, so it applies with $\ell=3m$ even though
$\vc(G_F)\geq11m/2$.

\subsubsection{The treedepth identity and the hardness gap}

Taking $\ell=3m$ gives the following identity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:xor-treedepth-identity}
For every normalized source system $F$, the graph $H=H_{3m}(G_F)$
has $18m+3$ vertices and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xor-treedepth-identity}
 \td(H)=\frac{19m}{2}-\OPT(F)+1.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each source part has size $3m$ by~\eqref{eq:xor-equal-parts}, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:max-part-anchor} and Lemma~\ref{lem:xor-cover} give
$\td(H)=3m+\vc(G_F)+1=19m/2-\OPT(F)+1$.
The graph has $9m+9m+3=18m+3$ vertices and $O(m^2)$ edges.
Repeated equations have distinct witness gadgets, so no parallel
edges arise. The source normalization changes size by a constant
factor, giving a polynomial construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:np-hardness}]
The leading NO and YES coefficients below approach $9$ and $17/2$
as the source error decreases, giving ratio $18/17$.
Fix $1\le r<18/17$. Since $9-r(17/2)>0$, choose a fixed rational
$0<\epsilon<1/4$ such that $9-\epsilon>r(17/2+\epsilon)$.
Put $\gamma=9-\epsilon-r(17/2+\epsilon)>0$. Take enough disjoint
copies of $F$ on disjoint variable sets that $\gamma m>r-1$.
This multiplies $m$ and $\OPT(F)$ by the same integer, preserving
the source promise and~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:xor-treedepth-identity} and the source
promise, the two cases satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\textup{YES: }\td(H)
 &\le19m/2-(1-\epsilon)m+1
   =(17/2+\epsilon)m+1,\\
\textup{NO: }\td(H)
 &\ge19m/2-(1/2+\epsilon)m+1
   =(9-\epsilon)m+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The NO lower bound minus $r$ times the YES upper bound is
$\gamma m+1-r>0$, proving the gap.
\end{proof}
