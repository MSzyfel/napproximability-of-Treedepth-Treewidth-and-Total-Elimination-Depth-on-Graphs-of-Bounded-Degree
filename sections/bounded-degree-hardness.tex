\section{Hardness on graphs of bounded degree}
\label{sec:bounded-degree-hardness}

\subsection{The degree-reduction approach}

Let $H$ be one of the dense graphs constructed above, with
$N=|V(H)|$. Its edge set consists of $E_0\subseteq\binom{V(H)}2$,
which we keep during sparsification, and clique and biclique factors
indexed by finite disjoint sets $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal B$.
Each clique factor $f\in\mathcal C$ has a nonempty support
$U_f\subseteq V(H)$ and contributes
all pairs in $\binom{U_f}{2}$. Each biclique factor has disjoint nonempty sides
$A_f,B_f\subseteq V(H)$, support $U_f=A_f\mathbin{\dot\cup}B_f$, and contributes
all edges $uv$ with $u\in A_f$ and $v\in B_f$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-edge-union}
 E(H)=E_0\cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C}\binom{U_f}{2}
       \cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal B}\{uv:u\in A_f,\ v\in B_f\}.
\end{equation}
For each factor choose an integer threshold $t_f\ge1$. Two
disjoint vertex sets are \emph{anticomplete} if no edge joins them.
For an integer $t\ge1$, a \emph{$(t,t)$ hole} is a pair of
anticomplete sets of $t$ vertices each. In a bipartite graph with specified sides, the two sets lie
on opposite sides. A clique replacement on $U_f$ has no hole at threshold $t_f$
if every two disjoint $t_f$-subsets of $U_f$ have at least one edge
between them. A biclique replacement on
$A_f\mathbin{\dot\cup}B_f$ has no hole at threshold $t_f$ if every
$t_f$-subset of $A_f$ and every $t_f$-subset of $B_f$ have at least
one edge between them.

Different factors may share vertices or edges. A factor specifies
edges to be included, not an induced subgraph, so other factors or
$E_0$ may add further edges on its support.

Replace each factor by a subgraph of its specified edge set on
the same support. Let $J$ be the union of these replacement edges
with $E_0$. Then $J$ is a spanning subgraph of $H$. The construction
has two steps.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Preserve the YES bound by deleting edges.}
   Every elimination forest or tree decomposition of $H$ is also
   valid for $J$. Thus treedepth, treewidth, and total elimination
   depth do not increase.
 \item \emph{Recover a dense solution from a sparse one.}
   Each replacement satisfies its no-hole condition at threshold
   $t_f$. For clique factors the subsets are disjoint, and for
   biclique factors they lie on opposite sides.
   The completion lemmas show that, after deleting a small set $D$
   of exceptional vertices, an arbitrary forest or decomposition
   of $J$ has every edge of $H-D$ represented by comparable
   endpoints or by vertices in a common bag. For forests,
   restore $D$ as a chain above all existing roots. For tree
   decompositions, add $D$ to every bag. The height or width
    increases by at most $|D|$, and the total depth by at most
    $N|D|$.
\end{enumerate}

These steps give a sparse instance and bound the additive
loss incurred when recovering a dense solution. The following lemma
states when such a loss preserves the approximation gap.

\begin{lemma}[Preservation of approximation gaps]
\label{lem:gap-preservation}
Let a minimization promise problem be hard to distinguish between
$\OPT(I)\le Y(N)$ in the YES case and $\OPT(I)\ge r'Y(N)$ in the
NO case, where $r'>1$, $Y(N)>0$, and $N$ is the source size.
Assume the bounds below admit a polynomial-time computable separating
threshold whenever they are strictly separated.
Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time reduction maps each source
instance $I$ to a target minimization instance $I'$. Assume that for
every fixed pair $\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}}>0$ we
can choose the reduction's fixed parameters and a size threshold
$N_0$ so that, on promised instances with $N\ge N_0$,
\[
 \OPT(I')\le(1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}})Y(N)\quad(\text{YES}),\qquad
 \OPT(I')\ge(r'-\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}})Y(N)\quad(\text{NO}).
\]
Also assume that the source promise remains hard after any fixed size
threshold. Then every fixed $1\le r<r'$ remains hard after the
reduction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose positive $\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}}$
so that $\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}}+r\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}}<r'-r$.
Then $r(1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}})<r'-\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}}$, so
an $r$-approximation returning a feasible solution has value at most
$r(1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{YES}})Y(N)$ in the YES case and at least
$(r'-\epsilon_{\mathrm{NO}})Y(N)$ in the NO case. Comparing its
value with the supplied computable threshold distinguishes the cases
on sufficiently large instances.
\end{proof}

\emph{Keep the total loss below the hardness gap.}
Choose $t_f=\lceil\zeta|U_f|\rceil$, so that
$t_f-1<\zeta|U_f|$ for each clique or biclique factor.
If every vertex lies in at most a fixed number
of factor supports, then
$\sum_{f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B}|U_f|=O(N)$. Hence
$|D|\le\varepsilon N$ for any prescribed fixed
$\varepsilon>0$, by choosing the fixed positive $\zeta$ sufficiently small.
The resulting losses are at most $\varepsilon N$ for treedepth
and treewidth, and $\varepsilon N^2$ for total depth. Given a
target factor $r$ below the dense threshold, choose a dense factor
$r'>r$. The YES bound $Y$ has the same linear or quadratic scale
as the corresponding loss, so the threshold fractions can make
the loss at most $\delta Y$ for any fixed $\delta>0$. Taking
$\delta<(r'-r)/(r+1)$ lets Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation}
preserve factor $r$.

For the maximum degree to be bounded, we also require bounded degree
in $E_0$. If a biclique has a side smaller than its threshold, its
no-hole condition is vacuous and we may use the empty replacement.
Otherwise a threshold proportional to its total support size bounds
the ratio of its side sizes. This ratio bounds the number of auxiliary
vertices mapped to each prescribed vertex. The expander construction then
gives the required no-hole condition with a constant degree
contribution from each factor. Since each vertex lies in a bounded number of
factor supports, its total degree is bounded. These constants may
depend on the desired approximation ratio, but not on $N$. For
treewidth, each
side of every biclique must additionally be partitionable into a
bounded number of sets, each contained in a clique-factor support.
The corresponding clique-factor bags anchor the side sets in the
biclique completion lemma.
For the UGC applications, the bounded-degree source promise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-degree-kr-source} bounds the degree of
the retained source edges $E_0$.

\subsection{Completing sparse factors}

The no-hole conditions above are inherited by deleting vertices. Recall that
forest restriction preserves the ancestor order between surviving
vertices and never increases their depths.

For an elimination forest $F$ of $J$, traverse its trees one after
another in depth-first order. Introduce a vertex on arrival and forget it after
its child subtrees. Give all events distinct integer times. For each
$v\in V(F)$, let $t_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ and $t_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ be its
introduction and forgetting times, and put
$I_v=[t_{\mathrm{in}}(v),t_{\mathrm{out}}(v)]$. These intervals are
nested for comparable vertices and disjoint for incomparable ones.
The introduced but unforgotten vertices form the current ancestor
path. A forgotten vertex and an unintroduced vertex have disjoint
intervals, so they are incomparable; in an elimination forest of $J$,
no edge of $J$ joins them. We use these same traversal facts for
clique and biclique completion.

The following lemmas identify a small exceptional set whose deletion
allows a sparse factor to be completed. For a clique, the surviving
vertices must lie on one ancestor path or in one bag. For a biclique,
it is enough that every surviving cross-pair is comparable or appears
together in a bag.

\subsubsection{Clique factors}

\begin{lemma}[Clique completion in an elimination forest]\label{lem:sparse-clique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph, let $U\subseteq V(J)$, and let
$t\ge1$ be an integer. Suppose $J[U]$ has no two disjoint
anticomplete sets of size $t$. Every elimination forest $F$ of $J$ has an
ancestor path $P$ of $F$ such that
$|U\setminus V(P)|\leq2(t-1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $|U|<t$, then $|U|\leq t-1\leq2(t-1)$, so the empty path
satisfies the claim. Now assume $|U|\geq t$. Traverse each rooted
tree as above, and let $v$ be the $t$th vertex of $U$ to be forgotten.
Immediately before forgetting $v$, let $P$ be the current ancestor
path, and let $L,R\subseteq U$ consist of the already forgotten and
not yet introduced vertices, respectively. Then $|L|=t-1$, and
every vertex of $U\setminus(L\cup R)$ lies on $P$.
Immediately after this event, $L\cup\{v\}$ is anticomplete to the
unchanged set $R$, by the traversal facts above. Since
$|L\cup\{v\}|=t$, the no-hole condition gives $|R|\le t-1$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clique-forest-sweep}
 U\setminus V(P)\subseteq L\cup R,\qquad |L|=t-1,\qquad |R|\leq t-1.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 |U\setminus V(P)|
 \overset{\eqref{eq:clique-forest-sweep}}{\leq}|L|+|R|
 \overset{\eqref{eq:clique-forest-sweep}}{\leq}(t-1)+(t-1)=2(t-1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clique completion in a tree decomposition]
\label{lem:tw-clique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph, let $U\subseteq V(J)$, and let
$t\ge1$ be an integer. Suppose $J[U]$ has no two disjoint
anticomplete sets of size $t$. Every tree decomposition of $J$ has a node $u$ whose bag
$B_u$ satisfies $|U\setminus B_u|\leq3(t-1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ be the supplied decomposition.
If $|U|<t$, choose any node $u$ of $T$. Then
$|U\setminus B_u|\leq|U|\leq t-1\leq3(t-1)$. Otherwise consider an edge of the
decomposition tree. Its deletion leaves two sides. Call a side
heavy if it contains $T_v$ for at least $t$ vertices $v\in U$.
If both sides were heavy, choose a set $X\subseteq U$ of exactly $t$
vertices with $T_v$ contained in one side, and a set
$Y\subseteq U$ of exactly $t$ vertices with $T_v$ contained in
the other side. The sets are disjoint, since no nonempty $T_v$ can
lie on both sides, so they contain $2t$ vertices in total. For
$x\in X$ and $y\in Y$, we have $T_x\cap T_y=\varnothing$, so no bag
contains both $x$ and $y$. Thus $X$ and $Y$ are anticomplete,
contrary to the hypothesis. Orient the edge toward its
heavy side if one exists, and arbitrarily if neither is heavy.
Following outgoing edges cannot revisit a node, since the underlying
tree has no cycles. The walk therefore ends at a node with no outgoing
edge. Call such a node a \emph{sink}, and let
$u$ be one.

For each component $C$ of $T-u$, at most $t-1$ vertices $v\in U$
have $T_v\subseteq V(C)$. Otherwise, the edge from $u$ into $C$ would be
oriented away from the sink $u$. Let $W=U\setminus B_u$. Every
vertex $v\in W$ has $T_v$ contained in one component of $T-u$.
Write $W_C=\{v\in W:T_v\subseteq V(C)\}$. Thus $|W_C|\leq t-1$ for
each component $C$. If $t=1$, this gives $W=\varnothing$. For
$t\geq2$, suppose $|W|\geq3t-2$. Select components one at a time
until the sum of their $|W_C|$ first reaches $t$, and let $W_1$ be
the union of their $W_C$ sets. The last component added contributes at most $t-1$
vertices, so, together with the assumed lower bound on $|W|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tw-clique-group-sizes}
 t\leq|W_1|\leq2t-2,\qquad |W|\geq3t-2.
\end{equation}
Let $W_2=W\setminus W_1$. Then
$|W_2|\overset{\eqref{eq:tw-clique-group-sizes}}{\geq}(3t-2)-(2t-2)=t$.
For $x\in W_1$ and $y\in W_2$,
the sets $T_x,T_y$ lie in different components of $T-u$ and are
therefore disjoint, so no bag contains both $x$ and $y$. Hence $W_1,W_2$ are anticomplete in
$J$, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus $|W|\leq3t-3$.
Since $W=U\setminus B_u$ and $3t-3=3(t-1)$, this is the required bound.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Biclique factors}

\begin{theorem}[K\"onig's theorem
{\cite[Theorem~2.1.1 and Section~2.1]{Diestel2017}}]
\label{thm:konig}
In a finite bipartite graph with parts $A,B$, the maximum matching
size equals the minimum vertex-cover size. A maximum matching can
be found in polynomial time. Given such a matching, start at the
unmatched vertices of $A$ and traverse unmatched edges toward $B$
and matched edges toward $A$. If $Z$ is the set of vertices reached,
then $(A\setminus Z)\cup(B\cap Z)$ is a minimum vertex cover.
\end{theorem}

Both biclique completion lemmas use the following interval argument.

\begin{lemma}[Interval completion]\label{lem:interval-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph, let $A,B\subseteq V(J)$ be disjoint,
and let $t\ge1$ be an integer. Assign each $v\in A\cup B$ a nonempty interval
$I_v$ in a common finite linear order. Suppose every edge of $J$
between $A$ and $B$ has intersecting endpoint intervals, and the
bipartite graph between $A$ and $B$ has no $(t,t)$ hole.
There is a set $D\subseteq A\cup B$ with $|D|\le4(t-1)$ such that
$I_u\cap I_v\ne\varnothing$ for all $u\in A\setminus D$ and
$v\in B\setminus D$. If every disjoint cross-pair has its $A$-interval
before its $B$-interval, the bound is $|D|\le2(t-1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be the bipartite graph on $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ whose edges
are the pairs with disjoint intervals. Partition its edges according
to which endpoint interval comes first.
Suppose a matching contains $m_0\ge2t-1$ edges $u_iv_i$, $i\in[m_0]$, with
$u_i\in A$, $v_i\in B$, and the $u_i$-interval first. Order these
edges by the last point of $I_{u_i}$. For $i\in[t]$ and
$j\in\{m_0-t+1,\ldots,m_0\}$, we have $i\le j$ and
\[
 \max I_{u_i}\le\max I_{u_j}<\min I_{v_j}.
\]
Thus $\{u_1,\ldots,u_t\}$ and
$\{v_{m_0-t+1},\ldots,v_{m_0}\}$ are anticomplete $t$-sets in $J$:
their vertices are distinct because the edges form a matching, and
their intervals are disjoint. This contradicts the no-hole condition.
Interchanging $A,B$ gives the same bound for the other orientation.
Every matching in $K$ therefore has at most $4t-4$ edges, or at most
$2t-2$ when only the first orientation occurs. Theorem~\ref{thm:konig}
gives a vertex cover $D$ of $K$ with the corresponding size bound.
No surviving cross-pair is an edge of $K$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

To compute the deletion set, enumerate the disjoint-interval pairs
to construct $K$, then apply Theorem~\ref{thm:konig} to find its
minimum vertex cover $D$ in polynomial time.

\begin{lemma}[Biclique completion in an elimination forest]\label{lem:sparse-biclique-completion}
Let $F$ be an elimination forest of a finite simple graph $J$,
let $A,B\subseteq V(J)$ be disjoint, and let $t\ge1$ be an integer.
Suppose the bipartite graph of $J$ between $A$ and $B$ has no
$(t,t)$ hole. There is a set $D\subseteq A\cup B$ such that
$|D|\leq4(t-1)$ and each pair in
$(A\setminus D)\times(B\setminus D)$ is comparable in $F$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the depth-first intervals $I_v$ defined above. They intersect
exactly when their vertices are comparable in $F$.
Every edge of $J$ therefore has intersecting endpoint intervals, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-completion} gives $D\subseteq A\cup B$ with
$|D|\le4(t-1)$ and intersecting intervals for every surviving
cross-pair. Such pairs are therefore comparable in $F$.
\end{proof}

For treewidth, we first complete the clique factors and use their
bags to anchor the two sides of each biclique.

\begin{lemma}[Intersections along a tree path]
\label{lem:subtree-path-intersection}
Let $S_1,S_2$ be connected vertex sets in a tree, with $x\in S_1$
and $y\in S_2$, and let $P$ be the path from $x$ to $y$.
Then $S_1,S_2$ intersect if and only if their intersections with $P$
intersect.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $z\in S_1\cap S_2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:three-tree-paths} gives a
vertex $p$ common to the $x$--$y$, $x$--$z$, and $y$--$z$ paths.
Connectedness gives
$p\in S_1\cap S_2\cap V(P)$. The reverse implication is immediate.
\end{proof}

When vertices are deleted from a graph, we restrict its tree
decomposition by removing them from every bag.

\begin{lemma}[Biclique completion between two bags]
\label{lem:tw-biclique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph and let $t\ge1$ be an integer.
Fix a tree decomposition $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ of $J$.
Suppose $A,B\subseteq V(J)$ are disjoint, $A\subseteq B_x$ and
$B\subseteq B_y$ for some $x,y\in V(T)$, and the
bipartite graph of $J$ between $A$ and $B$ has no $(t,t)$ hole.
There is a set $D\subseteq A\cup B$ with
$|D|\leq2(t-1)$ such that
for every $u\in A\setminus D$ and $v\in B\setminus D$, some
$z\in V(T)$ satisfies $\{u,v\}\subseteq B_z\setminus D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x=y$, take $D=\varnothing$. Otherwise let $P$ be the unique
path from $x$ to $y$ in $T$, and list its vertices as
$z_0=x,z_1,\ldots,z_\ell=y$ in their order along the path.
Give $V(P)$ the linear order induced by this list. For $u\in A$,
we have $x\in T_u$, and $T_u$ is connected. Thus
$T_u\cap V(P)=\{z_0,\ldots,z_a\}$ for some $0\leq a\leq\ell$,
the vertex set of a nonempty prefix of $P$. Similarly, for $v\in B$,
$T_v\cap V(P)=\{z_c,\ldots,z_\ell\}$ for some $0\leq c\leq\ell$,
the vertex set of a suffix ending at $y$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:subtree-path-intersection}, $T_u,T_v$ intersect if and only if these
path intervals intersect.

Every edge of $J$ between $A$ and $B$ has intersecting traces and
hence intersecting path intervals. A disjoint prefix and suffix
always have the prefix first. Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-completion}
therefore gives $D\subseteq A\cup B$ with $|D|\le2(t-1)$ such that
every surviving cross-pair has intersecting path intervals. Choose
a node $z$ in this intersection; then
$\{u,v\}\subseteq B_z\setminus D$, as required.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Comparison bounds}

\begin{definition}[Graphs and replacement conditions]
\label{def:factor-comparison}
Let $H$ be a finite simple graph whose edges are specified
by~\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union}. For each
$f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B$, fix an integer $t_f\ge1$.
Let $J\subseteq H$ be a spanning subgraph with $E_0\subseteq E(J)$.
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For each $f\in\mathcal C$, every two disjoint sets
   $X,Y\subseteq U_f$ with $|X|=|Y|=t_f$ have an edge of $J$
   between them.
 \item For each $f\in\mathcal B$, every $X\subseteq A_f$ and
   $Y\subseteq B_f$ with $|X|=|Y|=t_f$ have an edge of $J$
   between them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Each completion lemma deletes a small set so that a solution for
$J$ also covers one dense factor. Take the union $D$ of these sets.
Further deletions preserve each completion, so restricting the
solution gives one for $H-D$. Restoring $D$ above forest roots or
in every bag increases height or width by at most $|D|$, and total
depth by at most $N|D|$, where $N=|V(H)|$.

For treewidth, we first choose a deletion set $D_{\mathcal C}$
for the clique factors, so each $U_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ lies
in a bag. If every biclique side lies in a clique support, these
bags contain $A_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$, as required by the biclique
completion lemma. Summing the deletion bounds gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}
 \operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}=2\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+4\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}=3\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+2\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Treedepth comparison]\label{lem:factor-sparsification}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}, and let $\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$
be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}.
Every elimination forest $F$ of $J$ can be
completed to an elimination forest $\widehat F$ of $H$ with
$h(\widehat F)\le h(F)+\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$. In particular,
$\td(J)\le\td(H)\le\td(J)+\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an elimination forest $F$ of $J$. For each $f\in\mathcal C$,
choose the path $P_f$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-clique-completion}
and put $D_f=U_f\setminus V(P_f)$, so $|D_f|\le2(t_f-1)$.
For each $f\in\mathcal B$, let $D_f\subseteq A_f\cup B_f$ be
the set from Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-biclique-completion}, with
$|D_f|\le4(t_f-1)$. Set
$D=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B}D_f$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest-factor-exception-bound}
|D|\le\sum_{f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B}|D_f| \overset{\text{Lems.~\ref{lem:sparse-clique-completion}, \ref{lem:sparse-biclique-completion}}}{\leq}
 2\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+4\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1)
 \overset{\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}}{=}\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}.
\end{equation}

Restrict $F$ to a forest $F'$ on $V(H)\setminus D$.
The surviving vertices of each clique factor lie on $P_f$, and
the surviving cross-pairs of each biclique are comparable by the
choice of $D_f$. Retained edges are covered because $E_0\subseteq
E(J)$. Restriction preserves these ancestor relations, so
\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union} makes $F'$ an elimination forest of
$H-D$ with $h(F')\le h(F)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:restore-vertices},
restoring $D$ gives an elimination forest $\widehat F$ of $H$ with
\[
 h(\widehat F)
 \overset{\text{Lem.~\ref{lem:restore-vertices}}}{\leq}h(F)+|D|
 \overset{\eqref{eq:forest-factor-exception-bound}}{\leq}
 h(F)+\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}.
\]
Choosing $F$ of minimum height proves
$\td(H)\le\td(J)+\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$. The other comparison follows from
$J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Total-depth comparison]
\label{lem:total-depth-comparison}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}. Put $N=|V(H)|$, and let
$\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$ be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}.
Every elimination
forest $F$ of $J$ can be completed to an elimination forest
$\widehat F$ of $H$ with
$\totd(\widehat F)\le\totd(F)+N\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$.
In particular,
$\totd(J)\le \totd(H)\le\totd(J)+N\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an elimination forest $F$ of $J$, and use the exceptional set $D$
and restricted forest $F'$ from the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-sparsification}.
Put $d=|D|\le\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}$. Restriction does not
increase total depth, so $\totd(F')\le\totd(F)$. Restoring $D$
above all roots adds $d$ to the depths of the $N-d$ surviving
vertices and gives each of the $d$ new vertices depth at most $d$.
Consequently,
\[
 \totd(\widehat F)
 \overset{\text{Lem.~\ref{lem:restore-total-depth}}}{\leq}
 \totd(F)+d(N-d)+d^2
 =\totd(F)+Nd
 \le\totd(F)+N\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{td}}.
\]
Minimizing over $F$ gives the upper comparison, and
the lower comparison follows from $J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

The following comparison lemma assumes that each side of a biclique
lies within one clique-factor support. When a side is covered by
several such supports, Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-sparsification} splits the
biclique into sub-bicliques to meet this assumption.

\begin{lemma}[Treewidth comparison]
\label{lem:tw-factor-sparsification}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}, and let $\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}$
be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}. Suppose that for
every $f\in\mathcal B$ there are $g,h\in\mathcal C$ with
$A_f\subseteq U_g$ and $B_f\subseteq U_h$.
Every tree
decomposition of $J$ can be completed to one of $H$ whose width
increases by at most $\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}$. In particular,
$\operatorname{tw}(J)\le\operatorname{tw}(H)
 \le\operatorname{tw}(J)+\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a tree decomposition $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ of $J$.
For each $f\in\mathcal C$, choose $u_f$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-clique-completion} and put
$D_f=U_f\setminus B_{u_f}$, so $|D_f|\le3(t_f-1)$.
Set $D_{\mathcal C}=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C}D_f$ and restrict
the bags to $B_u\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$.
Then $U_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}\subseteq
B_{u_f}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ for each $f\in\mathcal C$.

For $f\in\mathcal B$, choose $g,h\in\mathcal C$ with
$A_f\subseteq U_g$ and $B_f\subseteq U_h$.
The restricted sides $A_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ lie in
$B_{u_g}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_{u_h}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$, respectively. They still
satisfy the no-hole condition at threshold $t_f$, since any
$t_f$-subsets of them were also subsets of the original sides.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-biclique-completion} therefore gives
$D_f\subseteq(A_f\cup B_f)\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ with
$|D_f|\le2(t_f-1)$.
Put $D=D_{\mathcal C}\cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal B}D_f$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:treewidth-factor-exception-bound}
|D|\le\sum_{f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B}|D_f| \overset{\text{Lems.~\ref{lem:tw-clique-completion}, \ref{lem:tw-biclique-completion}}}{\leq}
 3\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+2\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1)
 \overset{\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}}{=}\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}.
\end{equation}

In the bags $B_u\setminus D$, each surviving clique factor lies
in $B_{u_f}\setminus D$, each surviving biclique edge is covered
by the choice of $D_f$, and retained edges remain covered because
$E_0\subseteq E(J)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union}
therefore gives a decomposition of $H-D$: every surviving vertex
occurs at the same nodes as before, so its occurrences remain
nonempty and connected.

Define $\widehat B_u=(B_u\setminus D)\cup D$ for every $u\in V(T)$.
This retains all edges of $H-D$, covers every edge incident with
$D$, and makes each vertex of $D$ occur throughout $T$.
Thus $(T,(\widehat B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ is a decomposition of $H$.
Since $|\widehat B_u|\le|B_u|+|D|$ for every $u$, its width
increases by at most
$|D|\overset{\eqref{eq:treewidth-factor-exception-bound}}{\leq}
\operatorname{exc}_{\mathrm{tw}}$.
Choosing a minimum-width decomposition proves the upper comparison.
The lower comparison follows from $J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

All these completions are computable in polynomial time in the graph,
factor description, and supplied forest or decomposition encoding.
The forest paths are obtained by the stated depth-first sweeps.
For the clique bags, count the occurrence subtrees contained on each
side of every decomposition-tree edge, orient the edges, and find a
sink. Path traversals give the interval endpoints for anchored bicliques;
the matching and vertex-cover procedure above gives their deletion sets.
Finally take the union of the deletion sets and perform the stated
restriction and restoration. The running time includes the length of
the supplied decomposition, which may contain redundant bags.

\subsection{Sparse replacements at arbitrary sizes}

We now construct bounded-degree replacements satisfying the no-hole
conditions used above, with $t_f-1$ a small fixed fraction of the
factor size. The expander mixing lemma turns an eigenvalue bound
into an edge between two large sets. Fixed-length walk powers lower
the required set size, and grouping auxiliary vertices gives the
prescribed clique supports and biclique sides.
Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-expanders} records the resulting bounds.

\subsubsection{Edge expansion and adjacency matrices}

We begin with edge expansion, which requires a set to send many
edges to its complement. For our replacements, we must also force
an edge between two prescribed large sets, even when they do not
cover the graph. An eigenvalue bound will control these edge counts
through the mixing lemma.
The definitions and standard spectral facts below follow
\cite[Section~2]{HLW2006}.

Let $\mathcal E$ be a finite undirected multigraph on $M\ge1$
vertices, allowing loops and parallel edges. A \emph{step} traverses
one edge occurrence in a specified direction. A nonloop edge gives
one step in each direction, and a loop gives one step that stays at
its vertex. Parallel edge occurrences give distinct steps. In this
subsection, $d$-regular means that every vertex has $d$ possible
outgoing steps, counting loop steps once.

For vertex sets $S,T\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$, let
$e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)$ be the number of steps that start in $S$ and
end in $T$, counted with multiplicity. The sets may overlap. If
$S=T$, each nonloop internal edge contributes two steps and each loop
contributes one. If $S,T$ are disjoint, each edge between them
contributes one step from $S$ to $T$.
In particular,
$|\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|=e_{\mathcal E}(S,V(\mathcal E)\setminus S)$.

\begin{definition}[Edge expanders]
For an integer $d\ge1$ and a real $c>0$, a $d$-regular graph $\mathcal E$ is a
\emph{$(d,c)$-edge expander} if
\[
 |\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|\ge c|S|
 \quad\text{whenever }0<|S|\le M/2.
\]
A sequence of graphs whose orders tend to infinity is a
\emph{bounded-degree expander family} if the same constants $d,c$
work for every graph in the sequence.
\end{definition}

The boundary condition says that separating $S$ from its complement
requires deleting at least $c|S|$ edge occurrences. Bounded degree
keeps the total number of edges linear in the number of vertices.
To bound the edges between two specified sets, we express their
number using the adjacency matrix and then bound its eigenvalues.

Now assume that $\mathcal E$ is $d$-regular with $d\ge1$. Define its \emph{adjacency
matrix} $A_{\mathcal E}$ below, with $\mathbf1$ the all-ones vector
and $\mathsf T$ denoting transpose:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auxiliary-regular-adjacency}
 (A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}=e_{\mathcal E}(\{u\},\{v\})
 \quad(u,v\in V(\mathcal E)),\qquad
 A_{\mathcal E}^{\mathsf T}=A_{\mathcal E},\qquad
 A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1=d\mathbf1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, reversing steps gives
$(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}=(A_{\mathcal E})_{vu}$, and regularity gives
$\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}=d$
for every $u\in V(\mathcal E)$.
A real vector $x=(x_v)_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}$ assigns a real number to
each vertex, and matrix multiplication gives
$(A_{\mathcal E}x)_u=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}x_v$
for $u\in V(\mathcal E)$.
Thus this coordinate is the sum of the values at the endpoints of
the steps leaving $u$.

For $S\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$, its
\emph{indicator vector} $\mathbf1_S$ has coordinate one on $S$ and
zero elsewhere. For real vectors $x,y$ indexed by $V(\mathcal E)$,
$x^{\mathsf T}y=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}x_vy_v$. The Euclidean length of $x$ is
$\|x\|_2=(\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}x_v^2)^{1/2}$. Two vectors are
\emph{orthogonal} if $x^{\mathsf T}y=0$. In particular,
$x$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf1$ if and only if
$\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}x_v=0$.
The step count can now be written as
$e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)=\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1_T$.

A nonzero vector $x$ is an \emph{eigenvector} of $A_{\mathcal E}$
with \emph{eigenvalue} $\mu$ if $A_{\mathcal E}x=\mu x$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral theorem {\cite[Section~2]{HLW2006}}]
\label{thm:real-symmetric-spectral}
Every real symmetric matrix has a basis of eigenvectors of length
one that are pairwise orthogonal. Every vector is a unique linear
combination of these basis vectors.
\end{theorem}

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:real-symmetric-spectral} to $A_{\mathcal E}$,
taking the first basis vector to be $\mathbf1/\sqrt M$, with eigenvalue $d$.
All remaining basis vectors then have coordinate sum zero.
Moreover, multiplication by $A_{\mathcal E}$ preserves zero sums:
\[
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathcal E}x
 \overset{\eqref{eq:auxiliary-regular-adjacency}}{=}
 (A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1)^{\mathsf T}x
 \overset{\eqref{eq:auxiliary-regular-adjacency}}{=}
 d\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=0
 \quad\text{if }\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=0.
\]

Subtracting the average coordinate from every coordinate of a
vector produces a zero-sum vector. Thus zero-sum vectors describe
deviations from a constant assignment. The eigenvalue bound below
controls these deviations and will give the error bound in the
mixing lemma.

\begin{definition}[Spectral expanders]
Write $\mathbf1^\perp=\{x\in\mathbb R^M:
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=0\}$ for the zero-sum subspace. Since
$A_{\mathcal E}$ maps this subspace into itself, the graph has
\emph{nonconstant eigenvalue bound} $\lambda\ge0$ if every eigenvalue
of the restriction of $A_{\mathcal E}$ to $\mathbf1^\perp$ has
absolute value at most $\lambda$.
We call it a \emph{spectral expander} when $\lambda<d$.
A sequence of such graphs with orders tending to infinity is a
\emph{bounded-degree spectral expander family} if $d$ is fixed and
$\lambda/d$ has a common upper bound below one, independent of $M$.
\end{definition}

The word nonconstant excludes the chosen all-ones eigenvector,
not any further eigenvectors with the same eigenvalue $d$.
The absolute-value bound controls both positive and negative
eigenvalues, which will be needed when we take walk powers.
\begin{lemma}[Spectral edge expansion
{\cite[Theorem~2.4]{HLW2006}}]
\label{lem:spectral-edge-expansion}
Let $\mathcal E$ be a $d$-regular graph on $M$ vertices with
nonconstant eigenvalue bound $\lambda<d$. Then
\[
 |\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|\geq\frac{d-\lambda}{2}|S|
 \qquad\text{whenever }0<|S|\leq M/2.
\]
\end{lemma}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-edge-expansion}, such a spectral family
is an expander family under the preceding definition.

\subsubsection{The mixing lemma and walk powers}

Our goal is to show that the edge count between two large sets is
positive. If the $d|S|$ steps leaving $S$ had endpoints uniformly
distributed over the $M$ vertices, then $d|S||T|/M$ would end in $T$.
The expander mixing lemma bounds the difference between this
quantity and the actual step count. We use its standard form,
including when $S,T$ overlap.

\begin{lemma}[Expander mixing lemma {\cite[Lemma~2.5]{HLW2006}}]
\label{lem:expander-mixing}
Let $\mathcal E$ be a $d$-regular graph on $M$ vertices with
nonconstant eigenvalue bound $\lambda$. For all
$S,T\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$,
\[
 \left|e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)-\frac{d|S||T|}{M}\right|
 \le\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}.
\]
\end{lemma}

To guarantee a step between $S$ and $T$, it is enough that the
main term exceed the error term. For nonempty $S,T\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$,
the lemma gives
\[
 e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)
 \overset{\text{Lem.~\ref{lem:expander-mixing}}}{\geq}
 \frac{d|S||T|}{M}-\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}
 =d\sqrt{|S||T|}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|S||T|}}{M}-\frac\lambda d\right).
\]
Thus $e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)>0$ if
$\sqrt{|S||T|}/M>\lambda/d$. In particular, for any integer $t\ge1$,
sets of at least $t$ vertices are joined whenever $t>(\lambda/d)M$. Thus $\lambda/d$
determines the fraction of vertices above which holes are
impossible. A smaller ratio gives a smaller threshold and hence a
smaller completion loss.

To reduce this ratio, we use a graph whose steps represent sequences
of a fixed number of steps in the original graph. We define this
construction next and derive its eigenvalue bound.

For an integer $h\ge0$, a \emph{walk of length $h$} consists of vertices
$x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_h$ and a chosen step from $x_{i-1}$ to $x_i$
for each $1\le i\le h$. Vertices and edges may be repeated.
Different choices among parallel steps give different walks,
even if their vertex sequences agree.
Reversing the order and directions of its steps gives
a walk of the same length in the opposite direction.

For an integer $h\ge1$, the \emph{$h$th walk power} of
$\mathcal E$ has the same vertex set and one step from $u$ to $v$
for each length-$h$ walk from $u$ to $v$ in $\mathcal E$.
Its adjacency matrix is $A_{\mathcal E}^h$: matrix multiplication
counts walks by length. This matrix is symmetric, so the walk power
is undirected. There are $d$ choices at each step, giving degree
$d^h$. If $A_{\mathcal E}x=\mu x$, then
$A_{\mathcal E}^hx=\mu^hx$, so the nonconstant eigenvalue bound is
$\lambda^h$~\cite[Section~2]{HLW2006}. The ratio of this bound to the
degree is $\lambda^h/d^h=(\lambda/d)^h$.
When $\lambda/d<1$, a fixed sufficiently large $h$ makes this ratio
smaller than any prescribed positive constant. The new degree
$d^h$ still does not depend on the number of vertices. Thus $h$
counts steps in an auxiliary walk, whereas $t$ counts vertices in
the sets that a replacement must join.

We use the following explicit starting family.

\begin{theorem}[Margulis--Gabber--Galil expanders
 {\cite[Construction~8.1 and Theorem~8.2]{HLW2006}}]
\label{thm:auxiliary-expander-family}
For every integer $\nu\ge1$, put
$\mathbb Z_\nu=\{0,\ldots,\nu-1\}$, with arithmetic modulo $\nu$.
On vertex set $\mathbb Z_\nu^2$, define $\mathcal G_\nu$ by giving
each vertex $(x,y)$ the eight
outgoing steps with endpoints
\[
 (x\pm2y,y),\quad (x\pm(2y+1),y),\quad
 (x,y\pm2x),\quad (x,y\pm(2x+1)).
\]
The eight choices remain distinct steps when endpoints coincide,
including loop steps when the endpoint is $(x,y)$. They define an
eight-regular undirected multigraph under our step convention,
since the transformations with opposite signs are inverses.
Its nonconstant eigenvalues have absolute value at most $5\sqrt2$.
Put $\lambda_0=5\sqrt2/8<1$. For every integer $h\ge1$, the
$h$th walk power $\mathcal G_\nu^{[h]}$ has degree $8^h$ and
normalized transition operator
\[
 P_{\nu,h}=8^{-h}A_{\mathcal G_\nu}^h,\qquad
 \|P_{\nu,h}x\|_2\le\lambda_0^h\|x\|_2
 \quad(x\in\mathbf1^\perp).
\]
Thus its normalized operator norm on the zero-sum subspace is at
most $\lambda_0^h$; when $\nu=1$ that subspace is zero. The
walk-power assertion follows from the preceding calculation.
For fixed $h$, enumerating its $\nu^2 8^h$ steps constructs the
multigraph deterministically in time polynomial in $\nu$.
\end{theorem}

These graphs have square orders $\nu^2$. To obtain replacements
on arbitrary prescribed sets, we group auxiliary vertices and map
each group to one prescribed vertex, using separate maps for the
two biclique sides. The resulting lemma is proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:factor-expanders}.

\begin{lemma}[Mapping to prescribed vertex sets]
\label{lem:factor-expanders}
For every fixed $\zeta>0$ and $\kappa\ge1$, there is a constant
$d_{\zeta,\kappa}$ such that the following connected simple graphs,
each of maximum degree at most $d_{\zeta,\kappa}$, can be constructed
deterministically in polynomial time.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For any specified nonempty finite set $U$, a graph on $U$
 in which every two disjoint sets $X,Y\subseteq U$ with
 $|X|,|Y|\ge\lceil\zeta|U|\rceil$ have an edge between them.
 \item For any specified disjoint nonempty finite sets $A,B$ with
 $\max\{|A|,|B|\}/\min\{|A|,|B|\}\le\kappa$, a bipartite graph
 on $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ with parts $A,B$ in which every two sets
 $X\subseteq A$, $Y\subseteq B$ with
 $|X|,|Y|\ge\lceil\zeta(|A|+|B|)\rceil$ have an edge between them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
