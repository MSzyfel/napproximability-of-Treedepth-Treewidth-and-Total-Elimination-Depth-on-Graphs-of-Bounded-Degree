\section{Hardness on graphs of bounded degree}
\label{sec:bounded-degree-hardness}

\subsection{The degree-reduction approach}

Let $H$ be one of the dense graphs constructed above, with
$N=|V(H)|$. Its edge set consists of $E_0\subseteq\binom{V(H)}2$,
which we keep during sparsification, and clique and biclique factors
indexed by finite disjoint sets $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal B$.
Each clique factor $f\in\mathcal C$ has a nonempty support
$U_f\subseteq V(H)$ and contributes
all pairs in $\binom{U_f}{2}$. Each biclique factor has disjoint nonempty sides
$A_f,B_f\subseteq V(H)$, support $U_f=A_f\mathbin{\dot\cup}B_f$, and contributes
all edges $uv$ with $u\in A_f$ and $v\in B_f$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-edge-union}
 E(H)=E_0\cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C}\binom{U_f}{2}
       \cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal B}\{uv:u\in A_f,\ v\in B_f\}.
\end{equation}
For each factor choose an integer threshold $t_f\ge1$. Two
disjoint vertex sets are \emph{anticomplete} if no edge joins them.
A \emph{$(t,t)$ hole} is a pair of anticomplete sets of $t$ vertices
each. A clique replacement on $U_f$ has no hole at threshold $t_f$
if every two disjoint $t_f$-subsets of $U_f$ have at least one edge
between them. A biclique replacement on
$A_f\mathbin{\dot\cup}B_f$ has no hole at threshold $t_f$ if every
$t_f$-subset of $A_f$ and every $t_f$-subset of $B_f$ have at least
one edge between them.

Different factors may share vertices or edges. A factor specifies
edges to be included, not an induced subgraph, so other factors or
$E_0$ may add further edges on its support.

Replace each factor by a subgraph of its specified edge set on
the same support. Let $J$ be the union of these replacement edges
with $E_0$. Then $J$ is a spanning subgraph of $H$. The argument has
three steps.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Preserve the YES bound by deleting edges.}
   Every elimination forest or tree decomposition of $H$ is also
   valid for $J$. Thus treedepth, treewidth, and total elimination
   depth do not increase.
 \item \emph{Recover a dense solution from a sparse one.}
   Each replacement satisfies its no-hole condition at threshold
   $t_f$. For clique factors the subsets are disjoint, and for
   biclique factors they lie on opposite sides.
   The completion lemmas show that, after deleting a small set $D$
   of exceptional vertices, an arbitrary forest or decomposition
   of $J$ has every edge of $H-D$ represented by comparable
   endpoints or by vertices in a common bag. For forests,
   restore $D$ as a chain above all existing roots. For tree
   decompositions, add $D$ to every bag. The height or width
   increases by at most $|D|$, and the total depth by at most
   $N|D|$. Thus a sparse solution of value $z$ gives a dense
   solution of height or width at most $z+|D|$, or total depth at
   most $z+N|D|$.
 \item \emph{Keep the total loss below the hardness gap.}
   Choose $t_f$ so that $t_f-1$ is a small fraction of $|U_f|$
   for a clique factor and of $\min\{|A_f|,|B_f|\}$ for a
   biclique factor. If every vertex lies in at most a fixed number
   of factor supports, then $\sum_f |U_f|=O(N)$. Hence
   $|D|\le\varepsilon N$ for any prescribed fixed
   $\varepsilon>0$, by choosing those fractions sufficiently small.
   The resulting losses are at most $\varepsilon N$ for treedepth
   and treewidth, and $\varepsilon N^2$ for total depth. The dense
   gaps have these scales. If their leading YES and NO coefficients
   are $A$ and $B$ with $B>rA$, choose $\varepsilon$ so the loss
   coefficient is below $B-rA$.
\end{enumerate}

For the maximum degree to be bounded, we also need bounded degree
in $E_0$ and a bounded ratio between the two side sizes of every
biclique. This ratio bounds the number of auxiliary vertices mapped
to each prescribed vertex. The expander construction below then
gives the required no-hole condition with a constant degree
contribution from each factor. Since each vertex lies in a bounded number of
factor supports, its total degree is bounded. These constants may
depend on the desired approximation ratio, but not on $N$. For
treewidth, each
side of every biclique must additionally be partitionable into a
bounded number of sets, each contained in a clique-factor support.
The corresponding clique-factor bags anchor the side sets in the
biclique completion lemma.
The UGC applications require a bounded-degree independent-set source so
that their retained source edges satisfy the condition on $E_0$.

\subsection{Completing sparse factors}

The no-hole conditions above are inherited by deleting vertices. Recall that
forest restriction preserves the ancestor order between surviving
vertices and never increases their depths.

The following lemmas identify a small exceptional set whose deletion
allows a sparse factor to be completed. For a clique, the surviving
vertices must lie on one ancestor path or in one bag. For a biclique,
it is enough that every surviving cross-pair is comparable or appears
together in a bag.

\subsubsection{Clique factors}

\begin{lemma}[Clique completion in an elimination forest]\label{lem:sparse-clique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph, let $U\subseteq V(J)$, and let
$t\ge1$ be an integer. Suppose $J[U]$ has no two disjoint
anticomplete sets of size $t$. Every elimination forest $F$ of $J$ has an
ancestor path $P$ of $F$ such that
$|U\setminus V(P)|\leq2(t-1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $|U|<t$, then $|U|\leq t-1\leq2(t-1)$, so the empty path
satisfies the claim. Now assume $|U|\geq t$. Traverse each rooted
tree in depth-first order. Introduce a vertex when first reached,
traverse all its child subtrees, and forget it after those subtrees
have been traversed. Throughout the traversal maintain two subsets
of $U$: $L$ is the set of vertices already forgotten, and $R$ is the
set of vertices not yet introduced. Initially $L=\varnothing$ and
$R=U$. When a vertex of $U$ is introduced, remove it from $R$; when
it is forgotten, add it to $L$. The vertices in $U\setminus(L\cup R)$
have been introduced but not forgotten, so they lie on the current
ancestor path. We traverse the rooted trees one after another, with
this path empty between trees.

At every point, no edge of $J$ joins $L$ to $R$. Indeed, if $x\in L$ and
$y\in R$, then $y$ cannot be an ancestor of $x$, since ancestors are
introduced first. Also, $x$ cannot be an ancestor of $y$, since the
traversal finishes the subtree of $x$ before forgetting $x$. Thus
$x$ and $y$ are incomparable, and the elimination-forest property
rules out the edge $xy$.

Consider the instant immediately after the $t$th vertex of $U$ is
forgotten. Then $|L|=t$. If $|R|\geq t$, then $L$ and $R$ contain
disjoint anticomplete subsets of size $t$, contrary to the
hypothesis. Hence $|R|\leq t-1$. Immediately before this forgetting
event, $|L|=t-1$ and $R$ is unchanged. Let $P$ be the current
ancestor path then. The sets $L$, $R$, and $U\setminus(L\cup R)$
partition $U$, and every vertex in the third set lies on $P$. Hence
\[
 |U\setminus V(P)|\leq |L|+|R|\leq(t-1)+(t-1)=2(t-1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clique completion in a tree decomposition]
\label{lem:tw-clique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph, let $U\subseteq V(J)$, and let
$t\ge1$ be an integer. Suppose $J[U]$ has no two disjoint
anticomplete sets of size $t$. Every tree decomposition of $J$ has a node $u$ whose bag
$B_u$ satisfies $|U\setminus B_u|\leq3(t-1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ be the supplied decomposition. For
$v\in V(J)$, put $T_v=\{u\in V(T):v\in B_u\}$.
Each $T_v$ is a nonempty connected set of tree nodes.
If $|U|<t$, choose any node $u$ of $T$; then
$|U\setminus B_u|\leq|U|\leq t-1\leq3(t-1)$. Otherwise consider an edge of the
decomposition tree. Its deletion leaves two sides. Call a side
heavy if it contains the entire subtree $T_v$ for at least $t$ vertices $v\in U$.
If both sides were heavy, choose a set $X\subseteq U$ of exactly $t$
vertices whose subtrees lie entirely on one side, and a set
$Y\subseteq U$ of exactly $t$ vertices whose subtrees lie entirely on
the other side. The sets are disjoint, since a nonempty subtree cannot
lie on both sides, so they contain $2t$ vertices in total. No bag
can contain both a vertex of $X$ and a vertex of $Y$, since their bag
subtrees lie on opposite sides. Thus $X$ and $Y$ are anticomplete,
contrary to the hypothesis. Orient the edge toward its
heavy side if one exists, and arbitrarily if neither is heavy.
Following outgoing edges cannot revisit a node, since the underlying
tree has no cycles. The walk therefore ends at a node with no outgoing
edge. Call such a node a \emph{sink}, and let
$u$ be one.

For each component $C$ of $T-u$, at most $t-1$ vertices $v\in U$
have $T_v\subseteq V(C)$. Otherwise, the edge from $u$ into $C$ would be
oriented away from the sink $u$. Let $W=U\setminus B_u$. Every
vertex $v\in W$ has its entire subtree in one component of $T-u$;
write $W_C=\{v\in W:T_v\subseteq V(C)\}$. Thus $|W_C|\leq t-1$ for
each component $C$. If $t=1$, this gives $W=\varnothing$. For
$t\geq2$, suppose $|W|\geq3t-2$. Select components one at a time
until the sum of their $|W_C|$ first reaches $t$, and let $W_1$ be
the union of their $W_C$ sets. The last component added contributes at most $t-1$
vertices, so $t\leq|W_1|\leq2t-2$. Let $W_2=W\setminus W_1$;
then $|W_2|\geq(3t-2)-(2t-2)=t$. The subtrees of vertices in
$W_1$ and $W_2$ lie in disjoint groups of components of $T-u$,
so no bag contains a vertex from each set. Hence $W_1,W_2$ are anticomplete in
$J$, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore
$|U\setminus B_u|=|W|\leq3t-3=3(t-1)$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Biclique factors}

We use K\"onig's theorem~\cite[Theorem~2.1.1]{Diestel2017}: in a
bipartite graph, the maximum matching size equals the minimum
vertex-cover size.

\begin{lemma}[Biclique completion in an elimination forest]\label{lem:sparse-biclique-completion}
Let $F$ be an elimination forest of a finite simple graph $J$,
let $A,B\subseteq V(J)$ be disjoint, and let $t\ge1$ be an integer.
Suppose the bipartite graph of $J$ between $A$ and $B$ has no
$(t,t)$ hole. There is a set $D\subseteq A\cup B$ such that
$|D|\leq4(t-1)$ and each pair in
$(A\setminus D)\times(B\setminus D)$ is comparable in $F$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $W=A\cup B$ and traverse each rooted tree depth-first: introduce
a vertex when first reached, traverse its child subtrees, and forget
it after they are complete. Maintain the sets $L$ of vertices in $W$
already forgotten and $R$ of vertices in $W$ not yet introduced,
starting with $L=\varnothing$ and $R=W$. When a vertex of $W$ is
introduced, remove it from $R$; when it is forgotten, add it to $L$.
Thus $W\setminus(L\cup R)$ consists of the introduced, unforgotten
vertices on the current ancestor path. Assign a distinct time to
each introduction and forgetting event, traversing different rooted
trees consecutively. Let $t_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ and
$t_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ be the introduction and forgetting times of
$v$. The vertices in the subtree rooted at $v$ are visited during
$[t_{\mathrm{in}}(v),t_{\mathrm{out}}(v)]$.
Two vertices are incomparable if and only if these intervals
are disjoint. Define the bipartite graph $K$ by
$E(K)=\{uv:u\in A,\ v\in B,\ u,v\text{ are incomparable in }F\}$.
For each $uv\in E(K)$, take $u\in A$ and $v\in B$, and partition these edges according to
$t_{\mathrm{out}}(u)<t_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ or $t_{\mathrm{out}}(v)<t_{\mathrm{in}}(u)$.
If $K$ has a matching of size at least $4t-3$, one type contains
at least $2t-1$ matching edges. Consider first the type satisfying
$t_{\mathrm{out}}(u)<t_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$. Write its matching edges
as $u_iv_i$, for $1\le i\le m_0$, where $m_0\ge2t-1$, and order
them so that $t_{\mathrm{out}}(u_1)\le\cdots\le
t_{\mathrm{out}}(u_{m_0})$. For $i\le t$ and
$j\ge m_0-t+1$, we have $i\le j$ and therefore
$t_{\mathrm{out}}(u_i)\le t_{\mathrm{out}}(u_j)<
t_{\mathrm{in}}(v_j)$. Hence the sets
$X=\{u_1,\ldots,u_t\}$ and
$Y=\{v_{m_0-t+1},\ldots,v_{m_0}\}$ each have size $t$, and every
vertex of $X$ is incomparable with every vertex of $Y$. The matching
ensures that the vertices within each set are distinct. Since every
edge of $J$ has comparable endpoints in $F$, the sets are
anticomplete, a contradiction. For the other type, interchange the
roles of $A$ and $B$ and order by the forgetting times of the
vertices in $B$; the same argument gives a contradiction.
It follows that the maximum matching size of $K$ is at most $4t-4$.
K\"onig's theorem supplies a vertex cover $D$ of $K$ of that size.
For $u\in A\setminus D$ and $v\in B\setminus D$, we have
$uv\notin E(K)$ because $D$ covers $K$. By the definition of $K$,
$u,v$ are comparable in $F$.
\end{proof}

For treewidth, we first complete the clique factors and use their
bags to anchor the two sides of each biclique. We use the
path-projection observation from the proof of Bonnet's
Lemma~5~\cite{BonnetTreewidth}: if two subtrees contain opposite
endpoints of a path, they intersect if and only if their
intersections with that path intersect. The proof below recalls this
fact using the median of the two endpoints and an intersection node.
The path traces are prefixes and suffixes. The no-hole condition
then bounds a matching of uncovered pairs, and a vertex cover of
those pairs gives the deletion set.

When vertices are deleted from a graph, we restrict its tree
decomposition by removing them from every bag.

\begin{lemma}[Biclique completion between two bags]
\label{lem:tw-biclique-completion}
Let $J$ be a finite simple graph and let $t\ge1$ be an integer.
Fix a tree decomposition $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ of $J$.
Suppose $A,B\subseteq V(J)$ are disjoint, $A\subseteq B_x$ and
$B\subseteq B_y$ for some $x,y\in V(T)$, and the
bipartite graph of $J$ between $A$ and $B$ has no $(t,t)$ hole.
There is a set $D\subseteq A\cup B$ with $|D|\leq2(t-1)$ such that
for every $u\in A\setminus D$ and $v\in B\setminus D$, some
$z\in V(T)$ satisfies $\{u,v\}\subseteq B_z\setminus D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $v\in V(J)$, put $T_v=\{z\in V(T):v\in B_z\}$.
If $x=y$, take $D=\varnothing$. Otherwise let $P$ be the vertex
set of the path from $x$ to $y$, ordered from $x$ toward $y$.
For $u\in A$, the intersection $T_u\cap P$
is a nonempty prefix of $P$, starting at $x$. For $v\in B$,
$T_v\cap P$ is a suffix ending at $y$.
The subtrees $T_u,T_v$ intersect if and only if these path
intervals intersect. For the nontrivial direction, if both
contain some node $z$, they both contain the median of $x,y,z$,
which lies on $P$.

Define the bipartite graph $K$ by
$E(K)=\{uv:u\in A,\ v\in B,\ T_u\cap T_v=\varnothing\}$.
If it has a matching of size $m_0\ge2t-1$, write its edges
$u_iv_i$, with $u_i\in A$ and $v_i\in B$, and order them so that the endpoint of the prefix
$T_{u_i}\cap P$ is nondecreasing in $i$. The endpoint of each
$u_i$-prefix precedes the start of its matched $v_i$-suffix, since
the two subtrees are disjoint and the prefix starts at $x$ while the
suffix ends at $y$. Consequently, if $i\le t$ and
$j\ge m_0-t+1$, then $i\le j$ and the prefix for $u_i$ ends before
the suffix for $v_j$ starts. Thus the sets
$X=\{u_1,\ldots,u_t\}$ and
$Y=\{v_{m_0-t+1},\ldots,v_{m_0}\}$ each have size $t$, and their
bag subtrees are disjoint across the two sets. The matching ensures
that vertices within each set are distinct. An edge of $J$ requires
its endpoints to occur together in a bag, so $X,Y$ are anticomplete,
contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore the maximum matching size of
$K$ is at most $2t-2$.

K\"onig's theorem gives a vertex cover $D$ of $K$ with
$|D|\le2t-2$. If $u\in A\setminus D$ and $v\in B\setminus D$,
then $uv\notin E(K)$, so $T_u\cap T_v\ne\varnothing$.
Choose $z\in T_u\cap T_v$. Then $\{u,v\}\subseteq B_z\setminus D$,
as required.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Comparison bounds}

\begin{definition}[Graphs and replacement conditions]
\label{def:factor-comparison}
Let $H$ be a finite simple graph whose edges are specified
by~\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union}. For each
$f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B$, fix an integer $t_f\ge1$.
Let $J\subseteq H$ be a spanning subgraph with $E_0\subseteq E(J)$.
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For each $f\in\mathcal C$, every two disjoint sets
   $X,Y\subseteq U_f$ with $|X|=|Y|=t_f$ have an edge of $J$
   between them.
 \item For each $f\in\mathcal B$, every $X\subseteq A_f$ and
   $Y\subseteq B_f$ with $|X|=|Y|=t_f$ have an edge of $J$
   between them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Each preceding completion lemma starts with a solution for $J$ and
deletes a small set of vertices so that the same solution also covers
the edges of one completed factor. To cover all edges of $H$, take
the union $D$ of these deletion sets. Further deletions preserve each
completion, so the restricted solution is valid for $H-D$. We can
then restore $D$ above the roots of an elimination forest, or add
$D$ to every bag of a tree decomposition. Thus the number of deleted
vertices controls the increase in height or width. For total depth,
each restored vertex increases the cost by at most $N=|V(H)|$.

For treewidth, we first choose a deletion set $D_{\mathcal C}$
for the clique factors, so each $U_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ lies
in a bag. If every biclique side lies in a clique support, these
bags contain $A_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$, as required by the biclique
completion lemma. Summing the deletion bounds gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{td}}=2\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+4\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1),
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}=3\sum_{f\in\mathcal C}(t_f-1)+2\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}(t_f-1).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Treedepth comparison]\label{lem:factor-sparsification}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}, and let $\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$
be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}.
Every elimination forest $F$ of $J$ can be
completed to an elimination forest $\widehat F$ of $H$ with
$h(\widehat F)\le h(F)+\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$. In particular,
$\td(J)\le\td(H)\le\td(J)+\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an elimination forest $F$ of $J$. For each $f\in\mathcal C$,
choose the path $P_f$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-clique-completion}
and put $D_f=U_f\setminus V(P_f)$, so $|D_f|\le2(t_f-1)$.
For each $f\in\mathcal B$, let $D_f\subseteq A_f\cup B_f$ be
the set from Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-biclique-completion}, with
$|D_f|\le4(t_f-1)$. Set
$D=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C\cup\mathcal B}D_f$.
Thus $|D|\le\sum_f|D_f|\le\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.

Let $F'$ be the restriction of $F$ to $V(H)\setminus D$.
For $f\in\mathcal C$, the set
$U_f\setminus D\subseteq U_f\setminus D_f$ lies on $P_f$,
so every pair in $\binom{U_f\setminus D}{2}$ is comparable in $F'$.
For $f\in\mathcal B$, every pair in
$(A_f\setminus D)\times(B_f\setminus D)$ is comparable in $F'$ by the
choice of $D_f$. Finally, if
$uv\in E_0\cap\binom{V(H)\setminus D}{2}$, then $uv\in E(J)$,
so $u,v$ are comparable in $F$ and remain comparable in $F'$.
By~\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union}, these three cases cover every
edge of $H-D$. Thus $F'$ is an elimination forest of $H-D$ with
$h(F')\le h(F)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:restore-vertices},
restoring $D$ gives an elimination forest $\widehat F$ of $H$ with
$h(\widehat F)\le h(F)+|D|\le h(F)+\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.
Choosing $F$ of minimum height proves
$\td(H)\le\td(J)+\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$. The other comparison follows from
$J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Total-depth comparison]
\label{lem:total-depth-comparison}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}. Put $N=|V(H)|$, and let
$\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$ be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}.
Every elimination
forest $F$ of $J$ can be completed to an elimination forest
$\widehat F$ of $H$ with
$\totd(\widehat F)\le\totd(F)+N\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.
In particular, $\totd(J)\le \totd(H)\le \totd(J)+N\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix any elimination forest $F$ of $J$. Use the exceptional set
$D$ from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-sparsification} for
this forest, so $|D|\le\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$. That proof shows that the
restriction $F'$ of $F$ to $V(H)\setminus D$ is an elimination
forest of $H-D$.

For each $v\in V(H)\setminus D$, restriction gives
$\operatorname{depth}_{F'}(v)\le\operatorname{depth}_F(v)$.
Summing over $V(H)\setminus D$ gives $\totd(F')\le\totd(F)$. Restore
the vertices of $D$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:restore-vertices}, placing
them one at a time above all existing roots. Each restoration
increases the depths of the existing vertices by one and gives
the new root depth one. Its total cost is therefore the number of
vertices then present, at most $N$. After $|D|$ restorations, the
resulting elimination forest $\widehat F$ satisfies
$\totd(\widehat F)\le\totd(F)+N|D|\le\totd(F)+N\Delta_{\mathrm{td}}$.
Choosing $F$ of minimum total depth proves the upper
comparison. The lower comparison follows from $J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Treewidth comparison]
\label{lem:tw-factor-sparsification}
Let $H,J$ and the thresholds $t_f$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:factor-comparison}, and let $\Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}$
be defined by~\eqref{eq:completion-deletion-bounds}. Suppose that for
every $f\in\mathcal B$ there are $g,h\in\mathcal C$ with
$A_f\subseteq U_g$ and $B_f\subseteq U_h$.
Every tree
decomposition of $J$ can be completed to one of $H$ whose width
increases by at most $\Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}$. In particular,
$\operatorname{tw}(J)\le\operatorname{tw}(H)
 \le\operatorname{tw}(J)+\Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a tree decomposition $(T,(B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ of $J$.
For each $f\in\mathcal C$, choose $u_f$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-clique-completion} and put
$D_f=U_f\setminus B_{u_f}$, so $|D_f|\le3(t_f-1)$.
Set $D_{\mathcal C}=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal C}D_f$ and restrict
the bags to $B_u\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$.
Then $U_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}\subseteq
B_{u_f}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ for each $f\in\mathcal C$.

For $f\in\mathcal B$, choose $g,h\in\mathcal C$ with
$A_f\subseteq U_g$ and $B_f\subseteq U_h$.
The restricted sides $A_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_f\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ lie in
$B_{u_g}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ and
$B_{u_h}\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$, respectively. They still
satisfy the no-hole condition at threshold $t_f$, since any
$t_f$-subsets of them were also subsets of the original sides.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-biclique-completion} therefore gives
$D_f\subseteq(A_f\cup B_f)\setminus D_{\mathcal C}$ with
$|D_f|\le2(t_f-1)$.
Put $D=D_{\mathcal C}\cup\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal B}D_f$.
Then $|D|\le\sum_f|D_f|\le\Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}$.

For $f\in\mathcal C$, we have
$U_f\setminus D\subseteq B_{u_f}\setminus D$, so this bag covers
$\binom{U_f\setminus D}{2}$.
For $f\in\mathcal B$ and $x\in A_f\setminus D$,
$y\in B_f\setminus D$, the choice of $D_f$ gives a node $u\in V(T)$
with $\{x,y\}\subseteq B_u\setminus D$.
If $xy\in E_0\cap\binom{V(H)\setminus D}{2}$,
an original bag covering $xy\in E(J)$ still contains both endpoints
after restriction. Equation~\eqref{eq:factor-edge-union} now shows
that $(T,(B_u\setminus D)_{u\in V(T)})$ covers every edge of $H-D$.
Each vertex outside $D$ occurs at the same nodes as before, so its
bag occurrences remain nonempty and connected.

Define $\widehat B_u=(B_u\setminus D)\cup D$ for every $u\in V(T)$.
Every vertex of $D$ now occurs in every bag. The edges of $H-D$
remain covered. If $xy\in E(H)$ with $x,y\in D$, every bag contains
both endpoints. If $x\in D$ and $y\notin D$, any bag containing
$y$ also contains $x$.
Thus $(T,(\widehat B_u)_{u\in V(T)})$ is a decomposition of $H$.
Since $|\widehat B_u|\le|B_u|+|D|$ for every $u$, its width
increases by at most $|D|\le\Delta_{\mathrm{tw}}$.
Choosing a minimum-width decomposition proves the upper comparison.
The lower comparison follows from $J\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sparse replacements at arbitrary sizes}

The comparison lemmas only require an edge between any two disjoint
sets of $t_f$ vertices in a factor, or between sets of $t_f$ vertices
on opposite sides of a biclique. An expander can force these edges
while giving each vertex only a bounded number of neighbors. We
introduce the required expansion and eigenvalue bounds, then use
them to construct replacements on the prescribed vertex sets.

\subsubsection{Edge expansion and adjacency matrices}

We use families of bounded-degree graphs in which every vertex set
of size at most half the graph has a boundary proportional to its
size. We first define this expansion property, then introduce the
eigenvalue condition used to obtain our replacements.
The definitions and standard spectral facts below follow
\cite[Section~2]{HLW2006}.

Let $\mathcal E$ be a finite undirected multigraph on $M\ge1$
vertices, allowing loops and parallel edges. A \emph{step} traverses
one edge occurrence in a specified direction. A nonloop edge gives
one step in each direction, and a loop gives one step that stays at
its vertex. Parallel edge occurrences give distinct steps. In this
subsection, $d$-regular means that every vertex has $d$ possible
outgoing steps, counting loop steps once.

For vertex sets $S,T\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$, let
$e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)$ be the number of steps that start in $S$ and
end in $T$, counted with multiplicity. The sets may overlap. If
$S=T$, each nonloop internal edge contributes two steps and each loop
contributes one. If $S,T$ are disjoint, each edge between them
contributes one step from $S$ to $T$.
The \emph{edge boundary} $\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)$ is the multiset of
edge occurrences with one endpoint in $S$ and the other outside $S$.
Thus $|\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|=e_{\mathcal E}(S,V(\mathcal E)\setminus S)$.

\begin{definition}[Edge expanders]
For an integer $d\ge1$ and a real $c>0$, a $d$-regular graph $\mathcal E$ is a
\emph{$(d,c)$-edge expander} if
\[
 |\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|\ge c|S|
 \quad\text{whenever }0<|S|\le M/2.
\]
A sequence of graphs whose orders tend to infinity is a
\emph{bounded-degree expander family} if the same constants $d,c$
work for every graph in the sequence.
\end{definition}

The boundary condition says that separating $S$ from its complement
requires deleting at least $c|S|$ edge occurrences. Bounded degree
keeps the total number of edges linear in the number of vertices.
For our construction we use a spectral condition, which also bounds
the number of edges between any two specified vertex sets.

Now assume that $\mathcal E$ is $d$-regular. Its \emph{adjacency
matrix} $A_{\mathcal E}$ has rows and columns
indexed by $V(\mathcal E)$, with $(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}$ equal to the
number of steps from $u$ to $v$. It is symmetric, meaning
$(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}=(A_{\mathcal E})_{vu}$, because every step
between distinct vertices has a reverse step. Each row sum is $d$.
A real vector $x=(x_v)_{v\in V(\mathcal E)}$ assigns a real number to
each vertex, and matrix multiplication gives
$(A_{\mathcal E}x)_u=\sum_v(A_{\mathcal E})_{uv}x_v$.
Thus this coordinate is the sum of the values at the endpoints of
the steps leaving $u$.

Write $\mathbf1$ for the all-ones vector. For a set $S$, its
\emph{indicator vector} $\mathbf1_S$ has coordinate one on $S$ and
zero elsewhere. A superscript $\mathsf T$ denotes transpose, so
$x^{\mathsf T}y=\sum_vx_vy_v$. The Euclidean length of $x$ is
$\|x\|_2=(\sum_vx_v^2)^{1/2}$. Two vectors are
\emph{orthogonal} if $x^{\mathsf T}y=0$. In particular,
$x$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf1$ if and only if $\sum_vx_v=0$.
The step count can now be written as
$e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)=\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1_T$.

A nonzero vector $x$ is an \emph{eigenvector} of $A_{\mathcal E}$
with \emph{eigenvalue} $\mu$ if $A_{\mathcal E}x=\mu x$.
Since each row sum is $d$, we have
$A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1=d\mathbf1$. The spectral theorem for real
symmetric matrices states that there is a basis of eigenvectors
that have length one and are pairwise orthogonal, and every vector
is a unique linear combination of these basis vectors. We may take the
first basis vector to be $\mathbf1/\sqrt M$, with eigenvalue $d$.
All remaining basis vectors then have coordinate sum zero.
Moreover, multiplication by $A_{\mathcal E}$ preserves zero sums:
\[
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathcal E}x
 =(A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1)^{\mathsf T}x
 =d\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=0
 \quad\text{if }\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=0.
\]

\begin{definition}[Spectral expanders]
The graph $\mathcal E$ has \emph{nonconstant eigenvalue bound}
$\lambda\ge0$ if all eigenvalues belonging to the zero-sum
eigenvectors have absolute value at most $\lambda$.
We call it a \emph{spectral expander} when $\lambda<d$.
A sequence of such graphs with orders tending to infinity is a
\emph{bounded-degree spectral expander family} if $d$ is fixed and
$\lambda/d$ has a common upper bound below one, independent of $M$.
\end{definition}

The word nonconstant excludes the chosen all-ones eigenvector,
not any further eigenvectors with the same eigenvalue $d$.
The absolute-value bound controls both positive and negative
eigenvalues, which will be needed when we take walk powers.
The standard spectral lower bound on edge expansion
\cite[Theorem~2.4]{HLW2006} states that a $d$-regular graph with
nonconstant eigenvalue bound $\lambda<d$ satisfies
$|\cut_{\mathcal E}(S)|\ge(d-\lambda)|S|/2$ whenever
$0<|S|\le M/2$. Hence such a spectral family is an expander family
under the preceding definition.

\subsubsection{The mixing lemma and walk powers}

If the $d|S|$ steps leaving $S$ had endpoints uniformly distributed
over the $M$ vertices, then $d|S||T|/M$ would end in $T$.
The expander mixing lemma bounds the difference between this
quantity and the actual step count. We use its standard form,
including when $S,T$ overlap.

\begin{lemma}[Expander mixing lemma {\cite[Lemma~2.5]{HLW2006}}]
\label{lem:expander-mixing}
Let $\mathcal E$ be a $d$-regular graph on $M$ vertices with
nonconstant eigenvalue bound $\lambda$. For all
$S,T\subseteq V(\mathcal E)$,
\[
 \left|e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)-\frac{d|S||T|}{M}\right|
 \le\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}.
\]
\end{lemma}

To guarantee a step between $S$ and $T$, it is enough that the
main term exceed the error term. For nonempty $S,T$, the lemma gives
\[
 e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)
 \ge\frac{d|S||T|}{M}-\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}
 =d\sqrt{|S||T|}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|S||T|}}{M}-\frac\lambda d\right).
\]
Thus $e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)>0$ if
$\sqrt{|S||T|}/M>\lambda/d$. In particular, sets of at least $t$
vertices are joined whenever $t>(\lambda/d)M$. Reducing
$\lambda/d$ therefore lets us enforce the no-hole condition at
smaller thresholds. We do this by replacing steps with walks.

For an integer $k\ge0$, a \emph{walk of length $k$} consists of vertices
$x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_k$ and a chosen step from $x_{i-1}$ to $x_i$
for each $1\le i\le k$. Vertices and edges may be repeated.
Different choices among parallel steps give different walks,
even if their vertex sequences agree. A walk is \emph{closed} if
$x_0=x_k$. Reversing the order and directions of its steps gives
a walk of the same length in the opposite direction.

For an integer $k\ge1$, the \emph{$k$th walk power} of
$\mathcal E$ has the same vertex set and one step from $u$ to $v$
for each length-$k$ walk from $u$ to $v$ in $\mathcal E$.
Its adjacency matrix is $A_{\mathcal E}^k$. To see this, the
$(u,v)$ entry of $A_{\mathcal E}$ counts length-one walks, and
\[
 (A_{\mathcal E}^{k+1})_{uv}
 =\sum_z(A_{\mathcal E}^k)_{uz}(A_{\mathcal E})_{zv}
\]
counts a length-$k$ walk from $u$ to $z$, followed by a step from
$z$ to $v$. Summing over the penultimate vertex $z$ counts every
length-$(k+1)$ walk once. Reversing walks shows that the walk power
is undirected.

There are $d$ choices at each of the $k$ steps, so each vertex
starts $d^k$ walks and the walk power is $d^k$-regular.
If $A_{\mathcal E}x=\mu x$, then
$A_{\mathcal E}^2x=A_{\mathcal E}(\mu x)=\mu A_{\mathcal E}x=\mu^2x$,
and induction gives $A_{\mathcal E}^kx=\mu^kx$.
The orthonormal eigenbasis of $A_{\mathcal E}$ remains an eigenbasis
of $A_{\mathcal E}^k$. On each zero-sum basis vector, the new
eigenvalue satisfies $|\mu^k|=|\mu|^k\le\lambda^k$.
Consequently the walk power has nonconstant eigenvalue bound $\lambda^k$,
and the ratio of this bound to its degree is
$\lambda^k/d^k=(\lambda/d)^k$.
When $\lambda/d<1$, a fixed sufficiently large $k$ makes this ratio
smaller than any prescribed positive constant. The new degree
$d^k$ still does not depend on the number of vertices.

We use the following fixed family as the starting graph.
Here $\mathbb Z_\nu$ is the set of residues modulo $\nu$, so
$\mathbb Z_\nu^2$ is the set of $\nu^2$ ordered pairs of residues.

\begin{theorem}[Margulis--Gabber--Galil expanders
 {\cite[Construction~8.1 and Theorem~8.2]{HLW2006}}]
\label{thm:auxiliary-expander-family}
For every integer $\nu\ge1$, there is a deterministically
constructible eight-regular undirected multigraph on
$\mathbb Z_\nu^2$ whose nonconstant eigenvalues have absolute value
at most $5\sqrt2$. Its construction takes polynomial time in $\nu$.
\end{theorem}

Put $\lambda_0=5\sqrt2/8<1$. In the rest of this subsection,
$\mathcal E$ denotes the $k$th walk power of this graph, with
$M=\nu^2$ vertices and degree $d=8^k$.
Its nonconstant eigenvalue bound satisfies
$(5\sqrt2)^k=8^k(5\sqrt2/8)^k=d\lambda_0^k$.
Substituting this bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:expander-mixing} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-expander-mixing}
 e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)\ge\frac{d|S||T|}{M}
       -d\lambda_0^k\sqrt{|S||T|}
 =d\sqrt{|S||T|}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|S||T|}}{M}-\lambda_0^k\right).
\end{equation}
If $|S|,|T|\ge t>\lambda_0^kM$, then
$\sqrt{|S||T|}/M\ge t/M>\lambda_0^k$, so the right-hand side is
positive. Hence a step joins $S$ to $T$.

\subsubsection{Mapping to the prescribed vertex sets}

The auxiliary graph has a square number of vertices, whereas a
factor can have any size. We map the auxiliary vertices onto the
prescribed vertices and retain the images of its steps.
For a map $\pi:V(\mathcal E)\to U$ and a subset $X\subseteq U$,
the \emph{preimage} is
$\pi^{-1}(X)=\{x\in V(\mathcal E):\pi(x)\in X\}$.
The map is \emph{onto} if every vertex of $U$ has at least one
preimage. When $|U|=n\le M$, choose such a map by partitioning the
$M$ auxiliary vertices into $n$ groups of sizes
$\lfloor M/n\rfloor$ or $\lceil M/n\rceil$ and assigning one group
to each vertex of $U$. This gives
$1\le|\pi^{-1}(\{u\})|\le\lceil M/n\rceil$ for every $u\in U$.

Fix $k\ge1$ and use the following two constructions.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item\label{constr:one-set-replacement} \emph{One prescribed set.}
 Let $U$ have size $n\ge1$, put $M=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2$, and
 take the $M$-vertex graph $\mathcal E$ above. Choose an onto map
 $\pi:V(\mathcal E)\to U$ with the group sizes just specified.
 The replacement has vertex set $U$. Distinct vertices $u,v\in U$
 are adjacent if and only if some step $xy$ of $\mathcal E$ has
 $\pi(x)=u$ and $\pi(y)=v$. Steps whose endpoints have the same
 image give no edge, and repeated images give a single edge.
 \item\label{constr:two-side-replacement} \emph{Two prescribed sides.}
 Let $A,B$ be disjoint sets of sizes $n_A,n_B\ge1$, put
 $M=\lceil\sqrt{\max\{n_A,n_B\}}\rceil^2$, and take the
 $M$-vertex graph $\mathcal E$ above. Choose onto maps
 $\pi_A:V(\mathcal E)\to A$ and
 $\pi_B:V(\mathcal E)\to B$, with preimage sizes
 $\lfloor M/n_A\rfloor$ or $\lceil M/n_A\rceil$ for the first
 map, and $\lfloor M/n_B\rfloor$ or $\lceil M/n_B\rceil$ for
 the second. The replacement has vertex set $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$.
 Its edges are the pairs $\pi_A(x)\pi_B(y)$ arising from steps
 $xy$ of $\mathcal E$, keeping one edge for each distinct pair.
 There are no edges within either side. The two maps have the same
 domain, and their preimage groups may differ.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse replacements on prescribed vertex sets]
\label{lem:factor-expanders}
Fix an integer $k\ge1$. The graphs on $U$ and
$A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ in constructions~\ref{constr:one-set-replacement}
and~\ref{constr:two-side-replacement}, with the sizes $M,n,n_A,n_B$
specified there, are connected and simple and are constructible in
deterministic polynomial time.
The graph on $U$ has maximum degree at most
$8^k\lceil M/n\rceil$, and the bipartite replacement has maximum
degree at most
$8^k\max\{\lceil M/n_A\rceil,\lceil M/n_B\rceil\}$.
For every integer $t\ge1$ with $t>\lambda_0^kM$, the one-set
replacement has no two disjoint anticomplete $t$-subsets, and the
bipartite replacement has no anticomplete $t$-subsets on opposite
sides.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first bound degrees. In the one-set construction, every neighbor
of $u\in U$ is the image of a step starting at a vertex of
$\pi^{-1}(\{u\})$. Each such auxiliary vertex starts $d=8^k$
steps. Distinct steps may give the same output neighbor, so
$\deg(u)\le d|\pi^{-1}(\{u\})|\le8^k\lceil M/n\rceil$.
For $u\in A$ in the bipartite construction, the same argument uses
outgoing steps from $\pi_A^{-1}(\{u\})$, giving
$\deg(u)\le8^k\lceil M/n_A\rceil$.
For $v\in B$, use incoming steps at
$\pi_B^{-1}(\{v\})$. Each auxiliary vertex has $d$ incoming steps
because its adjacency matrix is symmetric and has row sum $d$.
Thus $\deg(v)\le8^k\lceil M/n_B\rceil$, proving both degree bounds.

We next verify the no-hole conditions. Fix an integer $t\ge1$ with
$t>\lambda_0^kM$.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For the one-set construction, take disjoint sets
 $X,Y\subseteq U$ with $|X|=|Y|=t$, and put
 $S=\pi^{-1}(X)$ and $T=\pi^{-1}(Y)$.
 Since $\pi$ is onto, each vertex of $X$ and $Y$ has a preimage,
 so $|S|\ge|X|=t$ and $|T|\ge|Y|=t$.
 The preimages are disjoint because $X,Y$ are disjoint and $\pi$
 assigns only one image to each auxiliary vertex.
 Equation~\eqref{eq:factor-expander-mixing} gives a step from $S$
 to $T$. Its endpoints map to a vertex of $X$ and a vertex of
 $Y$, which are distinct, so their pair is an output edge.
 \item For the bipartite construction, take
 $X\subseteq A$ and $Y\subseteq B$ with $|X|=|Y|=t$, and put
 $S=\pi_A^{-1}(X)$ and $T=\pi_B^{-1}(Y)$.
 Again, $|S|,|T|\ge t$ because both maps are onto, and
 \eqref{eq:factor-expander-mixing} gives a step $xy$ with
 $x\in S$ and $y\in T$. Its image
 $\pi_A(x)\pi_B(y)$ is an edge between $X$ and $Y$.
 Here $S,T$ may overlap, since they use different maps.
 Even a loop step $x=y$ gives a valid output edge, because its
 two images lie in the disjoint sets $A,B$.
\end{enumerate}

For connectedness, we first justify two properties of the
auxiliary graph $\mathcal E$. Its nonconstant eigenvalue bound is
$d\lambda_0^k<d$. If $\mathcal E$ were disconnected, let $S$ be a
nonempty proper connected component. Regularity and the absence of
steps crossing its boundary give
$A_{\mathcal E}\mathbf1_S=d\mathbf1_S$.
Put $x=\mathbf1_S-(|S|/M)\mathbf1$. Since $0<|S|<M$, this vector
is nonzero, and its coordinate sum is $|S|-(|S|/M)M=0$. Moreover,
\[
 A_{\mathcal E}x
 =d\mathbf1_S-\frac{|S|}{M}d\mathbf1
 =d\left(\mathbf1_S-\frac{|S|}{M}\mathbf1\right)=dx.
\]
This contradicts the nonconstant eigenvalue bound, so
$\mathcal E$ is connected.
If it were bipartite, with parts $S,T$, regularity would give
$d|S|=d|T|$ by counting the steps across the two sides.
Let $x$ take value one on $S$ and minus one on $T$. It would
therefore have zero sum. Every step goes to the opposite part, so
each coordinate of $A_{\mathcal E}x$ would be $-d$ times the
corresponding coordinate of $x$. The eigenvalue $-d$ would also
contradict the bound.
Thus $\mathcal E$ is nonbipartite and has an odd cycle, with a loop
counted as a cycle of length one.

In the one-set construction, take any two output vertices $u,v$
and choose preimages $x,y$. A walk from $x$ to $y$ exists in
$\mathcal E$. Apply $\pi$ to its vertex sequence. Consecutive
images are equal or adjacent in the output. Omitting consecutive
repetitions gives a walk from $u$ to $v$, proving connectedness.

For the bipartite construction, fix an auxiliary vertex $x$.
There is an odd closed walk from $x$ to itself: follow a path to
an odd cycle, traverse that cycle, and return along the reverse
path. The two path lengths add to an even number, so the total
length is odd. For every auxiliary vertex $y$, start with any
walk from $x$ to $y$. Prepending the odd closed walk changes its
length parity. Hence there are both even-length and odd-length
walks from $x$ to $y$.
A walk $x=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_\ell=y$ maps to the alternating sequence
$\pi_A(x_0),\pi_B(x_1),\pi_A(x_2),\ldots$ in the output.
Each consecutive pair is an edge by construction, using the
reverse auxiliary step when the sequence goes from $B$ to $A$.
An even-length walk reaches $\pi_A(y)$, and an odd-length walk
reaches $\pi_B(y)$. Thus $\pi_A(x)$ is connected to both images of
every $y$. Since both maps are onto, this reaches every output
vertex, proving connectedness.

Both outputs are simple by their edge definitions.
If $L$ is the largest prescribed set size,
then $M=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil^2\le(\sqrt L+1)^2\le4L$, since
$L\ge1$. The initial eight-regular graph is constructible in
polynomial time, and its $k$th walk power has degree $8^k$.
For fixed $k$, taking this power and mapping its steps to the
prescribed sets therefore takes polynomial time.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A bounded-degree independent-set source under UGC}
\label{sec:bounded-degree-ugc-source}

We derive a bounded-degree version of the independent-set promise
used in Section~\ref{sec:ugc}: independent-set density close to
$1/2$ in the YES case and arbitrarily small constant density in
the NO case. Austrin, Khot, and Safra prove under UGC that independent
set in maximum-degree-$d$ graphs is hard to approximate within a factor
$\Theta(d/\log^2 d)$, using a randomized reduction
\cite[Theorem~1.2 and Section~5.2]{AKS2011}. Their reduction obtains
bounded degree by random edge sampling and removal of edges incident
to high-degree vertices
\cite[Section~4, Steps~2--3]{AKS2011}. We instead use their weighted
density construction~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{AKS2011} and give a
deterministic route to the fixed-gap bounded-degree promise below,
using spectral sparsification~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BSS2012}.

The derivation has three steps. We first remove loops while retaining
the weighted density promise. We then replace each vertex by
independent copies in proportion to its weight, obtaining a
uniform bound on weighted degrees. Finally, a sparse reweighted
graph preserves positive edge weight inside every vertex set above
a fixed size threshold. Discarding edges turns the weighted-degree
bound into an ordinary degree bound.

\subsubsection{The weighted density source and removal of loops}

Let $\Psi$ be the cumulative distribution function of a standard
normal variable, with mean zero and variance one. The value $\Psi^{-1}(s)$
is its $s$-quantile. For $-1<\xi<1$, take jointly normal variables
$X,Y$ of mean zero, variance one, and covariance $\xi$, and define
$\Gamma_\xi(s)=\Pr[X\le\Psi^{-1}(s),\,Y\le\Psi^{-1}(s)]$.
Set $\Gamma_\xi(0)=0$ and $\Gamma_\xi(1)=1$.
This function is positive for $0<s<1$, nondecreasing, and convex.
Indeed, differentiating the joint distribution gives
$\Gamma_\xi'(s)=2\Psi(\sqrt{(1-\xi)/(1+\xi)}\,\Psi^{-1}(s))$,
which is nonnegative and increasing. These are the properties used
in the density analysis of~\cite[Theorem~3.1 and equation~(3.2)]{AKS2011}.

A bipartite \emph{Unique Games instance} has left vertices $U$,
right vertices $V$, and a finite label set, called the
\emph{alphabet}, $[\ell]$. Each edge $uv$ carries a permutation
$\pi$ of $[\ell]$ and requires the label at $v$ to be $\pi$ applied
to the label at $u$. A \emph{labeling} assigns one label to every
vertex. Its value is the fraction of constraints it satisfies,
counting parallel constraints separately. The instance's optimum
is the largest such fraction.
Khot and Regev show that ordinary UGC is equivalent to the
following regular bipartite promise, which AKS use
\cite[Conjecture~2.4]{AKS2011}: for every fixed $\gamma>0$, it is
NP-hard to distinguish a YES instance in which there is a set
$V_0\subseteq V$ of at least $(1-\gamma)|V|$ right vertices and one
labeling satisfies every constraint incident to a vertex in $V_0$,
from a NO instance of value at most $\gamma$. The underlying
bipartite multigraph is regular on each side.

Fix a rational $p\in(0,1/2)$ and put $\xi=-p/(1-p)$.
Theorem~3.1 of~\cite{AKS2011} gives a deterministic polynomial-time
reduction, for fixed alphabet size, to a vertex- and edge-weighted
graph $G$ with both total weights equal to one. If $a_v$ is the
vertex weight, write $a(S)=\sum_{v\in S}a_v$. The weighted degree
of $v$, the sum of its incident edge weights, is $2a_v$. For
$S\subseteq V(G)$, let $e_G(S)$ be the total weight of edges with
both endpoints in $S$. In the YES case,
$G$ has an independent set of weight at least $p-\gamma$. In the
NO case, $e_G(S)\ge\Gamma_\xi(a(S))-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}$ for every
$S\subseteq V(G)$. Here $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}>0$ is an arbitrarily small fixed analytic error
and the Unique Games error $\gamma$ is chosen sufficiently small. We allow loops in this
intermediate graph, counting a loop once in $e_G(S)$ and twice in
weighted degree.

We remove loops before replacing vertices by independent copies so that the NO-case
argument can compare internal edge weights through independent
choices of distinct original vertices. The following alphabet
padding makes the total loop weight arbitrarily small.

\begin{lemma}[Removing loops by alphabet padding]\label{lem:aks-loop-removal}
For every fixed $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}>0$, a fixed alphabet padding followed by the
AKS construction and deletion of all loops gives a loopless graph $G^-$
with vertex weights $a_v$ of total one, total edge weight at most
one, and weighted degrees at most $2a_v$.
Its YES independent-set weight is at least $p-\gamma$, and in the
NO case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aks-loop-free-density}
 e_{G^-}(S)\ge\Gamma_\xi(a(S))-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}
 \qquad(S\subseteq V(G^-)).
\end{equation}
The reduction is deterministic and polynomial for fixed parameters.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The edge distribution in~\cite[proof of Theorem~3.1]{AKS2011} first chooses two
constraints incident with the same left vertex, then samples
independent coordinate pairs $(x_j,y_j)$ with probabilities
$\Pr[x_j=0,y_j=0]=1-2p$,
$\Pr[x_j=0,y_j=1]=\Pr[x_j=1,y_j=0]=p$, and
$\Pr[x_j=1,y_j=1]=0$.
The endpoint strings are then permuted by the two constraints.
To control possible loops even when the Unique Games instance has
parallel constraints with different permutations, we first pad its
alphabet from $[\ell]$ to $[\ell]\times[t]$, replacing every
constraint $\pi$ by $(i,j)\mapsto(\pi(i),j)$.
Projecting any satisfying padded label pair to its first coordinate
shows that padding cannot increase the NO optimum. A YES labeling
extends by giving every labeled vertex the same second coordinate.
Regularity and the YES fraction are unchanged.

The coordinates $[\ell]\times\{j\}$, for a fixed $j\in[t]$,
form one coordinate block. Put $c=p^2+(1-p)^2<1$. Conditional on any choice of the two
constraints, a loop requires equality of the two permuted strings
in every one of the $t$ coordinate blocks. In each block, full
string equality implies equality at a fixed output coordinate. If
the two permutations select the same input coordinate there, equality
has probability $1-2p$. If they select different input coordinates,
the bits are independent with marginal probability $p$, so equality
has probability $c=p^2+(1-p)^2$. Both bounds are at most $c$.
The coordinate blocks are independent, so the probability of full
string equality, and hence the total loop weight, is at most $c^t$.
Choose a fixed $t$ with $c^t<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}$, and delete all loops.
This loses at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}$ internal edge weight in any set,
preserves the YES independent set, and does not increase weighted
degrees. Alphabet padding preserves the source promise and
regularity, so the construction above has the stated bounds.
All operations are deterministic, and $t$ is fixed.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Vertex unweighting and uniform degree bounds}

Write the padded alphabet size as $R$ and the fixed bias as
$p=a_0/a_1$, with positive integers $a_0<a_1$. For a binary
string $x\in\{0,1\}^R$, let $|x|$ be its
number of coordinates equal to one. The vertex weights in the AKS
construction, indexed by right vertices $v\in V$ and strings $x$,
have the form
\[
 a_{(v,x)}=
 \frac{a_0^{|x|}(a_1-a_0)^{R-|x|}}{|V|a_1^R},
 \qquad v\in V,\quad x\in\{0,1\}^R.
\]
Thus $N_{\mathrm{cl}}=|V|a_1^R$ is polynomial in the Unique Games
instance, since $a_1$ and $R$ are fixed. Replace each original vertex
$u$ by $m_u=N_{\mathrm{cl}}a_u$ independent copies, called
\emph{clones}, labeled
$(u,1),\ldots,(u,m_u)$. These copies form the \emph{clone block} of $u$.
If several source edges join the same pair of vertices, combine their
weights. For every resulting edge $uv$ of weight $w_{uv}$ in $G^-$,
join each clone of $u$ to each clone of $v$ by an edge of weight
$w_{uv}/(m_um_v)$. There are no other edges.
Denote this edge-weighted, vertex-unweighted
graph by $G_1$. It has $N_{\mathrm{cl}}$ vertices, total edge weight
at most one, and weighted degree at most $2/N_{\mathrm{cl}}$ at each vertex.

A clone of $u$ has weighted degree
$\deg_{G^-}(u)/m_u\le2a_u/(N_{\mathrm{cl}}a_u)=2/N_{\mathrm{cl}}$.
The YES independent set becomes one of the same normalized size by
selecting all its clone blocks.

\begin{lemma}[Density after vertex unweighting]\label{lem:aks-clone-density}
If $G^-$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:aks-loop-free-density}, then its
clone graph $G_1$ satisfies, for every $S'\subseteq V(G_1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aks-clone-density}
 e_{G_1}(S')\ge\Gamma_\xi(|S'|/N_{\mathrm{cl}})
 -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take any $S'\subseteq V(G_1)$ and let $f_u$ be the fraction of
clones of $u$ in $S'$. Include each original vertex $u$ in a random
set $S$ independently with probability $f_u$. Since $a_u=m_u/N_{\mathrm{cl}}$,
\[
 \mathbb E[a(S)]=\sum_u a_uf_u
 =\frac1{N_{\mathrm{cl}}}\sum_u m_uf_u=\frac{|S'|}{N_{\mathrm{cl}}}.
\]
There are no loops in $G^-$. Thus the two endpoints of each edge
are chosen independently, and
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{G^-}(S)]
 =\sum_{uv}w_{uv}f_uf_v
 =e_{G_1}(S').
\]
Taking expectations in~\eqref{eq:aks-loop-free-density} and using
convexity of $\Gamma_\xi$ gives
\[
 e_{G_1}(S')=\mathbb E[e_{G^-}(S)]
 \ge\mathbb E[\Gamma_\xi(a(S))]
   -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}
 \ge\Gamma_\xi(|S'|/N_{\mathrm{cl}})
   -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}.
\]
This is the argument of~\cite[Section~4, Step~1]{AKS2011}, applied
after the loop removal of Lemma~\ref{lem:aks-loop-removal}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Deterministic sparsification of a dense-set promise}

To obtain bounded ordinary degree, we sparsify the weighted graph
and discard edges of very small weight. There will be few enough
edges that discarding them loses little total weight. Every remaining
edge then has a lower weight bound, so a bound on weighted degree
limits the number of incident edges. We use spectral sparsification
to preserve the internal weight of large vertex sets throughout this
process.

For a loopless weighted graph $G$,
let $w_{uv}$ be the total weight of edges between distinct vertices
$u,v$. Its \emph{Laplacian} is the symmetric matrix with
$L_G(u,u)=\deg_G(u)$ and $L_G(u,v)=-w_{uv}$ for $u\ne v$.
Expanding the quadratic form gives
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}L_Gx
 =\sum_{uv\in E(G)}w_{uv}(x_u-x_v)^2.
\]
For a vertex set $S$, let $\operatorname{vol}_G(S)$ be the sum of
its vertices' weighted degrees, and let $w(\cut_G(S))$ be the
weight of edges with one endpoint in $S$. Thus
$\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}L_G\mathbf1_S=w(\cut_G(S))$ and
$\mathbf1_{\{v\}}^{\mathsf T}L_G\mathbf1_{\{v\}}=\deg_G(v)$.
The notation $L_G\preceq L_H$ means that
$x^{\mathsf T}L_Gx\le x^{\mathsf T}L_Hx$ for every vector $x$.
A \emph{spectral sparsifier} is a weighted subgraph with fewer
edges whose Laplacian preserves these quadratic forms within
multiplicative bounds. Finally,
$2e_G(S)=\operatorname{vol}_G(S)-w(\cut_G(S))$: an internal edge
contributes twice to the volume, while a crossing edge contributes
once to both the volume and the cut.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:deterministic-density-sparsification}
Fix positive constants $C_0,\zeta,\eta$. Let $G$ be a loopless
edge-weighted graph on $N\ge1$ vertices of total edge weight at most one
and weighted degree at most $C_0/N$ at every vertex. Suppose that
every set of at least $\eta N$ vertices has internal edge weight
at least $\zeta$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time
construction of a simple unweighted graph $J$ with
$V(J)=V(G)$ and $E(J)\subseteq E(G)$, whose
maximum degree is bounded by a constant depending only on
$C_0,\zeta$, and whose independent sets all have size less than
$\eta N$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a fixed rational $0<\theta\le\min\{1/12,\zeta/14\}$,
and put $q=\theta^{-2}$ and $d=\lceil q\rceil$. The spectral
sparsification theorem of Batson, Spielman, and
Srivastava~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BSS2012} states that for every $q>1$ there is a
weighted subgraph $G'$ on $V(G)$ with at most
$\lceil q(N-1)\rceil\le dN$ edges and
\[
 L_G\preceq L_{G'}\preceq
 \frac{q+1+2\sqrt q}{q+1-2\sqrt q}L_G.
\]
Substituting $q=\theta^{-2}$ gives
\[
 \frac{q+1+2\sqrt q}{q+1-2\sqrt q}
 =\left(\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\right)^2
 =1+\frac{4\theta}{(1-\theta)^2}
 \le1+\frac{576}{121}\theta<1+7\theta,
\]
since $\theta\le1/12$.

Applying this sandwich to $\mathbf1_{\{v\}}$ gives
$\deg_G(v)\le\deg_{G'}(v)\le(1+7\theta)C_0/N$. Summing the lower
bounds over $v\in S$ gives
$\operatorname{vol}_{G'}(S)\ge\operatorname{vol}_G(S)$.
Applying the sandwich to $\mathbf1_S$ gives
$w(\cut_{G'}(S))\le(1+7\theta)w(\cut_G(S))$. Every cut has weight at most the total edge weight, so
$w(\cut_G(S))\le1$. Also $\theta\le\zeta/14$ gives
$7\theta/2\le\zeta/4$. Therefore, whenever $|S|\ge\eta N$,
\begin{align*}
 2e_{G'}(S)&=\operatorname{vol}_{G'}(S)-w(\cut_{G'}(S))\\
 &\ge\operatorname{vol}_G(S)-(1+7\theta)w(\cut_G(S))\\
 &=2e_G(S)-7\theta w(\cut_G(S)),\\
 e_{G'}(S)&\ge e_G(S)-\tfrac{7\theta}{2}w(\cut_G(S))
 \ge\zeta-\tfrac{7\theta}{2}\ge3\zeta/4.
\end{align*}
Write $w'_e$ for the weight of $e\in E(G')$. Define the unweighted
graph $J$ by $V(J)=V(G)$ and
$E(J)=\{e\in E(G'):w'_e\ge\zeta/(4dN)\}$.
The edges in $E(G')\setminus E(J)$ have total weight at most
$|E(G')|\zeta/(4dN)\le\zeta/4$.
Thus every $S\subseteq V(G)$ with $|S|\ge\eta N$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_{\substack{e\in E(J)\\e\subseteq S}}w'_e
 \ge e_{G'}(S)-\sum_{e\in E(G')\setminus E(J)}w'_e
 \ge\frac{3\zeta}{4}-\frac\zeta4=\frac\zeta2>0.
\]
Consequently $J[S]$ contains an edge.
Thus every independent set of $J$ has size less than $\eta N$.

For $v\in V(J)$, every $e\in E(J)$ incident with $v$ has
$w'_e\ge\zeta/(4dN)$. Hence
\[
 \deg_J(v)\le\frac{4dN}{\zeta}\deg_{G'}(v)
 \le\frac{4(1+7\theta)C_0d}{\zeta},
\]
a constant depending only on $C_0,\zeta$. The construction is
deterministic and polynomial time by the
cited theorem.
\end{proof}

Choose the errors in loop removal and sparsification smaller than
the positive density bound $\Gamma_\xi(\eta)$. Then every set of at
least $\eta N$ vertices still contains an edge in the NO case. In
the YES case, deleting edges preserves the independent set obtained
after vertex unweighting. These choices yield the following source
promise for the UGC applications.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-degree independent-set source]
\label{thm:bounded-degree-kr-source}
For every fixed rational $0<\eta<1/10$, there is a constant
$D_\eta$ such that the ordinary UGC gives a deterministic
polynomial-time hardness reduction to simple unweighted graphs
$G$ of maximum degree at most $D_\eta$, with
$\alphaI(G)\ge(1/2-\eta)|V(G)|$ in the YES case and
$\alphaI(G)<\eta|V(G)|$ in the NO case.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $p=1/2-\eta/4$ and $\xi=-p/(1-p)$. Fix a positive rational
$g$ with $4g<\Gamma_\xi(\eta)$. Use analytic error
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dens}}\le g$ and loop-loss budget
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loop}}=g$, and choose the Unique Games error sufficiently
small for AKS Theorem~3.1 and for $\gamma\le\eta/4$.
Pad the alphabet with a fixed $t$ satisfying
$(p^2+(1-p)^2)^t<g$. After dropping loops and replacing vertices by
copies, the YES independent-set density is at least
$p-\gamma\ge1/2-\eta/2$. In the NO case,
equation~\eqref{eq:aks-clone-density} and monotonicity show that
every set of at least $\eta N$ vertices has internal weight
greater than $\Gamma_\xi(\eta)-2g>2g$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:deterministic-density-sparsification} with
$C_0=2$ and $\zeta=g$. Its output retains the YES
independent set and has no NO independent set of size $\eta N$.
Its degree bound depends only on $\eta$.
All alphabet parameters are constants for the fixed promise, so
the whole reduction is deterministic and polynomial time.
\end{proof}
