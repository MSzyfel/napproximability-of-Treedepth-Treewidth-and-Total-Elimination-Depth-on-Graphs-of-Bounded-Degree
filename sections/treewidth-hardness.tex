\subsection{Treewidth}
\label{sec:tw-transfer}

We prove UGC-hardness below $4/3$ by transferring a balanced,
unweighted tripartite Vertex Cover gap to treewidth. We derive this
gap from Khot--Regev using a multipartite product; B\'erczi,
Chandrasekaran, Kir\'aly, Lee, and Xu prove a related weighted
$k$-partite Vertex Cover gap under UGC~\cite[Section~3.5]{BCKLX2017}.
The same transfer gives the NP-hardness bound in
Section~\ref{subsec:np-treewidth}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tw-ugc}
Under the ordinary Unique Games Conjecture, approximating treewidth
within any fixed factor $1\leq r<4/3$ is hard, even on simple
unweighted graphs with a specified partition into three equal cliques.
\end{theorem}

The transfer uses Bonnet's completion of three parts to cliques
and his central-bag lower bound~\cite[Lemma~6]{BonnetTreewidth}.
We take disjoint source copies \emph{before} completing the three global parts.
This lets each of three paths attached to a central bag process
the copies one at a time: the additional bag size is bounded by one part of one copy,
while the cover contribution grows with the number of copies.

\subsubsection{A transfer from vertex cover to treewidth}

Let $G$ be a nonempty tripartite graph with specified independent
parts $V(G)=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\mathbin{\dot\cup}C$, and put
$n_{\max}=\max\{|A|,|B|,|C|\}$. Thus $G$ has no edge within a part.
For an integer $R\ge1$, take copies $G_1,\ldots,G_R$ on
$V(G)\times\{1\},\ldots,V(G)\times\{R\}$, writing $v_j=(v,j)$.
For $X\in\{A,B,C\}$, let $X_j=X\times\{j\}$ and
$X^*=X\times[R]$. Define $K_R(G)$ on $V(G)\times[R]$ by
writing $\binom{X^*}{2}$ for the set of unordered pairs of distinct
vertices in $X^*$:
\[
 E(K_R(G))=
 \bigcup_{X\in\{A,B,C\}}\binom{X^*}{2}
 \;\cup\;\bigcup_{j=1}^R\{\{u_j,v_j\}:uv\in E(G)\}.
\]
These are all the edges: each global part is a
clique, and vertices in different global parts are adjacent if and only if
they belong to the same copy and their source vertices are
adjacent in $G$.

Its vertex partition into three cliques makes $K_R(G)$
\emph{co-tripartite}.

\begin{lemma}[A bag covering the source edges]\label{lem:tw-central-bag}
Every tree decomposition of $K_R(G)$ has a bag containing a vertex
cover of $G_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}G_R$.
In particular, that bag has size at least $R\vc(G)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We apply the cover argument from~\cite[proof of Lemma~6]{BonnetTreewidth}
to the disjoint union of the $R$ source copies.
Fix a tree decomposition with underlying tree $T$ and bags $B_u$.
For each $X\in\{A,B,C\}$, choose a node $u_X$ whose bag contains the global
clique $X^*$. The bag subtrees of its vertices intersect pairwise,
since every pair is an edge. Pairwise-intersecting subtrees of a tree
have a common node (the Helly property), so some bag contains all of
$X^*$~\cite[Lemma~4]{BonnetTreewidth}.
An empty part can be assigned any node. Let $u$ be the median of
$u_A,u_B,u_C$, namely the unique common node of their three pairwise
connecting paths.

Consider an edge $xy$ of one of the original copies, with $x\in X^*$
and $y\in Y^*$ for different parts $X,Y$. If either $u_X$ or $u_Y$
equals $u$, the bag at $u$ already contains the corresponding endpoint.
Otherwise, $u_X$ and $u_Y$ lie in different components of $T-u$.
If neither endpoint belonged to the bag at $u$, the connected set of
bags containing $x$ would stay in the component containing $u_X$,
and the analogous set for $y$ would stay in the component containing
$u_Y$. These sets could not intersect, contrary to the existence of
a bag covering $xy$. Thus $B_u$ contains an endpoint of every
original edge. The disjoint union of the $R$ copies has vertex-cover number
$R\vc(G)$, so this bag has size at least $R\vc(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A decomposition from a source cover]\label{lem:tw-cover-decomposition}
For every vertex cover $S$ of $G$, the graph $K_R(G)$ has a tree
decomposition whose bags have size at most $R\max\{|S|,n_{\max}\}+n_{\max}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We adapt the three-path decomposition of~\cite[Section~3.3]{BonnetTreewidth}
by processing copies separately, so the temporary bag increase is at
most $n_{\max}$, independently of $R$.
Put $I=V(G)\setminus S$.
Let $S_j,I_j$ be its copies. Start with the central bag
$S^*=\bigcup_{j=1}^R S_j$, of size $R|S|$. Attach three paths to this
node, one for each part $X$. The path to $X$ ends with the bag $X^*$.
On this path process the copies in order $j=1,\ldots,R$. At step $j$,
first add all vertices of $I_j\cap X_j$, and then remove all vertices
of $S_j\setminus X_j$.

After processing the first $j$ copies, the bag is
\[
 B^X_j=\bigcup_{i\le j}X_i\;\cup\!\bigcup_{i>j}S_i
 \qquad(0\le j\le R).
\]
Here $B^X_0=S^*$ and $B^X_R=X^*$. Between $B^X_{j-1}$ and
$B^X_j$ insert the bag $B^X_{j-1}\cup(I_j\cap X_j)$.
The bags after completed steps have size
$j|X|+(R-j)|S|\le R\max\{|S|,|X|\}$. An inserted bag has at most
$|I\cap X|\le n_{\max}$ additional vertices. Every bag therefore has
size at most $R\max\{|S|,n_{\max}\}+n_{\max}$.

We verify the decomposition axioms. Every vertex is present in its
part's final bag. A vertex in $S_j\cap X_j$ occurs at the center and
throughout the $X$-path, and on each other path it is removed once.
A vertex in $I_j\cap X_j$ occurs only on the $X$-path, from its
addition to the end. Thus each vertex's bag occurrences form a
connected subtree. Every edge within a global clique is covered at
the corresponding final bag. An original cross-part edge with both
endpoints in $S^*$ is covered at the center. Both endpoints cannot
belong to $I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_R$, since each $I_j$ is independent
and original edges stay inside a copy. Finally, if one endpoint is
in $I_j\cap X_j$ and the other in $S_j\setminus X_j$, the latter
is still present when the former is added, so the edge is covered
on the $X$-path. All edges are covered, and the bag-size bound follows from the count above.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Replicated co-tripartite completion]\label{lem:tw-replication}
For every such $G$ and every integer $R\geq1$,
\[
 R\max\{\vc(G),n_{\max}\}-1
 \ \leq\ \operatorname{tw}(K_R(G))
 \ \leq\ R\max\{\vc(G),n_{\max}\}+n_{\max}-1.
\]
\end{lemma}


\begin{proof}
Every tree decomposition has a bag containing the largest global
clique, so its largest bag has size at least $Rn_{\max}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-central-bag}, it also has a bag of size at least
$R\vc(G)$. Therefore its largest bag has size at least
$R\max\{\vc(G),n_{\max}\}$, and subtracting one gives the lower
bound on treewidth. For the upper bound, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-cover-decomposition} to a minimum cover of $G$:
its largest bag has size at most
$R\max\{\vc(G),n_{\max}\}+n_{\max}$. Subtracting one gives the
stated upper bound.
\end{proof}

For fixed $R$, the construction has $R|V(G)|$ vertices and is
polynomial in the source size.

\subsubsection{The approximation gap}

For the UGC reduction we need cover thresholds $(1/2+\zeta)N$ and
$(2/3-\zeta)N$ on balanced tripartite graphs. More generally,
B\'erczi, Chandrasekaran, Kir\'aly, Lee, and Xu prove that for every fixed $k\geq3$ and sufficiently
small fixed $\epsilon,\eta,\beta>0$, it is UGC-hard to distinguish weighted $k$-partite
Vertex Cover instances with optimum at most
$k(1+\epsilon)/2+k\eta$ from instances with optimum at least
$(k-1)(1-\epsilon)(1-\beta)$; as $\epsilon,\eta,\beta$ tend to zero,
the gap ratio tends to $2(k-1)/k$~\cite[Section~3.5]{BCKLX2017}.
We derive the balanced, unweighted form needed here from the
Khot--Regev independent-set promise using the product below. Its degree
identity will also be used in
Section~\ref{sec:bounded-degree-hardness}.

\begin{lemma}[Multipartite product]\label{lem:multipartite-product}
For a simple graph $G$ on $n$ vertices and a positive integer $k$,
let $P_k(G)$ have vertex
set $V(G)\times[k]$, with $u_i v_j$ an edge if and only if
$i\ne j$ and $uv\in E(G)$. Its $k$ layers are independent
sets of size $n$. Moreover,
$k\alphaI(G)\le\alphaI(P_k(G))\le n+(k-1)\alphaI(G)$ and
$\Delta(P_k(G))=(k-1)\Delta(G)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All $k$ copies of an independent set of $G$ form an independent
set of $P_k(G)$, proving the first bound. For the upper bound, let
$I$ be any independent set in the product. Let $m_v$ count the copies
of $v$ in $I$, and put $D=\{v:m_v\ge2\}$. If distinct $u,v\in D$
were adjacent in $G$, choose a copy $u_i\in I$. The set $I$ contains
at least two copies of $v$, so one has a layer index $j\ne i$. Then
$u_iv_j$ would be an edge inside $I$. Thus $D$ is independent, and
$|I|=\sum_vm_v\le n+(k-1)|D|\le n+(k-1)\alphaI(G)$.
Each copy of a vertex has one neighbor in each of the other
$k-1$ layers for every source neighbor, giving the degree identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Balanced unweighted multipartite cover gap from Khot--Regev]
\label{thm:ugc-tripartite-cover}
For every fixed integer $k\geq3$ and fixed rational
$0<\zeta<1/12$, it is Unique Games-hard
to distinguish simple unweighted $k$-partite graphs $G$ on $N$
vertices, with a specified partition into $k$ parts of size $N/k$,
with $\vc(G)\leq(1/2+\zeta)N$ \textup{(YES)} from those with
$\vc(G)\geq(1-1/k-\zeta)N$ \textup{(NO)}.
\end{theorem}

The promise follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:kr} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:multipartite-product}. Appendix~\ref{app:multipartite-normalization}
records the parameter substitution. Taking $k=3$ and applying the
replication lemma proves the treewidth gap.



\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-ugc}]
Fix $1\le r<4/3$. Since $2/3>r/2$, choose a fixed rational
$0<\zeta<1/12$ and then a fixed integer $R\ge1$ such that
$2/3-\zeta>r(1/2+\zeta+1/(3R))$.
Take the $N$-vertex source from Theorem~\ref{thm:ugc-tripartite-cover}
with $k=3$. Its parts have size $N/3$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:tw-replication} gives
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \textup{YES:}&\operatorname{tw}(K_R(G))
       \le R(1/2+\zeta)N+N/3-1,\\
 \textup{NO:}&\operatorname{tw}(K_R(G))
       \ge R(2/3-\zeta)N-1.
 \end{array}
\]
The NO lower bound minus $r$ times the YES upper bound is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &[R(2/3-\zeta)N-1]
   -r[R(1/2+\zeta)N+N/3-1]\\
 &=R\bigl(2/3-\zeta-r(1/2+\zeta+1/(3R))\bigr)N+r-1>0,
\end{aligned}
\]
because the parameter choice makes the coefficient of $N$ positive
and $r-1\ge0$. Thus an $r$-approximation
distinguishes the source cases. The construction is polynomial,
and its three specified cliques each have size $RN/3$. The same
comparison, omitting the final $r-1$, gives the gap for maximum
bag size.
\end{proof}
