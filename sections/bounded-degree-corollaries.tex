\subsection{Hardness consequences}

The dense reductions give YES and NO bounds on the same scale:
linear in the output order $N$ for treedepth and treewidth, and
quadratic in $N$ for total depth. The sparsification theorems above
preserve the YES bounds and decrease the NO bounds by at most
$\tau N$ or $\tau N^2$, respectively. After division by the
corresponding scale, the loss is at most $\tau$ and can be made
arbitrarily small. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} therefore
preserves every factor below the dense hardness threshold, after
choosing $\tau$ sufficiently small and the source sufficiently large.
The resulting maximum degree is constant for this choice of
parameters. This standard argument gives the following three
corollaries. The factor checks and detailed calculations are in
Appendix~\ref{app:bounded-degree-hardness}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree treedepth hardness]
\label{cor:td-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:np-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:sse-bounded-degree}
For each fixed $r\ge1$, approximating treedepth within factor $r$
on simple unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree is
NP-hard if $r<18/17$, Unique Games-hard if $r<4/3$, and
SSE-hard for every fixed $r$.
The degree bound depends only on the excluded factor. All reductions
are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

The NP-hardness assertion answers the bounded-degree conjecture of
Schirrmacher, Siebertz, and
Vigny~\cite[Conjecture~1]{SchirrmacherSiebertzVigny2025}.
Weighted treedepth hardness at maximum degree ten is proved
in~\cite{DirksEtAl2025Weighted}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree treewidth hardness]
\label{cor:tw-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-sse-bounded-degree}
For each fixed $r\ge1$, approximating treewidth within factor $r$
on simple unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree is
NP-hard if $r<12/11$, Unique Games-hard if $r<4/3$, and
SSE-hard for every fixed $r$.
The degree bound depends only on the excluded factor. All reductions
are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

The SSE treewidth hardness at every fixed factor on unrestricted
graphs is due to Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{APW}. The bounded-degree
statement here follows by applying our treewidth sparsification
theorem to the line-graph construction.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree total-depth hardness]
\label{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:np-total-depth-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:total-depth-ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{cor:total-depth-sse-bounded-degree}
There is an absolute constant $\epsilon_0>0$ such that approximating
total depth within factor $1+\epsilon_0$ is NP-hard on simple
unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree. On such graphs,
total depth is also Unique Games-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor $1\le r<8/7$, and SSE-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor $r\ge1$. The degree bound depends only on the excluded
factor. All reductions are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Regular graphs and subdivisions}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:regularization}
For every finite simple graph $G$ of order $n$ and fixed odd $d\geq3$ with
$d>\Delta(G)$, one can construct in polynomial time a simple
$d$-regular graph $R$ containing $G$ as an induced subgraph, with
$(d+2)n\leq |V(R)|\leq(d+3)n$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \td(G)&\leq\td(R)\leq\td(G)+d+2,\\
 \operatorname{tw}(G)&\leq\operatorname{tw}(R)
 \leq\max\{\operatorname{tw}(G),d+2\},\\
 (d+2)\totd(G)&\leq\totd(R)
 \leq(d+3)\totd(G)+\tfrac12(d+2)(d+3)n.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each $v\in V(G)$, set $t=d-\deg_G(v)$, so $1\le t\le d$.
Take graphs $Q_v$ whose vertex sets are pairwise disjoint and
disjoint from $V(G)$. Choose $P_v\subseteq V(Q_v)$ with $|P_v|=t$,
and join $v$ to every vertex of $P_v$. Define $Q_v$ as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $t$ is even, take $K_{d+1}$ and remove a perfect matching
   on $P_v$.
 \item If $t\ge3$ is odd, take $K_{d+2}$ and remove a cycle with
   vertex set $P_v$ and a perfect matching on $V(Q_v)\setminus P_v$.
   The latter set has even size $d+2-t$.
 \item If $t=1$, write $P_v=\{p\}$, take $K_{d+2}$, and choose
   distinct $a,b\in V(Q_v)\setminus\{p\}$. Remove $pa,pb$ and a
   perfect matching on $V(Q_v)\setminus\{p,a,b\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $R$ be the graph obtained from $G$ by these attachments.
Every vertex in $P_v$ has degree $d-1$ inside $Q_v$ and gains one
edge to $v$. Every vertex of $V(Q_v)\setminus P_v$ has degree $d$
inside $Q_v$, and $v$ gains $t=d-\deg_G(v)$ neighbors. Thus all
degrees in $R$ are $d$.
Each $Q_v$ has minimum degree $d-1$ and at most $d+2$ vertices.
Every connected component therefore has at least $d$ vertices,
so $Q_v$ is connected because
$2d>d+2$ for $d\geq3$. The resulting graph $R$ is simple.

Appending $V(Q_v)$ as a chain below $v$ proves the upper bounds
for height and total depth. Attach a bag $\{v\}\cup V(Q_v)$ to an
existing bag containing $v$ in a tree decomposition of $G$.
Doing this for every $v$ proves the treewidth bound.

For the total-depth lower bound, partition $V(R)$ into connected sets
$X_v=\{v\}\cup V(Q_v)$. Fix an elimination forest $F$ of $R$.
The shallowest
vertex $r_v$ of $X_v$ is an ancestor of every vertex in $X_v$.
To see this, take a path in $X_v$ starting at $r_v$. If a next vertex
is a descendant of the current one, it remains below $r_v$. If it is
an ancestor of the current one, it lies on the path from the root to
the current vertex and cannot be above $r_v$, by the choice of $r_v$.
Thus every vertex of $X_v$ is below $r_v$. For every source edge $uv$,
the endpoints $u,v$ are comparable in the forest. Since $r_u$ and
$r_v$ are ancestors of $u$ and $v$, they are both ancestors of the
deeper endpoint and are therefore comparable. Restrict the forest to
the representatives $r_v$ and rename each $r_v$ as $v$. The result
$\overline F$ is an elimination forest of $G$. For every $x\in X_v$,
$\operatorname{depth}_F(x)\ge\operatorname{depth}_F(r_v)
\ge\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)$, so
\[
 \totd(F)\geq\sum_v|X_v|\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)
 \geq(d+2)\totd(\overline F)\geq(d+2)\totd(G).
\]
This argument also gives a polynomial-time projection of forests.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:regular-hardness}
The hardness ranges in Corollaries~\ref{cor:td-bounded-degree},
\ref{cor:tw-bounded-degree}, and~\ref{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree}
also hold on $d$-regular graphs for a suitable fixed odd degree $d$
depending only on the excluded factor. The NP-hardness constant for
total depth may decrease.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a target factor $r$ and a larger factor $r'$ in the same
hardness range. For NP-hardness of total depth, take
$1<r<r'<1+\epsilon_0$.
Choose an odd $d$ larger than the maximum degree of the
bounded-degree source family. We may increase $d$ further, then
require a sufficiently large source order $n$, using the
enlargements in Appendix~\ref{app:bounded-degree-hardness}.

For treedepth and treewidth, Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization}
changes the promise bounds by at most the fixed constant $d+2$.
Their linear scale therefore allows
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} to preserve the factor-$r$ gap.

For total depth, divide the regularized cost by $d+2$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization} gives
\[
 \totd(G)\le\frac{\totd(R)}{d+2}
 \le\left(1+\frac1{d+2}\right)\totd(G)+\frac{d+3}{2}n.
\]
In the promise reductions, the YES bound is quadratic in $n$.
First increasing $d$ makes the multiplicative error small, and
then increasing $n$ makes the additional linear term small relative
to that bound. The NO bound does not decrease. The gap part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} therefore applies.

For the NP total-depth family, the optimum itself is bounded
below by a fixed positive multiple of $n^2$, as shown in
Appendix~\ref{app:bounded-degree-total-depth}. Together with the
chain upper bound $\totd(G)\le n(n+1)/2$, this makes the normalized
error an arbitrarily small fraction of that lower bound for large
$d$ and then large $n$.
The projection in Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization}
converts every forest of $R$ of cost $z$ into one of $G$ of cost
at most $z/(d+2)$. Hence the solution part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} converts an $r$-approximation
on regular graphs into an approximation within the excluded
factor $r'$ on the source family.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:subdivision-hardness}
For every fixed $g\geq3$, the treedepth and treewidth hardness ranges above
hold on bounded-degree graphs that are bipartite, $2$-degenerate, and
have girth at least $g$. Here $2$-degenerate means that every
nonempty subgraph has a vertex of degree at most two, and girth is the
length of a shortest cycle (infinite for an acyclic graph). On the same class, total depth is
SSE-hard to approximate within every fixed factor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replace every edge of $G$ by a path of fixed even length
$\ell\geq\max\{2,g/3\}$, obtaining $G_\ell$. All original vertices
lie in one side of a bipartition, since every replacement path has
even length. Each internal vertex has degree two, and deleting all
internal vertices leaves the original vertices isolated, so
$G_\ell$ is $2$-degenerate. Every cycle uses at least three
replacement paths and therefore has length at least $3\ell\geq g$.
Contracting each replacement path shows $\operatorname{tw}(G)
\leq\operatorname{tw}(G_\ell)$. Conversely, for each path
$u=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_\ell=v$, attach the bags
$\{u,v,x_1\},\{v,x_1,x_2\},\ldots,
\{v,x_{\ell-2},x_{\ell-1}\}$ along a path to an old bag containing
$u,v$. These bags cover the replacement path and preserve the
connectedness condition for each vertex, so
$\operatorname{tw}(G_\ell)\leq\max\{\operatorname{tw}(G),2\}$.
If $\operatorname{tw}(G)\ge2$, the two bounds give equality.
If $G$ is a forest with an edge, subdivision preserves acyclicity,
so both graphs have treewidth one. An edgeless graph is unchanged.
Thus subdivision preserves treewidth in every case.
A path on $s$ vertices has an
elimination tree of height at most $\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil$: choose
a middle vertex as root and recurse on the two remaining subpaths.
Thus the $\ell-1$ internal vertices on each replaced edge have such
a tree of height $h=\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil$. Attach this tree below
the deeper of the original edge's endpoints in an elimination forest
of $G$. The original endpoints are comparable, so the edge to the
shallower endpoint is represented as well. Together with
minor-monotonicity, this gives
\[
 \td(G)\leq\td(G_\ell)\leq\td(G)+h,\qquad h=\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} absorbs the fixed height increase
$h$ in the linear promise bounds. For total depth, start with a
$d$-regular instance $G$ on $n$ vertices from
Corollary~\ref{cor:regular-hardness}.
Since $d$ is odd, add a perfect matching to make every degree even,
using auxiliary matching edges that may be parallel to original edges.
Each connected component of the augmented multigraph has even
degrees and therefore has an Euler tour, a closed walk traversing
every edge once. Orient its edges along this tour, then delete the
matching edges. Each vertex has outdegree
$(d+1)/2$ before deletion, so its outdegree in the original graph is
at least $(d-1)/2$.

Assign the internal vertices of each replacement path to the tail
of its oriented edge. For each vertex $v$, let $X_v$ consist of $v$
and the internal vertices assigned to it. The sets $X_v$ partition
$V(G_\ell)$; each is connected and has size at least
$s=1+(\ell-1)(d-1)/2$. In an elimination forest of $G_\ell$, let
$r_v$ be the shallowest vertex of $X_v$. Since $X_v$ is connected
and the endpoints of each graph edge are comparable, $r_v$ is an
ancestor of every vertex in $X_v$. Each original edge $uv$ has a
replacement path whose last edge joins $X_u$ to $X_v$, so $r_u$ and
$r_v$ are comparable. Restrict the forest to the representatives
$r_v$ and identify each $r_v$ with $v$. The resulting forest
$\overline F$ is an elimination forest of $G$. Restriction never
increases depth, and every vertex of $X_v$ was below $r_v$. Hence
the original depth sum is at least
$\sum_v|X_v|\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)
\ge s\totd(\overline F)\ge s\totd(G)$.

For the upper bound, take an optimal elimination forest of $G$ and,
for each original edge, attach a midpoint-recursive elimination tree
of its internal path below the deeper endpoint. Each of the
$\ell-1$ new vertices on that path has depth at most the depth of
that endpoint plus $h$. Each vertex is the deeper endpoint of at
most $d$ edges, so summing the original and new depths gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 s\totd(G)&\leq\totd(G_\ell)\\
 &\leq\totd(G)+(\ell-1)\sum_{uv\in E(G)}
       \operatorname{depth}_{F}(\text{deeper endpoint of }uv)
       +h(\ell-1)|E(G)|\\
 &\leq[1+d(\ell-1)]\totd(G)+h(\ell-1)|E(G)|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $1+d(\ell-1)\leq3s$, since
$3s-[1+d(\ell-1)]=2+(\ell-1)(d-3)/2\geq0$. The last term is
$O(n)$ because $d$ and $\ell$ are fixed. For a target factor $r$,
choose a fixed $r'>r$ and a source SSE gap with NO bound $Z>3r'Y$,
where $Y$ is its YES upper bound. The subdivided instance has YES
bound at most $3sY+O(n)$ and NO bound at least $sZ$.
Since $Y$ is quadratic in $n$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation}
absorbs the $O(n)$ term for sufficiently large instances and
preserves the factor-$r$ gap. Such orders are obtained from
parallel host-edge copies as in
Appendix~\ref{app:bounded-degree-hardness}.
\end{proof}

The total-depth conclusions of both corollaries also hold for average
depth, by division by the graph order, and for trivially perfect
completion, by~\eqref{eq:tp-adjusted-cost}.
