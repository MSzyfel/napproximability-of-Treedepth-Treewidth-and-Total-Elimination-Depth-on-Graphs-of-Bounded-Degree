\subsection{Hardness consequences}
\label{subsec:bounded-degree-consequences}

Fix a target factor $r$ below a dense hardness threshold, and choose
a dense factor $r'>r$. Write $Y$ for its YES upper bound. It is linear
in $N$ for treedepth and treewidth and quadratic for total depth. The
sparsification losses $\tau N$ and $\tau N^2$ can therefore be made
at most $\delta Y$ by choosing $\tau$ small. Taking
$\delta<(r'-r)/(r+1)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} transfers
the factor-$r$ hardness.
We verify the factor conditions using the following two observations.

For every variable $x$ of $F$ and $a\in\{0,1\}$, let
$W_x^a\subseteq V(G_F)$ consist of the witnesses for equations
containing $x$ whose represented assignments set $x=a$.

\begin{lemma}[Local factors of the parity graph]\label{lem:parity-local-factors}
The edges of $G_F$ are the union of its witness-star edges and the
bicliques $T_x^0\times T_x^1$, $T_x^0\times W_x^1$, and
$T_x^1\times W_x^0$ over all variables $x$.
The star edges have maximum degree three, every vertex belongs to
at most three local factors, and their side-size ratios are at
most $12/5$. Moreover, $W_x^0\subseteq A\cup C$ and
$W_x^1\subseteq B\cup C$ for every variable $x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
These are the truth-block and inconsistency edges in the
construction. A truth vertex belongs to two local bicliques. A
witness belongs to one per variable of its equation, hence three.
The sizes $|T_x^a|=5d_x/6$ and $|W_x^a|=2d_x$ give the ratio
$12/5$. A witness setting $x=0$ lies in $A\cup C$, and one
setting $x=1$ lies in $B\cup C$, by the placement of the special
witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clique factors of a line graph]\label{lem:linegraph-factors}
Let $G$ be an unweighted host multigraph. Identify each edge
occurrence $e\in E(G)$ with its vertex in $\mathcal L(G)$, and put
$U_v=\{e\in E(G):v\text{ is an endpoint of }e\}$ for $v\in V(G)$.
The nonempty sets
$U_v$ give clique factors for $\mathcal L(G)$, and each line-graph
vertex belongs to at most two factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two line-graph vertices are adjacent if and only if their host edges
share an endpoint, so
$E(\mathcal L(G))=\bigcup_v\binom{U_v}{2}$.
An edge has at most two endpoints. A loop contributes one vertex
to the factor at its endpoint.
\end{proof}

For each promise transfer, fix a target factor and a larger dense
hardness factor, then choose $\tau$. We may require arbitrarily
large sources: disjoint copies preserve the satisfiable fraction
of a parity system and the independence fraction and maximum degree
of an independent-set source. A relative independent-set
approximation on the copies transfers to the original by choosing
the copy with the largest selected set. For SSE, parallel copies
of every host edge preserve normalized volumes and cuts and
multiply the line-graph order.

In all SSE cases, use Lemma~\ref{lem:sse-unweighted-gap} with source
parameter $\eta$ and $q=\lceil2/\eta\rceil$, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sse-hardness}. Write $N=|E(G^*)|$, counting
multiplicity, and $\beta=(c/2)\sqrt\eta\le1/4$.
The line graph has YES height bound $4\eta N$ and NO balanced-separator
bound $\beta N$, the latter by
Lemma~\ref{lem:linegraph-separators} with $\rho=1/2$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:linegraph-factors} gives $d_1=2$, $d_0=0$, and no
biclique factors. Since $\beta/\eta$ grows without bound as $\eta$
decreases, any fixed target gap can be obtained before sparsification.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree treedepth hardness]
\label{cor:td-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:np-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:sse-bounded-degree}
For each fixed $r\ge1$, approximating treedepth within factor $r$
on simple unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree is
NP-hard if $r<18/17$, Unique Games-hard if $r<4/3$, and
SSE-hard for every fixed $r$.
The degree bound depends only on the excluded factor. All reductions
are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:td-sparsification} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} in each case.

\emph{NP-hardness.}
Use the factors of Lemma~\ref{lem:parity-local-factors}, the three
anchor cliques, and, after omitting $z_A,z_B,z_C$, the three
anchor--part bicliques. Retain the witness-star edges.
Each source vertex belongs to at most three local factors and one
anchor--part biclique, while an anchor vertex belongs to its clique
and one biclique. Thus $d_1=4$, $d_0=3$, and $\kappa=12/5$.
    Deleting $z_A,z_B,z_C$ lowers treedepth by at most $3$, by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:restore-vertices}. The resulting graph has order
    $18m$, so sparsification lowers treedepth by at most $18\tau m$.
The resulting YES bound is $(17/2+\epsilon)m+1$, and the NO lower
bound is $(9-\epsilon)m-2-18\tau m$. Fix a target factor
$r<18/17$ and let
$\gamma=9-\epsilon-r(17/2+\epsilon)>0$, using the choice of
$\epsilon$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:np-hardness}. Choose
$\tau<\gamma/36$ and take enough source copies that
$m>2(2+r)/\gamma$. Then
\[
 (9-\epsilon)m-2-18\tau m
 -r\bigl((17/2+\epsilon)m+1\bigr)
 \ge \frac{\gamma m}{2}-(2+r)>0,
\]
so the gap survives both losses.

\emph{UGC hardness.}
Fix a target factor $r<4/3$, and choose $r'$ with
$r<r'<4/3$. Take $q,\eta$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ugc-hardness} for factor $r'$, and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-degree-kr-source} with this $\eta$ in
$S_q(G)$. The layer cliques give $d_1=1$, and the retained
cross-layer edges have maximum degree $(q-1)d_\eta$. Write $Y$ for
the dense YES upper bound. A layer clique gives
$n\le\td(S_q(G))\le Y$ in the YES case.
The sparsification loss is therefore at most $\tau qn\le\tau qY$.
Choose $\tau<(r'-r)/(q(r+1))$. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation}
then transfers the dense factor-$r'$ gap to factor $r$.

\emph{SSE hardness.}
Use the common line-graph factors above.
Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-by-td} converts the separator bound into
NO treedepth bound $\beta N$. For a target factor $r$, choose $\eta$
small enough that $\gamma:=\beta-4r\eta>0$, which is possible
since $\beta=(c/2)\sqrt{\eta}$. Choose $\tau<\gamma$. After
sparsification, the NO lower bound minus $r$ times the YES upper
bound is at least $(\gamma-\tau)N>0$.
\end{proof}

The NP-hardness assertion answers the bounded-degree conjecture of
Schirrmacher, Siebertz, and
Vigny~\cite[Conjecture~1]{SchirrmacherSiebertzVigny2025}.
Weighted treedepth hardness at maximum degree ten is proved
in~\cite{DirksEtAl2025Weighted}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree treewidth hardness]
\label{cor:tw-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:tw-sse-bounded-degree}
For each fixed $r\ge1$, approximating treewidth within factor $r$
on simple unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree is
NP-hard if $r<12/11$, Unique Games-hard if $r<4/3$, and
SSE-hard for every fixed $r$.
The degree bound depends only on the excluded factor. All reductions
are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-sparsification} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation}.

\emph{NP-hardness.}
In $K_R(G_F)$, use the three global cliques and the local factors
of Lemma~\ref{lem:parity-local-factors} in each copy, retaining
the witness-star edges. Then $d_1=4$, $d_0=3$, and $\kappa=12/5$.
Each truth block lies in one global clique, and each $W_x^a$
meets at most two. The nonempty intersections of a biclique side
with the global parts therefore partition it into at most two
sets, each contained in its global clique. Thus $s=2$.
Fix a target factor $r<12/11$. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-hardness} chooses $\epsilon,R$ so that
\[
 \gamma=R(6-\epsilon)-r\bigl(R(11/2+\epsilon)+3\bigr)>0.
\]
The dense YES and NO bounds are respectively
$R(11/2+\epsilon)m+3m-1$ and $R(6-\epsilon)m-1$.
Since the output order is $9Rm$, sparsification preserves the YES
upper bound and lowers the NO bound by at most $9\tau Rm$. Choose
$\tau<\gamma/(18R)$. The sparse NO lower bound minus $r$ times the
sparse YES upper bound is
\[
 \bigl(R(6-\epsilon)m-1-9\tau Rm\bigr)
 -r\bigl(R(11/2+\epsilon)m+3m-1\bigr)
 =(\gamma-9\tau R)m+(r-1)
 >\frac{\gamma m}{2}+r-1>0.
\]

\emph{UGC hardness.}
Fix a target factor $r<4/3$, and choose $r'$ with
$r<r'<4/3$. Take $\zeta,R$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-ugc} for factor $r'$, and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-degree-kr-source} with $\eta=\zeta$,
which is admissible since $\zeta<1/12<1/10$. Apply it in
$K_R(P_3(G))$.
Replace the three global cliques and retain the product edges.
Thus $d_1=1$ and $d_0=2d_\eta$, with no biclique factors.
Lemma~\ref{lem:multipartite-product} gives YES cover bound
$(3/2+3\zeta)n$ and NO cover bound
$(2-2\zeta)n\geq(2-3\zeta)n$ for $P_3(G)$, as required by
Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-ugc}.
The dense graph has global cliques of size $Rn$, so its treewidth is
at least $Rn-1$. Write $Y$ for the dense YES upper bound, so
$Y\ge Rn-1$. Since the graph has order $3Rn$, the sparsification
loss is at most $3\tau Rn\le6\tau(Rn-1)\le6\tau Y$ when $Rn\ge2$. Choose
$\tau<(r'-r)/(6(r+1))$. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} then
transfers the dense factor-$r'$ gap to factor $r$.

\emph{SSE hardness.}
Use the common line-graph factors.
Every finite nonempty graph $H$ satisfies
$b(H)\le\operatorname{tw}(H)+1$~\cite[Lemma~5.3]{APW}.
Thus the sparse NO width is at least $\beta N-1-\tau N$.
The YES line-graph construction has treedepth at most $4\eta N$.
For an elimination forest of height $h$, connect its roots to form
a tree and use one bag per forest vertex, containing that vertex and
all its ancestors. The bags containing a fixed vertex form its
descendant subtree, and every graph edge is covered at its deeper
endpoint. Thus these bags give a tree decomposition of width at most
$h-1$. Hence the dense YES treewidth is at most $4\eta N$, and
deleting edges to obtain the sparse graph cannot increase it.
Fix $\eta$ so that $\gamma:=\beta-4r\eta>0$, which is possible
because $\beta=(c/2)\sqrt\eta$. Choose $\tau<\gamma/2$ and take
sufficiently many parallel copies so that $N>2/\gamma$. Then
\[
 \beta N-1-\tau N>(\beta-\gamma)N=4r\eta N,
\]
so the sparse NO width exceeds $r$ times the YES bound.
\end{proof}

The SSE treewidth hardness at every fixed factor on unrestricted
graphs is due to Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{APW}. The bounded-degree
statement here follows by applying our treewidth sparsification
theorem to the line-graph construction.

For the total-depth transfer below, we use the following bound for
lifting an approximate solution through a reduction.

\begin{lemma}[Lifting approximate solutions]
\label{lem:solution-lifting}
Suppose each solution of a minimization instance $I'$ with cost $z$
can be converted in polynomial time into a solution of $I$ with cost
at most $z/a+\eta L(I)$, where $a>0$, $\eta\ge0$, and
$0\le L(I)\le\OPT(I)$. If
$\OPT(I')\le a\OPT(I)+\eta aL(I)$, then an $r$-approximation for
$I'$ gives an $(r+(r+1)\eta)$-approximation for $I$. If instead
$\OPT(I')\le a\OPT(I)$, the factor is $r+\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An $r$-approximate solution has $z\le r\OPT(I')$, so its conversion
has cost at most
\[
\frac za+\eta L(I)
 \le\frac{r\OPT(I')}{a}+\eta L(I)
 \le r\OPT(I)+(r+1)\eta L(I)
 \le\bigl(r+(r+1)\eta\bigr)\OPT(I).
\]
When $\OPT(I')\le a\OPT(I)$, use $z/a\le r\OPT(I)$ to bound the
conversion cost by
$r\OPT(I)+\eta L(I)\le(r+\eta)\OPT(I)$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree total-depth hardness]
\label{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:np-total-depth-bounded-degree}
\label{thm:total-depth-ugc-bounded-degree}
\label{cor:total-depth-sse-bounded-degree}
There is an absolute constant $\epsilon_0>0$ such that approximating
total depth within factor $1+\epsilon_0$ is NP-hard on simple
unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree. On such graphs,
total depth is also Unique Games-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor $1\le r<8/7$, and SSE-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor $r\ge1$. The degree bound depends only on the excluded
factor. All reductions are deterministic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:total-depth-sparsification}.
It loses at most $\tau N^2$ and completes sparse forests at the
same additive cost.

\emph{NP-hardness.}
Use the dense family $H=H^\Sigma_M(G(J))$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:np-total-depth}, where the cubic source $J$ has
$n$ vertices and $M=\Theta(n)$. Choose $0<\epsilon_0<1/2$ so that this
family is hard within factor $1+2\epsilon_0$.
The three part cliques and the three bicliques joining each part
$X$ to $T_X$ give $d_1=2$. Retain $E(G(J))$, of maximum degree
three. Here $N=11n$ and
$M=\lceil N(N+1)/(2\epsilon_0n)\rceil$. Since $n\ge4$ and
$\epsilon_0<1$,
\[
 \frac{121n}{2\epsilon_0}\le M
 \le\frac{121n}{2\epsilon_0}+\frac{11}{2\epsilon_0}+1
 \le\frac{64n}{\epsilon_0}.
\]
The parts have sizes $2n,9n/2,9n/2$, so $M>9n/2$ and each
biclique $X\times T_X$ has side-size ratio
$M/|X|\le32/\epsilon_0$. Thus $\kappa=32/\epsilon_0$ suffices.

The output order is $N=11n+3M$. The bounds on $M$ give
\[
 \frac{Mn}{N^2}\geq
 \frac{121/(2\epsilon_0)}{(11+192/\epsilon_0)^2}=:c_*>0.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:np-total-depth-added-vertices-bounds} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:np-total-depth-cubic} give
\[
 \totd(H)\ge MW(G(J))
 \ge M(37n/2+3)\ge Mn=\Omega(N^2).
\]
Choose the fixed sparsification parameter $\tau\le\epsilon_0c_*$,
so that $\tau N^2\le\epsilon_0 Mn$ for every source order $n$.
Since the sparse graph $H'\subseteq H$
has $\totd(H')\le\totd(H)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:solution-lifting},
with $L(H)=Mn$, lifts a
$(1+\epsilon_0)$-approximate sparse forest to a
$(1+2\epsilon_0)$-approximate dense forest, a contradiction.
Also $\totd(H')\ge\totd(H)-\tau N^2\ge(1-\epsilon_0)Mn
=\Omega(N^2)$. We use this lower bound in the regular-graph
transfer below.

\emph{UGC hardness.}
Fix a target factor $r<8/7$, and choose $r'$ with
$r<r'<8/7$. Use the parameters $q,\eta$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:total-depth-ugc} for factor $r'$, with the same
layer factors and retained edges as in the treedepth proof. Thus
$d_1=1$ and $d_0=(q-1)d_\eta$.
Each layer is a clique, so its $n$ vertices lie on one ancestor
path and have depths at least $1,2,\ldots,n$. Summing over all $q$
layers gives the lower bound
\[
 L:=q\,\frac{n(n+1)}2\le\totd(S_q(G)),\qquad
 L\ge\frac{qn^2}{2}=\frac{N^2}{2q},\quad N=qn.
\]
Write $Y$ for the dense YES upper bound. Since the layer cliques give
$L\le\totd(S_q(G))\le Y$ in the YES case, the sparsification loss
$\tau N^2$ is at most $2q\tau L\le2q\tau Y$. Choose
$\tau<(r'-r)/(2q(r+1))$. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} then
transfers the dense factor-$r'$ gap to factor $r$.

\emph{SSE hardness.}
The YES line-graph construction has height at most $4\eta N$ and
has $N$ vertices, so its total depth is at most
$N\cdot4\eta N=4\eta N^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-by-td} gives NO total depth
at least $\beta N^2/2$. For a target factor $r$, choose $\eta$
small enough that $\gamma:=\beta/2-4r\eta>0$, which is possible
since $\beta=(c/2)\sqrt{\eta}$. Choose $\tau<\gamma$. After
sparsification, the NO lower bound minus $r$ times the YES upper
bound is $(\gamma-\tau)N^2>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular graphs and subdivisions}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:regularization}
For every finite simple graph $G$ of order $n$ and fixed odd $d\geq3$ with
$d>\Delta(G)$, one can construct in polynomial time a simple
$d$-regular graph $R$ containing $G$ as an induced subgraph, with
$(d+2)n\leq |V(R)|\leq(d+3)n$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \td(G)&\leq\td(R)\leq\td(G)+d+2,\\
 \operatorname{tw}(G)&\leq\operatorname{tw}(R)
 \leq\max\{\operatorname{tw}(G),d+2\},\\
 (d+2)\totd(G)&\leq\totd(R)
 \leq(d+3)\totd(G)+\tfrac12(d+2)(d+3)n.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each $v\in V(G)$, set $t=d-\deg_G(v)$, so $1\le t\le d$.
Take graphs $Q_v$ whose vertex sets are pairwise disjoint and
disjoint from $V(G)$. Choose $P_v\subseteq V(Q_v)$ with $|P_v|=t$,
and join $v$ to every vertex of $P_v$. Define $Q_v$ as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $t$ is even, take $K_{d+1}$ and remove a perfect matching
   on $P_v$.
 \item If $t\ge3$ is odd, take $K_{d+2}$ and remove a cycle with
   vertex set $P_v$ and a perfect matching on $V(Q_v)\setminus P_v$.
   The latter set has even size $d+2-t$.
 \item If $t=1$, write $P_v=\{p\}$, take $K_{d+2}$, and choose
   distinct $a,b\in V(Q_v)\setminus\{p\}$. Remove $pa,pb$ and a
   perfect matching on $V(Q_v)\setminus\{p,a,b\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $R$ be the graph obtained from $G$ by these attachments.
Every vertex in $P_v$ has degree $d-1$ inside $Q_v$ and gains one
edge to $v$. Every vertex of $V(Q_v)\setminus P_v$ has degree $d$
inside $Q_v$, and $v$ gains $t=d-\deg_G(v)$ neighbors. Thus all
degrees in $R$ are $d$.
Each $Q_v$ has minimum degree $d-1$ and at most $d+2$ vertices.
Every connected component therefore has at least $d$ vertices,
so $Q_v$ is connected because
$2d>d+2$ for $d\geq3$. The resulting graph $R$ is simple.

Appending $V(Q_v)$ as a chain below $v$ increases the height by at
most $d+2$. If the chain has $|V(Q_v)|$ vertices, their total depth is
at most
\[
 |V(Q_v)|\operatorname{depth}_F(v)
 +\frac{|V(Q_v)|(|V(Q_v)|+1)}2
 \leq(d+2)\operatorname{depth}_F(v)+\frac{(d+2)(d+3)}2.
\]
Summing over $v$ and including the original vertices gives the stated
total-depth upper bound. Attach a bag $\{v\}\cup V(Q_v)$ to an
existing bag containing $v$ in a tree decomposition of $G$.
Doing this for every $v$ proves the treewidth bound.

For the total-depth lower bound, partition $V(R)$ into connected sets
$X_v=\{v\}\cup V(Q_v)$. Fix an elimination forest $F$ of $R$.
The shallowest
vertex $r_v$ of $X_v$ is an ancestor of every vertex in $X_v$.
To see this, take a path in $X_v$ starting at $r_v$. If a next vertex
is a descendant of the current one, it remains below $r_v$. If it is
an ancestor of the current one, it lies on the path from the root to
the current vertex and cannot be above $r_v$, by the choice of $r_v$.
Thus every vertex of $X_v$ is below $r_v$. For every source edge $uv$,
the endpoints $u,v$ are comparable in the forest. Since $r_u$ and
$r_v$ are ancestors of $u$ and $v$, they are both ancestors of the
deeper endpoint and are therefore comparable. Restrict the forest to
the representatives $r_v$ and rename each $r_v$ as $v$. The result
$\overline F$ is an elimination forest of $G$. For every $x\in X_v$,
$\operatorname{depth}_F(x)\ge\operatorname{depth}_F(r_v)
\ge\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)$, so
\[
 \totd(F)\geq\sum_v|X_v|\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)
 \geq(d+2)\totd(\overline F)\geq(d+2)\totd(G).
\]
This argument also gives a polynomial-time projection of forests.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:regular-hardness}
The hardness ranges in Corollaries~\ref{cor:td-bounded-degree},
\ref{cor:tw-bounded-degree}, and~\ref{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree}
also hold on $d$-regular graphs for a suitable fixed odd degree $d$
depending only on the excluded factor. The NP-hardness constant for
total depth may decrease.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a target factor $r$ and a larger factor $r'$ in the same
hardness range. For NP-hardness of total depth, take
$1<r<r'<1+\epsilon_0$.
Choose an odd $d$ larger than the maximum degree of the
bounded-degree source family. We may increase $d$ further, then
require a sufficiently large source order $n$, using the
enlargements described in Section~\ref{subsec:bounded-degree-consequences}.

For treedepth and treewidth, Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization}
changes the promise bounds by at most the fixed constant $d+2$.
Their YES bounds have linear scale, so this is $o(Y)$ as the source
order grows. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} preserves the
factor-$r$ gap.

For total depth, divide the regularized cost by $d+2$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization} gives
\[
 0\le\frac{\totd(R)}{d+2}-\totd(G)
 \le\frac{\totd(G)}{d+2}+\frac{d+3}{2}n
 \le\frac{n(n+1)}{2(d+2)}+\frac{d+3}{2}n.
\]
Let $Y=\Theta(n^2)$ be the YES upper bound. For any fixed
$\epsilon>0$, choose $d$ large enough that the first term in the
last bound is at most $\epsilon Y/2$ for all sufficiently large $n$,
then choose $n$ large enough that the second term is at most
$\epsilon Y/2$. The normalized error is then at most $\epsilon Y$,
and the NO bound does not decrease. Choosing
$\epsilon<(r'-r)/(r+1)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation}
preserves the factor-$r$ gap.

For the NP total-depth family, let $G=H'$ be the sparse instance
from the preceding proof, and write $s=|V(G)|=11n+3M$, with $n$
and $M$ as defined there. That proof gives
$\OPT(G)=\totd(H')\geq(1-\epsilon_0)Mn=:L$. Since $M=\Theta(n)$,
we have $s=\Theta(n)$ and $L=\Omega(s^2)$. Choose $\epsilon>0$
such that $r+(r+1)\epsilon<r'$. Applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization} to a graph of order $s$ gives
\[
 0\leq\frac{\totd(R)}{d+2}-\totd(G)
 \leq\frac{s(s+1)}{2(d+2)}+\frac{d+3}{2}s.
\]
Fix $c>0$ such that $L\geq cs^2$ throughout this family. For
$s\geq1$, the first term is at most $s^2/(d+2)\leq
L/(c(d+2))$. Choose an odd $d$, also larger than the maximum degree
of $G$, so that $1/(c(d+2))\leq\epsilon/2$. For this fixed $d$,
the second term is at most $\epsilon L/2$ whenever
$s\geq(d+3)/(c\epsilon)$. Therefore
\[
 \frac{\totd(R)}{d+2}-\totd(G)\leq\epsilon L,
 \qquad
 \OPT(R)\leq(d+2)\OPT(G)+(d+2)\epsilon L.
\]
The projection in Proposition~\ref{prop:regularization} converts
every forest of $R$ of cost $z$ into one of $G$ of cost at most
$z/(d+2)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:solution-lifting}, with $a=d+2$ and
$\eta=\epsilon$, therefore turns an $r$-approximation on regular
graphs into an approximation of factor at most
$r+(r+1)\epsilon<r'$ on the source family.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:subdivision-hardness}
For every fixed $g\geq3$, the treedepth and treewidth hardness ranges above
hold on bounded-degree graphs that are bipartite, $2$-degenerate, and
have girth at least $g$. Here $2$-degenerate means that every
nonempty subgraph has a vertex of degree at most two, and girth is the
length of a shortest cycle (infinite for an acyclic graph). On the same class, total depth is
SSE-hard to approximate within every fixed factor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replace every edge of $G$ by a path of fixed even length
$\ell\geq\max\{2,g/3\}$, obtaining $G_\ell$. All original vertices
lie in one side of a bipartition, since every replacement path has
even length. Each internal vertex has degree two, and deleting all
internal vertices leaves the original vertices isolated, so
$G_\ell$ is $2$-degenerate. Every cycle uses at least three
replacement paths and therefore has length at least $3\ell\geq g$.
Treewidth and treedepth are minor-monotone: vertex and edge deletions
and edge contractions cannot increase either parameter. Contracting
each replacement path therefore gives
$\operatorname{tw}(G)\leq\operatorname{tw}(G_\ell)$ and
$\td(G)\leq\td(G_\ell)$. Conversely, for each path
$u=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_\ell=v$, attach the bags
$\{u,v,x_1\},\{v,x_1,x_2\},\ldots,
\{v,x_{\ell-2},x_{\ell-1}\}$ along a path to an old bag containing
$u,v$. These bags cover the replacement path and preserve the
connectedness condition for each vertex, so
$\operatorname{tw}(G_\ell)\leq\max\{\operatorname{tw}(G),2\}$.
If $\operatorname{tw}(G)\ge2$, the two bounds give equality.
If $\operatorname{tw}(G)=1$, then $G$ is a forest with an edge,
so subdivision preserves acyclicity and both graphs have treewidth
one. If $\operatorname{tw}(G)=0$, then $G$ is edgeless and the
construction leaves it unchanged. Thus subdivision preserves
treewidth in every case.
A path on $s$ vertices has an
elimination tree of height at most $\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil$: choose
a middle vertex as root and recurse on the two remaining subpaths.
Thus the $\ell-1$ internal vertices on each replaced edge have such
a tree of height $h=\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil$. Attach this tree below
the deeper of the original edge's endpoints in an elimination forest
of $G$. The original endpoints are comparable, so the edge to the
shallower endpoint is represented as well. This gives
\[
 \td(G_\ell)\leq\td(G)+h,\qquad h=\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-preservation} absorbs the fixed height increase
$h$, since the YES bounds grow linearly with the source order. For
total depth, start with a
$d$-regular instance $G$ on $n$ vertices from
Corollary~\ref{cor:regular-hardness}.
Since $d$ is odd, add a perfect matching to make every degree even,
using auxiliary matching edges that may be parallel to original edges.
Each connected component of the augmented multigraph has even
degrees and therefore has an Euler tour, a closed walk traversing
every edge once. Orient its edges along this tour, then delete the
matching edges. Each vertex has outdegree
$(d+1)/2$ before deletion, so its outdegree in the original graph is
at least $(d-1)/2$.

Assign the internal vertices of each replacement path to the tail
of its oriented edge. For each vertex $v$, let $X_v$ consist of $v$
and the internal vertices assigned to it. The sets $X_v$ partition
$V(G_\ell)$. Each is connected and has size at least
$s=1+(\ell-1)(d-1)/2$. In an elimination forest of $G_\ell$, let
$r_v$ be the shallowest vertex of $X_v$. Since $X_v$ is connected
and the endpoints of each graph edge are comparable, $r_v$ is an
ancestor of every vertex in $X_v$. Each original edge $uv$ has a
replacement path whose last edge joins $X_u$ to $X_v$, so $r_u$ and
$r_v$ are comparable. Restrict the forest to the representatives
$r_v$ and identify each $r_v$ with $v$. The resulting forest
$\overline F$ is an elimination forest of $G$. Restriction never
increases depth, and every vertex of $X_v$ was below $r_v$. Hence
the original depth sum is at least
$\sum_v|X_v|\operatorname{depth}_{\overline F}(v)
\ge s\totd(\overline F)\ge s\totd(G)$.

For the upper bound, take an optimal elimination forest of $G$ and,
for each original edge, attach a midpoint-recursive elimination tree
of its internal path below the deeper endpoint. Each of the
$\ell-1$ new vertices on that path has depth at most the depth of
that endpoint plus $h$. Each vertex is the deeper endpoint of at
most $d$ edges, so summing the original and new depths gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 s\totd(G)&\leq\totd(G_\ell)\\
 &\leq\totd(G)+(\ell-1)\sum_{uv\in E(G)}
       \operatorname{depth}_{F}(\text{deeper endpoint of }uv)
       +h(\ell-1)|E(G)|\\
 &\leq[1+d(\ell-1)]\totd(G)+h(\ell-1)|E(G)|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $1+d(\ell-1)\leq3s$, since
$3s-[1+d(\ell-1)]=2+(\ell-1)(d-3)/2\geq0$. The last term is
$O(n)$ because $d$ and $\ell$ are fixed. For a target factor $r$,
choose a fixed $r'>r$ and a source SSE gap with NO bound $Z>3r'Y$,
where $Y$ is its YES upper bound. For a fixed $C$, the subdivided
instance has YES bound at most $3sY+Cn$ and NO bound at least $sZ$.
Therefore the NO lower bound minus $r$ times the YES upper bound is
at least
\[
 sZ-r(3sY+Cn)>3s(r'-r)Y-rCn>0
\]
for sufficiently large instances, since $Y$ is quadratic in $n$.
Such orders are obtained from
parallel host-edge copies as described in
Section~\ref{subsec:bounded-degree-consequences}.
\end{proof}
