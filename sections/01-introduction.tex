\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Treedepth measures the height of a recursive vertex elimination:
a chosen vertex is removed, and the remaining connected components
are processed independently. Equivalently, it is the minimum height
of a rooted forest in which, for every graph edge, one endpoint is
an ancestor of the other. Such a forest is an \emph{elimination forest}.
This formulation connects treedepth to graph search,
vertex ranking, and symbolic elimination
\cite{Reidl2014,BodlaenderRankings}. Treewidth is the minimum, over
tree decompositions, of one less than the largest bag size. It has a
central role in algorithms based on dynamic programming. The two
parameters constrain different features of a decomposition: treedepth
controls its depth, whereas treewidth controls its largest bag. We
write $\td(G)$ for treedepth
and $\operatorname{tw}(G)$ for treewidth. Although
$\operatorname{tw}(G)+1\leq\td(G)$, a NO-case lower bound on treedepth
does not lower-bound treewidth. A treewidth hardness gap needs a
separate NO-case width bound and a suitably small YES-case decomposition.

Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski proved that approximating treedepth
within factor $1.0003$ is NP-hard~\cite{BNS}. Bonnet proved the
corresponding $1.00005$ hardness bound for treewidth
~\cite{BonnetTreewidth}. For treedepth, Anand's greedy
region-growing method gives an $O(\log n)$-approximation
~\cite{Anand2026}. These bounds leave a wide range of approximation
factors unresolved. Restricting the maximum degree raises a further
question: the dense clique structure used in the recent hardness
reductions is absent, and NP-hardness of computing treedepth on
unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree was conjectural
\cite[Conjecture~1]{SchirrmacherSiebertzVigny2025}.

\subsection{Results}

We strengthen the approximation lower bounds for both parameters
and preserve them on graphs of bounded maximum degree. UGC denotes
the ordinary Unique Games Conjecture, and SSEH denotes the Small Set
Expansion Hypothesis.
Each row states a promise-hardness result under its named assumption.
NP-hardness means unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Treedepth and treewidth approximation bounds]
\label{thm:main-results}
For each row below and every fixed factor $r$ in the indicated
range, approximating the corresponding parameter within factor $r$
is hard in the stated sense.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textup{Hardness} & \textup{Treedepth} & \textup{Treewidth}\\
\midrule
\textup{NP-hard} & $1\leq r<18/17$ & $1\leq r<12/11$\\
\textup{Unique Games-hard} & $1\leq r<4/3$ & $1\leq r<4/3$\\
\textup{SSE-hard} & \textup{every fixed }$r\geq1$
                & \textup{every fixed }$r\geq1$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All statements hold on finite simple unweighted graphs of maximum
degree at most a constant depending only on $r$. The reductions are
deterministic.
\end{theorem}

The dense gaps transfer to bounded degree by the sparsification
theorems in Section~\ref{sec:bounded-degree-hardness}. The resulting
bounds are collected in Corollaries~\ref{cor:td-bounded-degree}
and~\ref{cor:tw-bounded-degree}. Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and
Liu~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{APW} proved SSE hardness for treewidth at
every fixed factor on general graphs. Our line-graph reduction gives
SSE hardness for both treewidth and treedepth at every fixed factor.
Our sparsification theorems give the bounded-degree versions.

The bounded-degree treedepth result answers the conjecture of
Schirrmacher, Siebertz, and Vigny~\cite[Conjecture~1]{SchirrmacherSiebertzVigny2025}
and strengthens it to approximation hardness. Dirks, Schirrmacher,
Siebertz, and Vigny prove
NP-completeness of weighted treedepth on graphs of maximum degree
ten~\cite{DirksEtAl2025Weighted}. Our instances are unweighted.
For treewidth, NP-completeness is already known on cubic
graphs~\cite{BodlaenderEtAl2023Cubic}. The degree bounds in our
approximation results depend on the target ratio.

Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu's earlier incidence construction also
implies SSE hardness for treedepth at every fixed factor after an
additional elimination-forest argument. We give that derivation in
Appendix~\ref{app:incidence-proof}.
The line-graph proof in Section~\ref{sec:sseh} gives balanced-separator
bounds proportional to the output order, which we use in several
applications.

We also consider \emph{total elimination depth}: the minimum sum
of vertex depths in an elimination forest, with roots at depth one.
This objective, denoted by $\totd(G)$, is the vertex-partition-tree
sum (VPT-sum) studied in~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}.
A separate analysis of the layered
construction and a reduction from cubic Independent Set give the
following bounds.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Total elimination depth]\label{thm:total-depth-main}
There are absolute constants $\epsilon_0>0$ and $\Delta$ such that
approximating total elimination depth within factor $1+\epsilon_0$
on simple unweighted graphs of maximum degree at most $\Delta$ is
NP-hard. Under ordinary UGC, every fixed factor $1\leq r<8/7$ is
hard, also on graphs of maximum degree bounded by a constant
depending only on $r$. Under SSEH, every fixed approximation
factor is hard, also at bounded maximum degree. All reductions
are deterministic.
\end{theorem}

The dense NP and UGC bounds are
Theorems~\ref{thm:np-total-depth} and~\ref{thm:total-depth-ugc}.
Their bounded-degree forms, together with the SSE statement, follow
from Corollary~\ref{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree}.
The same statements apply to average elimination depth, since
the graph order is fixed in each instance.

\subsection{Applications}

Our main application is to \emph{Trivially Perfect Completion},
where edges are added until the graph is a forest closure. Two
distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if one is an ancestor of
the other in a rooted forest. The objective is the number of added
edges. Every fixed constant approximation factor is SSE-hard, even at bounded degree
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tp-completion-sse}). The SSE proof uses the same
line graph as our SSE treedepth reduction, but needs a bound on the \emph{sum} of
elimination depths. A balanced separator, whose deletion leaves every
component with at most half the vertices, supplies this estimate,
and an adjusted-cost argument handles the offset between total depth
and the number of added edges. The identity relating these objectives
is established in~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}. Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and
Liu prove SSE hardness for chordal Minimum Fill-In at every fixed
factor~\cite{APW}, but that result
does not cover trivially perfect completions, which form a more
restricted target class.
The adjusted-cost argument also transfers both constant total-depth
gaps to this completion objective, even on graphs of bounded maximum
degree: some factor $1+\epsilon_0>1$ is NP-hard, and every fixed
factor below $8/7$ is Unique Games-hard.

Section~\ref{sec:applications} derives the following consequences
from these constructions and from established reductions:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Deletion and labeling objectives.} Vertex integrity
   inherits UGC hardness below $4/3$ and SSE hardness at every fixed
   factor. The SSE separator gap also gives this hardness for fracture
   number, starwidth, average and maximum general labels, and average
    hierarchical labels, using the comparisons
   in~\cite{GimaEtAlIntegrity} and the label lower bound
   of Blum and Storandt~\cite{BlumStorandt2023}.
    The SSE line-graph construction also gives edge-ranking hardness
    at every fixed factor on simple bipartite hosts, via
    $\operatorname{er}(B)=\td(\mathcal L(B))$~\cite{BodlaenderRankings}.
 \item \emph{Automata and hypergraph widths.} Gruber's established
   transformations~\cite{Gruber2008} carry the treedepth gaps to
   star height of regular languages represented by trim binary
   bideterministic partial automata. Replacing each vertex
   by a pair of adjacent copies carries the treewidth gaps to fractional, generalized,
   and ordinary hypertree width on rank-two hypergraphs, following
   the treewidth-hardness strategy of~\cite{Marx2010Fractional}.
 \item \emph{Junction-tree table size.} For binary variables, the
   total state space is hard to approximate within $2^{\chi n}$
   for every fixed $0<\chi<1/18$ under $\PP\ne\NP$, and for
   every fixed $0<\chi<1/6$ under UGC. These explicit exponents
   follow from the linear width gaps. Constant-factor hardness
   was proved by Bodlaender and Fomin~\cite{BodlaenderFomin2005}.
\end{itemize}
Established equivalent formulations of treedepth, including vertex
ranking and minimum symbolic elimination height, are covered by the
same parameter bounds rather than stated as separate results.

\subsection{Proof overview}

\paragraph{Treedepth and total depth under UGC.}
We replace the Khot--Regev source~\cite{KhotRegev} by clique layers,
with cross-layer edges encoding source adjacency. A YES vertex cover
forms a common chain, below which independent-set copies occupy
separate layer chains. For soundness, analyze the first branch:
each child uses fewer layers, and source vertices repeated across
children form an independent set. The child cover numbers sum to
at most the source cover number. Induction and a branching inequality
then force a long prefix or a deep child. For total depth, we also
count the prefix's depth shifts and restore missing child copies.
A quadratic branching inequality bounds the combined savings across
all children.

\paragraph{Treewidth under UGC.}
A multipartite product gives a balanced tripartite cover gap.
We replicate this source, then complete its global parts to cliques.
Bonnet's central-bag argument~\cite[Lemma~6]{BonnetTreewidth} forces
a bag covering every source edge. Conversely, a cover supplies the
center of a decomposition whose three paths process copies one at
a time. The temporary bag increase depends on one source part,
while the cover contribution grows with replication.

\paragraph{Treedepth under SSEH.}
The Raghavendra--Steurer--Tulsiani host~\cite{RST} has a YES
partition into equal-volume classes with few crossing edges, whereas
NO sets of medium degree volume have large boundaries. In its line
graph, crossing host edges form a short chain above separate class
chains. Conversely, an output separator splits surviving host edges
into two large collections with disjoint endpoint sets. These sets
define a host cut contained in the deleted edges. The boundary bound
therefore forces a large separator, which lower-bounds both height
and total depth.

\paragraph{Parity witnesses for NP-hardness.}
We refine the truth-block and anchor construction
of~\cite{BNS}, using the parity gap
of~\cite[Theorem~10]{HazanSafraSchwartz2003}. Truth blocks encode
variable values, and four witnesses per equation represent its
satisfying assignments. A star on the witnesses limits the gain from
leaving variables unassigned. Randomly completing a partial assignment
lets us choose block sizes that pay for this gain, so the independence
number records the optimum number of satisfied equations. Balanced
parts and shorter anchor cliques transfer this gap to treedepth.
Replicating the same source and completing its global parts transfers
it to treewidth.

\paragraph{Total depth under $\PP\ne\NP$.}
Complete three parts to cliques and give each part many independent
neighbors. Moving these neighbors below their deepest clique vertex
never increases total depth. They amplify the sum of the depths
where the three clique parts end. Shared portions of the three
ancestor paths encode successive cover costs. A construction from
cubic Independent Set~\cite{AlimontiKann2000} uses an added matching
to force one designated part to separate first. Its cover cost
encodes the source optimum, allowing a good total-depth approximation
to recover a nearly optimal independent set.

\paragraph{Preserving gaps at bounded degree.}
Replace each clique or biclique factor by a sparse graph obtained
from an expander, preserving an edge between every two sufficiently
large sets in the relevant parts. Edge deletion preserves YES solutions. For soundness, the
remaining edges force a sparse forest or decomposition to represent
the dense factors after a small vertex deletion. Restore the deleted
vertices above the forest roots or in every bag. Bounded factor
incidence controls both the total loss and the replacement degree.
For treewidth, clique-factor bags provide the bags needed to complete
bicliques. The UGC reductions also need bounded-degree source graphs.
We obtain them from the weighted density promise
of~\cite{AKS2011}, using spectral sparsification~\cite{BSS2012} and
removing light edges while preserving an edge in every large NO set.

\subsection{Related work and attribution}

Bonnet's treewidth reduction~\cite{BonnetTreewidth} introduced the
truth-block and assignment-witness framework with three clique parts
used in Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski's treedepth reduction~\cite{BNS}.
Our NP-hardness proof inherits that encoding and their anchor graph.
We use their anchor theorem as a
black box, extending the anchor cliques only in the pairwise-incomparable
case and analyzing comparable anchors separately. Our changes are the
parity-witness stars and the partial-assignment analysis determining
the block sizes. Appendix~\ref{subsec:np-provenance} gives the detailed
comparison. The treewidth argument also uses Bonnet's central-bag
lower bound. Our replication lemma controls
the completeness overhead for the source gaps used here.

Khot and Regev~\cite{KhotRegev} supply the ordinary-UGC source for
the layered construction. B\'erczi, Chandrasekaran, Kir\'aly, Lee,
and Xu~\cite[Sections~3.5--3.6]{BCKLX2017} prove a weighted $k$-partite
Vertex Cover gap with ratio approaching $2(k-1)/k$. Our treewidth
transfer uses an equal-part unweighted gap, which we derive from
Khot--Regev by a multipartite product in
Section~\ref{sec:tw-transfer}. The
bounded-degree version uses the Gaussian density source of Austrin,
Khot, and Safra~\cite{AKS2011}. Our expander replacements use the
standard Margulis--Gabber--Galil family, as presented
in~\cite[Construction~8.1 and Theorem~8.2]{HLW2006}.

Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu~\cite{APW} use the expansion reductions
of Raghavendra, Steurer, and Tulsiani~\cite{RST} to prove SSE hardness
for treewidth, pathwidth, and related objectives at every fixed factor.
For our
bounded-degree result, Section~\ref{sec:bounded-degree-hardness} also
derives the degree bound from the weighted source by the same
factor-completion tools used for NP and UGC hardness.

H\o gemo, Bergougnoux, Brandes, Paul, and Telle~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021} introduce VPT-sum,
identify its relation to trivially perfect completion, and prove
NP-hardness. In vertex-query graph search, a query at $v$ either finds
the hidden target or identifies the component of $G-v$ containing it.
Thus, on a connected graph, a search decision tree is an elimination
tree whose child subtrees correspond to those components. With uniform
target probabilities and unit query costs, the expected search cost
is the mean vertex depth, so its optimum is $\totd(G)/|V(G)|$
~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021,Szyfelbein2025GraphSearch}.
Szyfelbein~\cite{Szyfelbein2025GraphSearch} gives an
$O(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation for the more general search
problem with nonuniform target weights and query costs. A subsequent
preprint also studies target-dependent costs~\cite{Szyfelbein2026GraphSearch}.
Our NP and UGC total-depth reductions have connected outputs. The UGC
graph $S_q(G)$ is connected whenever $G$ has an edge. The edgeless
source is always a YES instance and can be mapped to $K_1$. The NP
construction joins its three part cliques by source-edge paths. The
bounded-degree replacements retain these joining edges and use
connected sparse factor graphs from Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-expanders}.
Thus the same
constant-factor hardness holds for average-case search with uniform
weights and unit query costs, also at bounded maximum degree.

The edge-query analogue is Dasgupta's similarity-based hierarchical
clustering objective~\cite{Dasgupta2016}. For a hierarchy $T$ with
leaves $V(G)$, its cost is
$\sum_{uv\in E(G)}w_{uv}\,|C_T(u,v)|$,
where $C_T(u,v)$ is the leaf set below the least common ancestor
of $u$ and $v$. H\o gemo, Bergougnoux, Brandes, Paul, and Telle~\cite[Theorem~6]{HogemoEtAl2021}
identify its unweighted optimum with EPT-sum, the sum of original
vertex-leaf depths in an edge-partition tree. The unweighted problem
is NP-hard by H\o gemo, Paul, and
Telle~\cite{HogemoPaulTelle2020}. Charikar and
Chatziafratis~\cite{CharikarChatziafratis2017} give an
$O(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation and prove hardness within every
fixed constant factor under SSEH, including for unweighted graphs.
Szyfelbein and Dereniowski~\cite{SzyfelbeinDereniowski2026}
extend this SSE hardness to variants that stop at clusters inducing
trees or graphs of bounded diameter.
The vertex-query and edge-query formulations have different feasible
decompositions. The total-depth bounds proved here concern vertex
queries.
