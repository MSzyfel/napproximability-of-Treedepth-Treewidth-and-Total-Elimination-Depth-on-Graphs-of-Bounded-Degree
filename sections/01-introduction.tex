\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Treedepth is the minimum height of an \emph{elimination forest}: a rooted forest on
the graph's vertices in which the endpoints of every edge are comparable in the
ancestor order. For connected graphs, every elimination forest is a tree.
An optimal elimination tree can be chosen to encode a recursive
vertex-query strategy: querying a vertex either finds the target or
identifies the component containing it after that vertex is removed.
Here the target must be queried even when it is the only remaining
candidate; we call this the confirming convention.
Treedepth is also characterized by vertex ranking and
symbolic elimination~\cite{BodlaenderRankings}, and it is studied directly in
parameterized algorithms~\cite{Reidl2014}. Low-treedepth decompositions are
used in the structural theory of sparse graphs. Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
survey these methods, and their earlier work connects them to subgraph coloring
and homomorphism bounds~\cite{LowTreeDepthSurvey,TreeDepthColoring}.

A \emph{tree decomposition} consists of a tree of vertex sets, called bags,
such that every graph edge has both endpoints in some bag and the bags
containing any fixed vertex form a nonempty connected subtree. Treewidth is the minimum
possible largest bag size minus one. Decompositions of small width support
dynamic programming for many graph problems and have uses in graph-minor
theory. For surveys of these applications, see~\cite{BodlaenderTouristGuide,
BodlaenderApplications}. We write $\td(G)$ and $\operatorname{tw}(G)$ for treedepth and treewidth respectively.

Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski proved that approximating
treedepth within factor $1.0003$ is NP-hard~\cite{BNS}. Bonnet proved the
corresponding $1.00005$ hardness bound for treewidth~\cite{BonnetTreewidth}.
Under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis (SSEH), treewidth is hard to
approximate within every fixed constant factor~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{APW}. On the
algorithmic side, writing $n=|V(G)|$, Anand gives an $O(\log n)$-approximation for
treedepth~\cite{Anand2026}, while Feige, Hajiaghayi, and Lee give a polynomial-time
$O(\sqrt{\log(\operatorname{tw}(G))})$-approximation for
treewidth~\cite{FeigeHajiaghayiLee2005}. We strengthen the hardness bounds and
show that they persist on graphs of bounded maximum degree, resolving the
conjecture of Schirrmacher, Siebertz, and Vigny
~\cite[Conjecture~1]{SchirrmacherSiebertzVigny2025}.

We also study \emph{total elimination depth}, $\totd(G)$, the minimum
sum of vertex depths in an elimination forest (roots have depth one).
This is the vertex-partition-tree sum of~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}. On a
connected graph this is the min-sum cost of vertex-query
search for a target under the confirming convention
~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021,Szyfelbein2025GraphSearch}.

\subsection{Our results and techniques}
\label{subsec:results-techniques}

The degree bound depends only on the excluded approximation factor.
All reductions are deterministic.
Figure~\ref{fig:introduction-reduction-map} traces the source normalizations
and constructions, followed by the common degree reduction, its

The UGC reductions for treedepth and total elimination depth start from a
bounded-degree form of the Khot--Regev independent-set gap~\cite{KhotRegev},
which we derive from Austrin, Khot, and Safra~\cite{AKS2011}. We place copies of the
source graph in clique layers. A large independent set gives a shallow
elimination forest by putting the other vertices on a common prefix and
branching below it. For the lower bound, vertices in different child
subtrees are incomparable, so no source edge can join vertices represented
in different children. This limits the height saved by branching. For
total depth, we also count the depth shift that the prefix imposes on each
child, then bound the combined savings across the children.

For treewidth, a multipartite product first gives a vertex-cover gap on
tripartite graphs with equal-sized parts. We replicate these source graphs before
completing each global part to a clique. Bonnet's central bag argument
then forces a cover from every copy into one bag, while the replication
keeps the additional YES cost to one copy's scale~\cite[Lemma~6]{BonnetTreewidth}.

Under SSEH, we transfer the Raghavendra--Steurer--Tulsiani partition
gap~\cite{RST} to treedepth and total depth through a line graph whose
vertices represent source edges. The YES partition gives a shallow forest.
In the NO case, a small balanced separator in the line graph would divide
the surviving source edges into two large groups with disjoint endpoint
sets. Their endpoints would give a balanced source cut whose boundary
lies among the host edges represented by the separator, contradicting
the source expansion bound. The same separator bounds yield the total
depth gap. Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu already prove the corresponding
SSE treewidth hardness~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{APW}.

For treedepth under $\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$, we refine the truth block
and anchor construction of Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski~\cite{BNS}
using the parity gap of Hazan, Safra, and Schwartz
~\cite[Theorem~10]{HazanSafraSchwartz2003}. The two truth blocks of each
variable encode its values. Each equation has four witnesses for its
satisfying assignments, and star edges ensure that at most three can be
selected together. Counting witnesses consistent with a partial
assignment gives the independent-set bound. Balanced parts and the anchor
graph then transfer this gap to treedepth. The same source gap transfers to treewidth by the
replicated clique completion above.

For total depth, we reuse the normalized bounded-occurrence parity source
of Theorem~\ref{thm:xor-source}; Appendix~\ref{app:xor-normalization}
derives its balance and divisibility conditions. A different tripartite
construction uses literal blocks and auxiliary vertex pairs. Completing
the parts to cliques and attaching three equally sized independent
terminal sets makes the leading total-depth cost depend on three path
lengths, by Lemmas~\ref{lem:np-total-depth-added-vertices}
and~\ref{lem:np-total-depth-path-cost}. The pair gadgets encode the number
of satisfied equations exactly
(Proposition~\ref{prop:np-total-depth-parity-identity}). Fixed block and
padding sizes give hardness below $63/62$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:np-total-depth-dense-gap});
Corollary~\ref{cor:total-depth-bounded-degree} preserves every fixed factor
below it on bounded-degree graphs.

The unrestricted reductions use cliques and bicliques to enforce
comparability and bag constraints, so their output degrees can be large.
The bounded degree section replaces these dense factors with bounded
degree graphs built from expanders. Their no hole condition says that every
two sufficiently large disjoint subsets of a clique factor have an edge
between them, and that sufficiently large subsets on opposite sides of a
biclique factor also have an edge between them. Completion lemmas show how
to extend a sparse solution to the original graph with an additive
increase controlled by a small exceptional set. We choose the thresholds
so this increase stays within the hardness gap. Edge deletion preserves
the YES bounds, so the same reductions prove bounded degree hardness for all three
parameters.
The general sparsification theorems require bounded incidence of factors
at each vertex and bounded degree of the retained edges, but no balance
assumption on biclique sides. If a side is smaller than the no-hole
threshold, that condition is vacuous and the factor can be deleted;
otherwise its side ratio is bounded automatically.

\subsection{Applications}

Our main application is to \emph{Trivially Perfect Completion},
where edges are added until the graph is a forest closure. Two
distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if one is an ancestor of
the other in a rooted forest. The objective is the number of added
edges. For every fixed $C\ge1$, approximating this objective within
factor $C$ is SSE-hard on graphs of maximum degree bounded by a
constant depending only on $C$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tp-completion-sse}). The SSE proof uses the same
line graph as our SSE treedepth reduction, but needs a bound on the \emph{sum} of
elimination depths. A balanced separator, whose deletion leaves every
component with at most half the vertices, supplies this estimate,
and an adjusted-cost argument handles the offset between total depth
and the number of added edges. The identity relating these objectives
is established in~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}. Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and
Liu prove SSE hardness for chordal Minimum Fill-In at every fixed
factor~\cite{APW}, but that result
does not cover trivially perfect completions, which form a more
restricted target class.
The adjusted-cost argument also transfers both constant total-depth
gaps to this completion objective, even on graphs of bounded maximum
degree: every fixed factor below $63/62$ is NP-hard, and every fixed
factor below $8/7$ is Unique Games-hard.

Section~\ref{sec:applications} derives the following consequences
from these constructions and from established reductions:
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Deletion and labeling objectives.} Vertex integrity
   minimizes the number of deleted vertices plus the largest remaining
   component. It inherits UGC hardness below $4/3$ and SSE hardness at every fixed
   factor. The SSE separator gap also gives this hardness for fracture
   number, starwidth, average and maximum general customizable-label size,
   and average hierarchical customizable-label size, using the comparisons
   in~\cite{GimaEtAlIntegrity} and the label lower bound
   of Blum and Storandt~\cite{BlumStorandt2023}.
   The SSE line-graph construction also gives hardness at every fixed
   factor for the edge-ranking number $\operatorname{er}(B)$ on simple
   bipartite graphs $B$, via
    $\operatorname{er}(B)=\td(\mathcal L(B))$~\cite{BodlaenderRankings}.
   For hierarchical customizable labels and upward vertex search spaces
   in customizable contraction hierarchies (CCHs), the minimum total size
   equals $\totd(G)$. Their total and
   average sizes therefore also inherit NP hardness below $63/62$
   and UGC hardness below $8/7$, including on bounded-degree graphs
   (Corollary~\ref{cor:hierarchical-label-hardness}).
 \item \emph{Automata and hypergraph widths.} Gruber's established
   transformations~\cite{Gruber2008} carry the treedepth gaps to
   star height of regular languages represented by trim binary
   bideterministic partial automata. Replacing each vertex
   by a pair of adjacent copies carries the treewidth gaps to fractional, generalized,
   and ordinary hypertree width on rank-two hypergraphs, following
   the treewidth-hardness strategy of~\cite{Marx2010Fractional}.
 \item \emph{Junction-tree table size.} For $n$ binary variables, the
   total state space is hard to approximate within $2^{\chi n}$
   for every fixed $0<\chi<1/18$ under $\PP\ne\NP$, and for
   every fixed $0<\chi<1/6$ under UGC. These exponents
   follow from the linear width gaps. Constant-factor hardness
   was proved by Bodlaender and Fomin~\cite{BodlaenderFomin2005}.
\end{itemize}
Established equivalent formulations of treedepth, including vertex
ranking and minimum symbolic elimination height, inherit the same
parameter bounds.

\subsection{Related work and attribution}

Bonnet's treewidth reduction~\cite{BonnetTreewidth} introduced the
truth-block and assignment-witness framework with three clique parts
used in Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski's treedepth reduction~\cite{BNS}.
Our NP-hardness proofs for treedepth and treewidth inherit that encoding.
For treedepth, we also use their anchor graph and apply their anchor theorem
as a black box, extending the anchor cliques only in the pairwise-incomparable
case and analyzing comparable anchors separately. Our changes are the
parity-witness stars and the partial-assignment analysis determining
the block sizes. Appendix~\ref{subsec:np-provenance} gives the detailed
comparison. The treewidth argument also uses Bonnet's central-bag
lower bound. Our replication lemma controls
the completeness overhead for the source gaps used here.

Khot and Regev~\cite{KhotRegev} supply the independent-set gap underlying
our ordinary-UGC reductions. We use its bounded-degree form throughout,
derived from the Gaussian density source of Austrin, Khot, and
Safra~\cite{AKS2011}. B\'erczi, Chandrasekaran, Kir\'aly, Lee,
and Xu~\cite[Sections~3.5--3.6]{BCKLX2017} prove a weighted $k$-partite
Vertex Cover gap with ratio approaching $2(k-1)/k$. Our treewidth
transfer uses an equal-part unweighted gap, which we derive from
the bounded-degree independent-set source by a multipartite product in
Section~\ref{sec:tw-transfer}. Our expander replacements use the
standard Margulis--Gabber--Galil family, as presented
in~\cite[Construction~8.1 and Theorem~8.2]{HLW2006}.

Wu, Austrin, Pitassi, and Liu~\cite{APW} use the expansion reductions
of Raghavendra, Steurer, and Tulsiani~\cite{RST} to prove SSE hardness
for treewidth, pathwidth, and related objectives at every fixed factor.
% Their incidence-graph reduction also yields SSE treedepth hardness after a
% separator-to-forest argument. We give that derivation in
% Appendix~\ref{app:incidence-proof}.

For our
bounded-degree result, Section~\ref{sec:bounded-degree-hardness}
derives the degree bound from the weighted source by the same
factor-completion tools used for NP and UGC hardness.

H\o gemo, Bergougnoux, Brandes, Paul, and Telle~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}
study VPT-sum, relate it to trivially perfect completion, and prove
NP-hardness. The vertex-query interpretation described above gives
the same objective as expected confirming search cost up to the factor
$|V(G)|$. Under the alternative convention that stops once the target
is uniquely identified, the summed optimum differs by at most $|V(G)|$.
Section~\ref{subsec:vertex-query-search} proves this comparison and
transfers our NP and UGC hardness ranges to average-case search under
either convention, also on connected bounded-degree graphs.
Szyfelbein~\cite{Szyfelbein2025GraphSearch} gives an
$O(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation for the more general search
problem with nonuniform target weights and query costs, using the
identification convention. The same asymptotic bound applies to our
uniform confirming objective, as explained in
Section~\ref{subsec:vertex-query-search}. A subsequent
preprint also studies target-dependent costs~\cite{Szyfelbein2026GraphSearch}.

The edge-query analogue is Dasgupta's similarity-based hierarchical
clustering objective~\cite{Dasgupta2016}. For a hierarchy $T$ with
leaves $V(G)$, its cost is
$\sum_{uv\in E(G)}w_{uv}\,|C_T(u,v)|$,
where $w_{uv}$ is the weight of edge $uv$ and $C_T(u,v)$ is the leaf set below the least common ancestor
of $u$ and $v$. For connected graphs, H\o gemo, Bergougnoux, Brandes,
Paul, and Telle~\cite[Theorem~6]{HogemoEtAl2021} identify its
unweighted optimum with EPT-sum, the minimum sum of leaf depths in an
edge-partition tree. The unweighted problem
is NP-hard by H\o gemo, Paul, and
Telle~\cite{HogemoPaulTelle2020}. Charikar and
Chatziafratis~\cite{CharikarChatziafratis2017} give an
$O(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation and prove hardness within every
fixed constant factor under SSEH, including for unweighted graphs.
Szyfelbein and Dereniowski~\cite{SzyfelbeinDereniowski2026}
extend this SSE hardness to variants that stop at clusters inducing
trees or graphs of bounded diameter.
The total-depth bounds here concern vertex queries, whose feasible
decompositions differ from those of edge queries.
