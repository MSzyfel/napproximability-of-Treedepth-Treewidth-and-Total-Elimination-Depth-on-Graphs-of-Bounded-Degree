\section{Source promises, normalizations, and provenance}
\label{sec:source-normalizations}

The subsections follow their first references in the main text:
the provenance of the NP-hardness construction, the Khot--Regev
promise, the balanced multipartite promise, the global SSE promise,
and the parity normalization. The unweighting lemma precedes
its use in the Khot--Regev proof. The final subsection records an
additional pairwise-cover bound for the parity witness graph.

\subsection{Provenance of the NP-hardness construction}
\label{subsec:np-provenance}

The reduction in Section~\ref{subsec:np-treedepth} passes from a constraint
system to tripartite Vertex Cover and then to Treedepth, following
Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski~\cite[Sections~3 and~4]{BNS}. Their
truth-block and assignment-witness construction adapts Bonnet's earlier
treewidth reduction~\cite[full version, Section~3.1]{BonnetTreewidth}.
We inherit two opposite truth blocks per variable, complete bipartite
edges between them, and witnesses for satisfying local assignments.
Each witness is adjacent to the truth block that assigns each
variable the value used by that witness. Variable-dependent block
sizes also occur in Bonnet's full version~\cite[Section~3.5]{BonnetTreewidth}.

Here the source predicates are three-variable parity equations. Each
has four satisfying assignments. We add a star on their four
witnesses, place its monochromatic witness $000$ or $111$ in a truth
part, and place the other three in the third part. We choose the block
size $5d_x/6$ by analyzing arbitrary partial assignments: uniformly
completing the unassigned variables bounds the excess witnesses of an
equation by $5r_j/6$, where $r_j$ is its number of unassigned variables.
This is the soundness argument proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:xor-cover}.
The corresponding argument in~\cite[proof of Lemma~10]{BNS} uses
variable-by-variable exchanges in a vertex cover.
Pairing the source with a complemented copy then makes all three
parts have size $3m$. These are the modifications to the earlier
encoding used in this proof.

The anchor graph is the one in~\cite[Lemma~6]{BNS}. We use their
identity as a black box at its stated anchor length, together
with the cover-first upper bound and Claims~7 and~9 of their proof.
The main-text corollary extends the identity to anchors as short
as the largest part. Its derivation is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-proof}. For our balanced source graph this
allows $\ell=3m$ even though $\vc(G_F)\ge11m/2$. Combined with the
parity-witness bound, the anchor identity yields the factor below
$18/17$.

\subsection{The Khot--Regev independent-set promise}
\label{app:kr-normalization}

We first record the unweighting step used below.

\begin{lemma}[Unweighting by copies]\label{lem:copy-unweighting}
Let $G$ be a simple graph with positive rational vertex weights $w_v$,
and let $L>0$ make every $m_v=Lw_v$ a positive integer. Define $G'$
on $\{v_i:v\in V(G),\ i\in[m_v]\}$, with $u_iv_j$ an edge if and
only if $uv\in E(G)$. Thus the copies of each vertex are independent,
and a source edge joins its two copy sets completely.
Minimum vertex-cover weight and maximum independent-set weight
scale by $L$: $\vc(G')=L\vc_w(G)$ and $\alphaI(G')=L\alpha_w(G)$.
The construction preserves a specified multipartition, multiplying
each part's weight by $L$ to obtain its new size. It is polynomial
when $\sum_v m_v$ is polynomial in the source encoding length.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For an independent set $I\subseteq V(G)$, take all $m_v$ copies of
each $v\in I$. No two chosen copies are adjacent: copies of the same
vertex are independent, and copies of distinct chosen vertices are
adjacent only if their original vertices are adjacent in $G$.
Thus the copies of vertices in $I$ form an independent set of $G'$ of size
$\sum_{v\in I}m_v=L\sum_{v\in I}w_v$. Maximizing over $I$ proves
$\alphaI(G')\geq L\alpha_w(G)$.

Conversely, let $J$ be any independent set of $G'$ and let $I$ consist
of the source vertices with at least one copy in $J$. If $uv$ were
an edge of $G$ with $u,v\in I$, any chosen copy of $u$ would be
adjacent to every chosen copy of $v$, contradicting independence.
Thus $I$ is independent in $G$. It follows that
\[
 |J|\leq\sum_{v\in I}m_v
 =L\sum_{v\in I}w_v\leq L\alpha_w(G).
\]
Together, the two inequalities prove the independent-set identity.

A vertex set is a cover if and only if its complement is independent.
Using this fact in the weighted source graph and in $G'$ gives
\[
 \vc(G')=|V(G')|-\alphaI(G')
 =L\sum_vw_v-L\alpha_w(G)=L\vc_w(G).
\]
When $G$ is nonempty, neither the independent-set density nor the
cover density changes:
\[
 \frac{\alphaI(G')}{|V(G')|}
 =\frac{\alpha_w(G)}{\sum_vw_v},\qquad
 \frac{\vc(G')}{|V(G')|}
 =\frac{\vc_w(G)}{\sum_vw_v}.
\]

For a specified multipartition, replace each part by the union of
the copy sets of its vertices. There were no source edges within a
part, so there are no new edges within a part either. Its new size
is $L$ times its original weight. Finally, the output has
$\sum_vm_v$ vertices and at most $(\sum_vm_v)^2$ edges. Hence it can
be constructed in polynomial time under the stated size assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:kr}]
Khot and Regev's weighted independent-set
gap~\cite[Section~4]{KhotRegev} has YES independent-set density
$1-1/k-2\epsilon$ and NO density less than $\delta$.
Their Theorem~3.2 derives the intermediate
source promise from the ordinary UGC. Set $k=2$,
$\epsilon=\eta/2$, and $\delta=\eta$. The YES density
becomes $1-1/k-2\epsilon=1/2-\eta$, and the NO density is less
than $\eta$. These are the two
independent-set thresholds in Theorem~\ref{thm:kr}. It remains to
remove the weights without altering either density.

Let $G$ denote the weighted graph in the Khot--Regev construction.
Let $X$ be the set of source variables, let $s$ be the fixed size of
their label set, and write
the fixed rational bias as $p=a_0/a_1$ for integers $0<a_0<a_1$. A vertex
indexed by $(x,F)$, where $x\in X$ and $F\subseteq[s]$, has weight
\[
 w_{x,F}=\frac1{|X|}p^{|F|}(1-p)^{s-|F|}
 =\frac{a_0^{|F|}(a_1-a_0)^{s-|F|}}{|X|a_1^s}.
\]
The weights sum to one, since for each $x$ the sum over $F$ is
$|X|^{-1}(p+(1-p))^s=|X|^{-1}$.
Thus the weighted optimum is already its normalized density.

Take $L=|X|a_1^s$. Then $Lw_{x,F}=a_0^{|F|}(a_1-a_0)^{s-|F|}$ is a
positive integer. Replace $(x,F)$ by this many copies, and
join two copy sets completely if and only if the source vertices were
adjacent. For each fixed $x$, the number of new vertices is
\[
 \sum_{F\subseteq[s]}a_0^{|F|}(a_1-a_0)^{s-|F|}
 =\sum_{i=0}^s\binom{s}{i}a_0^i(a_1-a_0)^{s-i}=a_1^s.
\]
Consequently the new graph has $n=|X|a_1^s=L$ vertices.
Lemma~\ref{lem:copy-unweighting} gives
$\alphaI(G')/n=\alpha_w(G)$, so a YES instance still has
$\alphaI(G')\geq(1/2-\eta)n$, and a NO instance still has
$\alphaI(G')<\eta n$. The strict NO inequality is preserved
because both sides scale by $L$.

For each fixed gap, $a_1$ and $s$ are constants. Hence $|X|a_1^s$ is
polynomial in the source instance size. The construction has no loops
or parallel edges and uses only unweighted vertices and edges. It
therefore gives the simple unweighted source asserted in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The balanced multipartite source}
\label{app:multipartite-normalization}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ugc-tripartite-cover}]
Apply the unweighted source of Theorem~\ref{thm:kr} with
$\eta=\zeta/2$. This is an admissible fixed rational parameter,
since $0<\zeta<1/12$ gives $0<\eta<1/24<1/10$.
For a source graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, form $P_k(G)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:multipartite-product}. It has $N=kn$ vertices,
with $n=N/k$ vertices in each of its $k$ independent layers.

In the YES case, $\alphaI(G)\geq(1/2-\eta)n$.
Taking all $k$ copies of a source independent set gives
\[
 \alphaI(P_k(G))\geq k\alphaI(G)
 \geq k(1/2-\eta)n=(1/2-\eta)N.
\]
Taking complements, we obtain
\[
 \vc(P_k(G))=N-\alphaI(P_k(G))
 \leq(1/2+\eta)N\leq(1/2+\zeta)N.
\]
The last inequality uses $\eta=\zeta/2\leq\zeta$.

In the NO case, $\alphaI(G)<\eta n$, and the upper bound from
Lemma~\ref{lem:multipartite-product} yields
\[
 \alphaI(P_k(G))\leq n+(k-1)\alphaI(G)
 <n+(k-1)\eta n.
\]
Subtracting this strict upper bound from $N=kn$ gives
\[
 \vc(P_k(G))
 >kn-n-(k-1)\eta n
 =\left(1-\frac1k-\frac{k-1}{k}\eta\right)N
 \geq\left(1-\frac1k-\zeta\right)N.
\]
Here $(k-1)\eta/k\leq\eta=\zeta/2<\zeta$, so the claimed
non-strict NO threshold follows in particular. The product has no
loops because edges require distinct layer indices, and no parallel
edges because $G$ is simple. Its $k$ is fixed, so its size is
polynomial in that of $G$. This proves the stated balanced,
simple, unweighted promise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The global SSE partition promise}
\label{app:sse-normalization}

For $-1<\xi<1$, let $\Gamma_\xi(\mu)$ be the probability that
both of two standard normal variables of correlation $\xi$ are
below the threshold whose one-variable lower-tail probability is $\mu$.

We spell out the Gaussian estimate on the interval needed here.
The corresponding Gaussian probability bounds are recorded in the
author-hosted full version of~\cite[Fact~2.22 and Remark~2.25]{APW}.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian separation at medium measure]
\label{lem:gaussian-medium-separation}
There are absolute constants $a,\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for
$0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, $\varepsilon'\in[\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon]$,
and $1/10\le\mu\le1/2$,
$\mu-\Gamma_{1-\varepsilon'/2}(\mu)\ge a\sqrt\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $X,Z$ be independent standard normal random variables, put
$\xi=1-\varepsilon'/2$, and write
$Y=\xi X+\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\,Z$. Then $X,Y$ are standard normal
with correlation $\xi$. If $\Pr[X\leq t]=\mu\in[1/10,1/2]$,
the threshold $t$ lies in a fixed bounded interval with $t\leq0$.
Moreover, $\mu-\Gamma_\xi(\mu)=\Pr[X\leq t<Y]$.
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon'$, we have $0\leq\xi<1$.
On the event $t-\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\leq X\leq t$ and $Z>1$,
we have $X\leq t$ and
\[
 Y>\xi\bigl(t-\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\bigr)
       +\sqrt{1-\xi^2}
 =\xi t+(1-\xi)\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\geq t.
\]
The last inequality uses $t\leq0$ and $\xi\leq1$.
The standard normal density has a positive minimum on the fixed
bounded interval containing all these possible values of $X$.
Thus the first event has probability at least an absolute constant
times its interval length $\sqrt{1-\xi^2}$. The event $Z>1$
has a fixed positive probability and is independent of $X$.
Their intersection therefore has probability at least
$2a\sqrt{1-\xi^2}$ for some absolute constant $a>0$.
Since $\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\geq\sqrt{1-\xi}
=\sqrt{\varepsilon'/2}\geq\frac12\sqrt{\varepsilon}$,
we obtain $\mu-\Gamma_{1-\varepsilon'/2}(\mu)
\geq a\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ for $1/10\leq\mu\leq1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sse-source}]
Fix $q$ and $\varepsilon$ as in the statement, and choose a fixed
$\varepsilon'\in[\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon]$.
Instantiate Theorem~3.5 of~\cite{RST} with error parameter
$\varepsilon'$ and a fixed source error $\gamma$, to be chosen below.

\emph{YES case.}
In the YES case the source theorem gives $q$ disjoint classes of
normalized volume $1/q$ and expansion
$\varepsilon'+o(\varepsilon')$. The sum of their normalized
volumes is $q\cdot(1/q)=1$. Since normalized volume is additive
on disjoint sets and the whole graph has normalized volume one,
the vertices outside these classes have total volume zero.
Such vertices have degree zero and can be assigned to any class
without changing its volume or its boundary. We therefore obtain
a partition of the entire vertex set with the required
class volumes. Choose the remaining fixed source parameters so
that the error term is at most $\varepsilon'$. Every class then
has expansion at most
$\varepsilon'+\varepsilon'=2\varepsilon'\leq2\varepsilon$.

\emph{NO case.}
In the NO case, Theorem~3.5 gives, for every set of positive volume,
the lower bound
\[
  \Phi_G(A)\geq
  1-\frac{\Gamma_{1-\varepsilon'/2}(\mu_G(A))+\gamma}{\mu_G(A)}.
\]
Let $a$ be the constant in Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-medium-separation},
and choose $0<\gamma\le a\sqrt\varepsilon/20$, as the source
theorem permits.
For $\mu_G(A)\in[1/10,1/2]$, multiply the source expansion
inequality by $2\mu_G(A)$ and use
$w(\cut_G(A))/W=2\mu_G(A)\Phi_G(A)$, which follows from
$\vol_G(A)=2W\mu_G(A)$. Therefore
\[
  \frac{w(\cut_G(A))}{W}
  \geq2\bigl(\mu_G(A)-\Gamma_{1-\varepsilon'/2}(\mu_G(A))\bigr)-2\gamma
  \geq2a\sqrt{\varepsilon}-2\gamma\geq\frac{a}{10}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
\]
The final inequality uses $2\gamma\leq(a/10)\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. If
$\mu_G(A)\in[1/2,9/10]$, its complement has measure
$1-\mu_G(A)\in[1/10,1/2]$. A boundary edge has one endpoint in
each side, so $\cut_G(V(G)\setminus A)=\cut_G(A)$ as multisets.
Applying the preceding argument to the complement therefore gives
the same cut bound for $A$. Thus the
claimed NO cut bound holds throughout $[1/10,9/10]$ with $c=a/10$.
Reducing $\varepsilon_0$ if needed ensures the Gaussian stability
estimate used above. All parameters are fixed constants for each source
promise, so the source reduction remains polynomial for each fixed
$(q,\varepsilon)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The parity source normalization}
\label{app:xor-normalization}

\begin{lemma}[Balancing parity right-hand sides]\label{lem:parity-balance}
A system $F_0$ of $m_0$ three-variable parity equations can be paired
with a system $F_0'$ on fresh variables so that their union has
$2m_0$ equations, optimum $2\OPT(F_0)$, and $m_0$ equations with
each right-hand side.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Make a copy $F_0'$ on fresh
variables, replacing every equation $x+y+z=a$ by
$x'+y'+z'=1-a$. For an assignment $\sigma$ to $F_0$, assign
$x'=1-\sigma(x)$ in $F_0'$. Since each equation contains three variables,
\[
 (1-\sigma(x))+(1-\sigma(y))+(1-\sigma(z))
 \equiv1-(\sigma(x)+\sigma(y)+\sigma(z))\pmod2.
\]
Thus the copied equation is satisfied if and only if the original
one is satisfied. Complementing all variable values is a bijection
between assignments of the two systems, so
$\OPT(F_0')=\OPT(F_0)$.

The systems have disjoint variable sets. Any assignment to their
union satisfies at most $\OPT(F_0)+\OPT(F_0')$ equations. Choosing
an optimal assignment independently in each system attains this
bound. Hence their union has $2m_0$ equations and optimum
$2\OPT(F_0)$. Every original equation is paired with one having
the opposite right-hand side, so each right-hand side occurs in
$m_0$ equations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the normalized form in Theorem~\ref{thm:xor-source}]
The gap is established in the proof of
\cite[Theorem~5.4]{Hastad2001}. The proof of Lemma~5.13 there also uses
this gap explicitly. The source can be unweighted because that proof eliminates
weights by duplicating equations. A negated Boolean literal is
$1-x$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Replacing it by $x$ and changing the
right-hand side by one preserves the equation's truth value for
every assignment. Doing this for each negation puts the equations
in the form $x+y+z=a$ without changing either source case.

We impose the additional conditions in~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}.
Call the initial system $F_0$, and let $m_0$ be its number of
equations. First delete unused variables. This does not change the
number of equations or the optimum. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:parity-balance} to pair $F_0$ with its
complemented copy.

Next repeat every equation in the union six times, and call the
resulting system $F$. An assignment either satisfies all six copies
of an equation or satisfies none. Consequently
\[
 m=12m_0,\qquad \OPT(F)=12\OPT(F_0),\qquad
 \frac{\OPT(F)}m=\frac{\OPT(F_0)}{m_0}.
\]
In particular, a YES instance of the original promise satisfies
$\OPT(F)\geq(1-\epsilon)m$, and a NO instance satisfies
$\OPT(F)\leq(1/2+\epsilon)m$.

Each remaining variable occurred at least once before repetition,
and its final occurrence count is six times a positive integer.
Thus $d_x\in6\mathbb Z_{>0}$. Counting the three variable
occurrences in every equation gives $\sum_xd_x=3m$.
The repetition creates $6m_0=m/2$ equations with each right-hand
side, so the required balance also holds and $m$ is even. These
operations increase the number of equations by the fixed factor
twelve and therefore take polynomial time. This proves all the
conditions in~\eqref{eq:xor-normalization}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pairwise covers of the parity witness graph}

The independent-set identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:xor-cover} also bounds
the vertex-cover number on each pair of parts of $G_F$.

\begin{lemma}[Pairwise covers]\label{lem:xor-pair-covers}
For distinct parts $X,Y$ of $G_F$,
$\vc(G_F[X\cup Y])\ge7m/2-\OPT(F)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each union of two parts has $6m$ vertices by
\eqref{eq:xor-equal-parts}. Fix any two distinct parts $X,Y$.
Every independent set in $G_F[X\cup Y]$ is also independent in
$G_F$: an induced subgraph has the same edges between
its vertices as the whole graph. Hence
$\alphaI(G_F[X\cup Y])\leq\alphaI(G_F)$.
By the independent-set identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:xor-cover},
$\alphaI(G_F)=5m/2+\OPT(F)$. Complementing an independent set
within the $6m$ vertices of $G_F[X\cup Y]$ therefore gives
\[
 \vc(G_F[X\cup Y])
 =6m-\alphaI(G_F[X\cup Y])
 \geq6m-\alphaI(G_F)=\frac{7m}{2}-\OPT(F).
\]
\end{proof}
