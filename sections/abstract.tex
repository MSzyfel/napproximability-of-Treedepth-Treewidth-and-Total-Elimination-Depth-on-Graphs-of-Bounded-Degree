\begin{abstract}
We provide inapproximability results for treedepth, treewidth, and
total elimination depth. Our lower bounds improve upon previous results in several ways. First, we obtain stronger inapproximability factors for each parameter. In particular treedepth is NP-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor below $18/17$, and treewidth within every fixed factor
below $12/11$. Under the Unique Games Conjecture (UGC), both
parameters are hard within every fixed factor below $4/3$. For total
elimination depth, the corresponding UGC threshold is $8/7$, and we
also prove NP-hardness within some absolute factor greater than one, excluding a PTAS. 
Under the stronger Small Set Expansion Hypothesis (SSEH), we also exclude any constant factor approximation for treedepth and total elimination depth (a similar result was previously known only for treewidth). Moreover, we show that our constructions can be transferred to bounded-degree instances. In particular, we show that for any approximation factor excluded by our results, there exist a degree bounded only by a constant depending on the factor, such that approximating a given parameter within that factor remains hard. The latter result is a non-trivial extensions, since known approximation hardness constructions, including our reductions use dense gadgets including cliques and bi-cliques to enforce certain structural properties.  In order to sparsify the resulting graphs, we show that we can replace every such gadget with an appropriate expander, which can be chosen so that the introduced cost distortion is small enough so that it does not affect the overall hardness guarantees.


Our treedepth and total elimination depth bounds also imply approximation hardness for trivially perfect completion by
number of added edges, vertex integrity, fracture number, starwidth,
average and maximum general customizable-label size, and average
hierarchical customizable-label size. The linear treewidth gaps
give explicit exponential approximation lower bounds for the total
state space of binary junction trees, measured by the sum of the table
sizes over all bags.
\end{abstract}
