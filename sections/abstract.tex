\begin{abstract}
We provide approximation lower bounds for treedepth, treewidth, and
total elimination depth on simple unweighted graphs of bounded
maximum degree. Treedepth is NP-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor below $18/17$, and treewidth within every fixed factor
below $12/11$. Under the ordinary Unique Games Conjecture (UGC), both
parameters are hard within every fixed factor below $4/3$. For total
elimination depth, the corresponding UGC threshold is $8/7$, and we
also prove NP-hardness within some absolute factor greater than one.
Under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis (SSEH), every fixed constant factor
is hard for treedepth and total elimination depth. Our line-graph
construction also gives bounded-degree treewidth instances for the
known SSE hardness at every fixed factor. In each result, the maximum
degree depends only on the excluded factor, and the reductions are
deterministic. The treedepth result resolves the conjecture that
computing unweighted treedepth remains NP-hard at bounded degree.

Our proofs use several structural ideas. For NP-hardness, we refine
the truth-block and anchor construction of Bonnet, Neuen, and
Soko\l owski using a parity source gap
~\cite{BNS,HazanSafraSchwartz2003}. For treedepth under UGC, copies
of the source graph form clique layers: a large independent set gives
a shallow elimination forest, while the first branch of any forest
limits how much height branching can save. For total depth, we also
count the depth shifts of all branches, and a quadratic inequality
bounds their total saving. For treewidth, replication of an equal-part
tripartite graph forces a large vertex cover into one bag of every
tree decomposition, while leaving the YES-case overhead unamplified.
Under SSEH, vertices of a line graph represent source
edges. A small separator would yield a balanced cut with small
boundary in the source graph. Finally, separate expander replacements
lower the degrees while preserving the NO bounds after deleting an
arbitrarily small fixed fraction of vertices. Since they delete edges,
the YES constructions remain valid.

These bounds also imply hardness for trivially perfect completion by
number of added edges, vertex integrity, fracture number, starwidth,
average and maximum general customizable-label size, and average
hierarchical customizable-label size. The linear treewidth gaps
give explicit exponential approximation lower bounds for the total
state space of binary junction trees, measured by the sum of the table
sizes over all bags.
\end{abstract}
