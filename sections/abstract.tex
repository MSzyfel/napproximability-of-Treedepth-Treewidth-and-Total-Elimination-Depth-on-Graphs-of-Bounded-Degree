\begin{abstract}
We prove approximation hardness for treedepth, treewidth, and total
elimination depth. Unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$, treedepth is hard
to approximate within every fixed factor below $18/17$, treewidth below
$12/11$, and total elimination depth below $63/62$.
Under the ordinary Unique Games Conjecture, the corresponding
thresholds are $4/3$, $4/3$, and $8/7$. Under the Small Set Expansion
Hypothesis, every fixed constant factor is hard for all three parameters.
These results hold on simple unweighted graphs of bounded maximum degree,
with the degree bound depending only on the target factor. In particular,
the treedepth result resolves a conjecture of Schirrmacher, Siebertz, and
Vigny.

The three NP-hardness reductions reuse one bounded-occurrence parity
constraint gap. The other reductions use independent-set and vertex-cover
gaps and an SSE partition gap. To bound the degree, we replace clique
and biclique factors by bounded-degree graphs built from expanders. A small
exceptional set lets us extend sparse solutions to the original graph
with additive cost below the hardness gap. Since these replacements delete
edges, the completeness bounds remain
valid. The general sparsification theorem does not require balanced
biclique sides.
Our results also imply hardness for trivially perfect completion, vertex
integrity, fracture number, starwidth, average and maximum general
customizable-label size, and average hierarchical customizable-label size.
For hierarchical customizable labels and upward vertex search spaces
in customizable contraction hierarchies,
the minimum total size equals total elimination depth, so their total and
average sizes inherit all three hardness ranges as well.
The treewidth gaps give exponential approximation lower bounds for the
total table size of binary junction trees.
\end{abstract}
