\subsection{Sparse replacements on prescribed vertex sets}
\label{app:factor-expanders}

Fix $\zeta>0$ and $\kappa\ge1$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-expanders}.
We map the vertices of a powered expander onto the prescribed sets
and verify the degree, no-hole, and connectedness guarantees.

Put $\lambda_0=5\sqrt2/8<1$. Fix an integer $k\ge1$ with
$\lambda_0^k<\zeta/4$, and set
$d_{\zeta,\kappa}=\lceil4\kappa\rceil8^k$. For each square order
$M=\nu^2$, let $\mathcal E$ be the $k$th walk power of the graph
in Theorem~\ref{thm:auxiliary-expander-family}, with degree $d=8^k$.
Its nonconstant eigenvalue bound satisfies
$(5\sqrt2)^k=8^k(5\sqrt2/8)^k=d\lambda_0^k$.
Substituting this bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:expander-mixing} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-expander-mixing}
 e_{\mathcal E}(S,T)\ge\frac{d|S||T|}{M}
       -d\lambda_0^k\sqrt{|S||T|}
 =d\sqrt{|S||T|}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|S||T|}}{M}-\lambda_0^k\right).
\end{equation}
If $|S|,|T|\ge t>\lambda_0^kM$, then
$\sqrt{|S||T|}/M\ge t/M>\lambda_0^k$, so the right-hand side is
positive. Hence a step joins $S$ to $T$.

The auxiliary graphs have square orders. A clique support can have
any size, and a biclique
requires two prescribed sides. We accommodate these sizes by
grouping auxiliary vertices and assigning each group to one
prescribed vertex. Each auxiliary step whose assigned vertices
differ gives an edge between them. The size of a group controls
the degree of its image vertex.

For a map $\pi:V(\mathcal E)\to U$ and a subset $X\subseteq U$,
the \emph{preimage} is
$\pi^{-1}(X)=\{x\in V(\mathcal E):\pi(x)\in X\}$.
The map is \emph{onto} if every vertex of $U$ has at least one
preimage. When $|U|=n\le M$, partition the $M$ auxiliary vertices
into $n$ groups. Write $M=n\lfloor M/n\rfloor+r$, where
$0\leq r<n$. Give $r$ groups size $\lfloor M/n\rfloor+1$ and the
other groups size $\lfloor M/n\rfloor$. Since $M\geq n$, every group
is nonempty. Assign one group to each vertex of $U$. This gives
$1\le|\pi^{-1}(\{u\})|\le\lceil M/n\rceil$ for every $u\in U$.

These maps preserve the required edge condition. For disjoint
output sets, a step between their preimages gives an output edge.
Since the maps are onto, $|\pi^{-1}(X)|\ge|X|$ and
$|\pi^{-1}(Y)|\ge|Y|$. For a biclique, use the separate maps for
its two sides. The mixing bound therefore applies at the same
threshold.

Use the following two constructions.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item\label{constr:one-set-replacement} \emph{One prescribed set.}
 Let $U$ have size $n\ge1$, put $M=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2$, and
 take the $M$-vertex graph $\mathcal E$ above. Choose an onto map
 $\pi:V(\mathcal E)\to U$ with the group sizes just specified.
 The replacement has vertex set $U$. Distinct vertices $u,v\in U$
 are adjacent if and only if some step $xy$ of $\mathcal E$ has
 $\pi(x)=u$ and $\pi(y)=v$. Steps whose endpoints have the same
 image give no edge, and repeated images give a single edge.
 \item\label{constr:two-side-replacement} \emph{Two prescribed sides.}
 Let $A,B$ be disjoint sets of sizes $n_A,n_B\ge1$, put
 $M=\lceil\sqrt{\max\{n_A,n_B\}}\rceil^2$, and take the
 $M$-vertex graph $\mathcal E$ above. Choose onto maps
 $\pi_A:V(\mathcal E)\to A$ and
 $\pi_B:V(\mathcal E)\to B$, with preimage sizes
 $\lfloor M/n_A\rfloor$ or $\lceil M/n_A\rceil$ for the first
 map, and $\lfloor M/n_B\rfloor$ or $\lceil M/n_B\rceil$ for
 the second. The replacement has vertex set $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$.
 Its edges are the pairs $\pi_A(x)\pi_B(y)$ arising from steps
 $xy$ of $\mathcal E$, keeping one edge for each distinct pair.
 There are no edges within either side. The two maps have the same
 domain, and their preimage groups may differ.
\end{enumerate}

A walk is \emph{closed} if its first and last vertices coincide.

\begin{restatedlemma}{Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-expanders}}
For every fixed $\zeta>0$ and $\kappa\geq1$, there is a constant
$d_{\zeta,\kappa}$ such that the following connected simple graphs,
each of maximum degree at most $d_{\zeta,\kappa}$, can be constructed
deterministically in polynomial time.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For any specified nonempty finite set $U$, there is a graph on
 $U$ in which every two disjoint sets $X,Y\subseteq U$ with
 $|X|,|Y|\geq\lceil\zeta|U|\rceil$ have an edge between them.
 \item For any specified disjoint nonempty finite sets $A,B$ with
 $\max\{|A|,|B|\}/\min\{|A|,|B|\}\leq\kappa$, there is a bipartite
 graph on $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ with parts $A,B$ in which every
 $X\subseteq A$, $Y\subseteq B$ with
 $|X|,|Y|\geq\lceil\zeta(|A|+|B|)\rceil$ have an edge between them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{restatedlemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-expanders}]
We first bound degrees. In the one-set construction, every neighbor
of $u\in U$ is the image of a step starting at a vertex of
$\pi^{-1}(\{u\})$. Each such auxiliary vertex starts $d=8^k$
steps. Distinct steps may give the same output neighbor, so
$\deg(u)\le d|\pi^{-1}(\{u\})|\le8^k\lceil M/n\rceil$.
For $u\in A$ in the bipartite construction, the same argument uses
outgoing steps from $\pi_A^{-1}(\{u\})$, giving
$\deg(u)\le8^k\lceil M/n_A\rceil$.
For $v\in B$, use incoming steps at
$\pi_B^{-1}(\{v\})$. Each auxiliary vertex has $d$ incoming steps
because its adjacency matrix is symmetric and has row sum $d$.
Thus $\deg(v)\le8^k\lceil M/n_B\rceil$.

For the one-set construction,
$M=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2\le(\sqrt n+1)^2\le4n$, since
$4n-(\sqrt n+1)^2=(3\sqrt n+1)(\sqrt n-1)\ge0$ for $n\ge1$.
Hence $\lceil M/n\rceil\le4$. For the bipartite construction, the
same estimate gives $M\le4\max\{n_A,n_B\}$. The side-size ratio
is at most $\kappa$, so
$M/n_A,M/n_B\le4\kappa$. Thus both constructions have maximum
degree at most $\lceil4\kappa\rceil8^k=d_{\zeta,\kappa}$.

We next verify the no-hole conditions at the thresholds in the
lemma. In the one-set case, put $t=\lceil\zeta n\rceil$. Then
$\lambda_0^kM<(\zeta/4)M\le\zeta n\le t$.
In the bipartite case, put $t=\lceil\zeta(n_A+n_B)\rceil$. Then
$\lambda_0^kM<(\zeta/4)M\le\zeta\max\{n_A,n_B\}
\le\zeta(n_A+n_B)\le t$.
It therefore suffices in each case to apply the mixing bound to
sets of size $t$. If no such admissible sets exist, the corresponding
no-hole condition is vacuous. Otherwise, any larger admissible sets
contain admissible $t$-element subsets.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For the one-set construction, take disjoint sets
 $X,Y\subseteq U$ with $|X|=|Y|=t$, and put
 $S=\pi^{-1}(X)$ and $T=\pi^{-1}(Y)$.
 Since $\pi$ is onto, each vertex of $X$ and $Y$ has a preimage,
 so $|S|\ge|X|=t$ and $|T|\ge|Y|=t$.
 The preimages are disjoint because $X,Y$ are disjoint and $\pi$
 assigns only one image to each auxiliary vertex.
 Equation~\eqref{eq:factor-expander-mixing} gives a step from $S$
 to $T$. Its endpoints map to a vertex of $X$ and a vertex of
 $Y$, which are distinct, so their pair is an output edge.
 \item For the bipartite construction, take
 $X\subseteq A$ and $Y\subseteq B$ with $|X|=|Y|=t$, and put
 $S=\pi_A^{-1}(X)$ and $T=\pi_B^{-1}(Y)$.
 Again, $|S|,|T|\ge t$ because both maps are onto, and
 \eqref{eq:factor-expander-mixing} gives a step $xy$ with
 $x\in S$ and $y\in T$. Its image
 $\pi_A(x)\pi_B(y)$ is an edge between $X$ and $Y$.
 Here $S,T$ may overlap, since they use different maps.
 Even a loop step $x=y$ gives a valid output edge, because its
 two images lie in the disjoint sets $A,B$.
\end{enumerate}

It remains to prove connectedness.
For a $d$-regular undirected graph, the eigenvalue $d$ has
multiplicity equal to the number of connected components, and
$-d$ is an eigenvalue if and only if some component is bipartite
\cite[Section~2.3]{HLW2006}. These criteria also apply to our
multigraph adjacency matrix, which counts parallel edges and loops
as steps. The nonconstant eigenvalue bound $d\lambda_0^k<d$
therefore makes $d$ simple and excludes $-d$, so $\mathcal E$ is
connected and nonbipartite.

In the one-set construction, applying $\pi$ to an auxiliary walk
gives a sequence of equal or adjacent output vertices. Deleting
consecutive repetitions gives an output walk. Connectedness of
$\mathcal E$ and surjectivity of $\pi$ thus prove connectedness of
the output.

For the bipartite construction, each step switches sides, so we
need auxiliary walks of both parities. Since $\mathcal E$ is
nonbipartite, it contains an odd cycle, allowing a loop as a cycle
of length one. From any vertex $x$, follow a path to this cycle,
traverse it, and return along the reverse path. This is an odd
closed walk. Prepending it to any walk from $x$ to $y$ changes
the length parity, so both parities are available. Map a walk
$x=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_\ell=y$ to the alternating sequence
$\pi_A(x_0),\pi_B(x_1),\pi_A(x_2),\ldots$ in the output.
Each consecutive pair is an edge by construction, using the
reverse auxiliary step when the sequence goes from $B$ to $A$.
An even-length walk reaches $\pi_A(y)$, and an odd-length walk
reaches $\pi_B(y)$. Thus $\pi_A(x)$ is connected to both images of
every $y$, and surjectivity gives connectedness.

Both outputs are simple by their edge definitions.
The auxiliary order $M$ is at most four times the largest prescribed
set size. The initial eight-regular graph is constructible in
polynomial time, and its $k$th walk power has degree $8^k$. Since
$k$ depends only on $\zeta$, constructing this power and mapping
its steps takes polynomial time for the fixed parameters.
\end{proof}
