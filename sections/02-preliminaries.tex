\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

All graphs produced by our reductions are finite, simple, and
unweighted. Source graphs may have weights, parallel edges, or loops
when explicitly stated. An \emph{independent set} contains no adjacent
pair. A \emph{clique} has every pair of distinct vertices adjacent.
A \emph{vertex cover} meets every edge. For an unweighted graph $G$,
let $\alphaI(G)$ denote the maximum independent-set size and
$\vc(G)$ the minimum vertex-cover size.
Taking complements gives $\vc(G)=|V(G)|-\alphaI(G)$.
We write $G[U]$ for the subgraph induced by $U$, $G-U$ for
$G[V(G)\setminus U]$, $\Delta(G)$ for maximum degree, and
$[q]=\{1,\ldots,q\}$. A spanning subgraph retains all vertices
and some of the edges. A \emph{matching} is a set of edges with
pairwise disjoint endpoints. A matching is \emph{perfect} if it meets
every vertex. A graph is \emph{cubic} if every
vertex has degree three.

For $S\subseteq V(G)$, the \emph{edge boundary} $\cut_G(S)$ consists
of the edges with one endpoint in $S$ and the other in
$V(G)\setminus S$. In a multigraph, it is a multiset, counting
parallel edge occurrences separately.

In a rooted forest, two vertices are \emph{comparable} if one is
an ancestor of the other. We count a vertex as its own ancestor
and descendant. A \emph{proper} ancestor is a different vertex.
The subtree rooted at a vertex consists of that vertex and all
its descendants. The path from the root of a component to one of its
vertices is an \emph{ancestor path}; we also allow the empty path.
The \emph{restriction} of a forest to a vertex subset deletes all
other vertices and attaches each survivor to its nearest surviving
proper ancestor, or makes it a root if none remains. It preserves
the ancestor order between survivors and never increases their depths.

An \emph{elimination forest} $F$ of a graph $H$ is a rooted forest on
$V(H)$ such that the endpoints of every edge of $H$ are comparable in
the ancestor order. Its height $h(F)$ is the maximum number of vertices on a
root-to-leaf path, with $h(\varnothing)=0$. The \emph{treedepth}
$\td(H)$ is the minimum height of an elimination forest of $H$.
We set $\td(\varnothing)=0$. For
disconnected graphs, treedepth is the maximum treedepth of a component.

The \emph{total depth} of an elimination forest $F$ is
$\totd(F)=\sum_{v\in V(F)}\operatorname{depth}_F(v)$, with roots at
depth one. Let $\totd(G)$ be the minimum total depth of an
elimination forest of $G$. This objective is also called
\emph{VPT-sum}~\cite{HogemoEtAl2021}. For disconnected graphs,
$\totd$ is the sum of its values on the components. Restricting a forest
to each component proves one inequality, and their disjoint union
proves the other.

A \emph{tree decomposition} of $H$ consists of a tree $T$ and bags
$B_t\subseteq V(H)$ for $t\in V(T)$. For each $v\in V(H)$, write
$T_v=\{t\in V(T):v\in B_t\}$ for the set of nodes whose bags contain
$v$. We require each $T_v$ to be nonempty and to induce a connected
subtree of $T$, and $T_u\cap T_v\ne\varnothing$ for every edge
$uv\in E(H)$. Its width is
$\max_t|B_t|-1$, and the \emph{treewidth} $\operatorname{tw}(H)$ is
the minimum such width.

\subsection{Additive errors in hardness reductions}

The following observation lets us absorb small transformation errors
without repeating the gap comparison. Here $\OPT(I)$ denotes the
optimum of a minimization instance $I$.

\begin{lemma}[Preservation of approximation gaps]
\label{lem:gap-preservation}
Let $I\mapsto I'$ be a polynomial-time transformation between
minimization problems. Suppose that, for fixed $a>0$ and
$0\le\epsilon<1$, it satisfies
\[
 |\OPT(I')-a\OPT(I)|\le\epsilon aL(I),
 \qquad 0<L(I)\le\OPT(I).
\]
A promise with YES upper bound $Y>0$ and NO lower bound $r'Y$,
where $r'>1$ and $Y$ may depend on the input size, then has
transformed bounds $a(1+\epsilon)Y$ and $a(1-\epsilon)r'Y$.
Thus every fixed $1\le r<r'$ is preserved when
$\epsilon<(r'-r)/(r'+r)$.

More generally, suppose the transformation is known to have additive
error at most $\epsilon aY$ on both promise cases, without a lower
bound $L(I)\le\OPT(I)$. Then the transformed YES and NO bounds are
$a(1+\epsilon)Y$ and $a(r'-\epsilon)Y$. Their gap has factor greater
than $r$ whenever
\[
 \epsilon<\frac{r'-r}{r+1},\qquad
 a(r'-\epsilon)Y-r a(1+\epsilon)Y
 =aY\bigl(r'-r-(r+1)\epsilon\bigr)>0.
\]
In particular, an additive error $o(aY)$ is harmless on all
sufficiently large promised instances.

If, in addition, every solution of $I'$ of cost $z$ can be converted
in polynomial time into one of $I$ of cost at most
$z/a+\epsilon L(I)$, then an $r$-approximation for $I'$ gives an
$(r+(r+1)\epsilon)$-approximation for $I$. If
$\OPT(I')\le a\OPT(I)$, the latter factor improves to $r+\epsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The additive bound and $L(I)\le\OPT(I)$ give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \OPT(I')&\ge a\OPT(I)-\epsilon aL(I)\ge a(1-\epsilon)\OPT(I),\\
 \OPT(I')&\le a\OPT(I)+\epsilon aL(I)\le a(1+\epsilon)\OPT(I).
 \end{aligned}
\]
These inequalities give the stated YES and NO bounds. Their ratio is
$r'(1-\epsilon)/(1+\epsilon)$. It exceeds $r$ if and only if
$r(1+\epsilon)<r'(1-\epsilon)$, or
$\epsilon<(r'-r)/(r'+r)$. For the solution claim,
an $r$-approximate solution has $z\le r\OPT(I')$, so its conversion
has cost at most
\[
 \frac za+\epsilon L(I)
 \le\frac{r\OPT(I')}{a}+\epsilon L(I)
 \le r\OPT(I)+(r+1)\epsilon L(I)
 \le\bigl(r+(r+1)\epsilon\bigr)\OPT(I).
\]
When $\OPT(I')\le a\OPT(I)$, use $z/a\le r\OPT(I)$ to bound the
conversion cost by
$r\OPT(I)+\epsilon L(I)\le(r+\epsilon)\OPT(I)$.
Under the alternative additive-error hypothesis, YES values increase
by at most $\epsilon aY$ and NO values decrease by at most the same
amount, giving the displayed transformed bounds and gap condition.
\end{proof}

An additive error of order $o(L(I))$ is at most
$\epsilon aL(I)$ on all sufficiently large instances, for any
fixed $a,\epsilon>0$. Thus the lemma also applies when its
hypotheses hold only above a fixed
size, provided the source promise remains hard under that
restriction. Each application below verifies this condition.
