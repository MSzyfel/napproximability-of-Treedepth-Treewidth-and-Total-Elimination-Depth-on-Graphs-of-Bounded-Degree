\title{Inapproximability of Treedepth, Treewidth, and\\
Total Elimination Depth on Graphs of Bounded Degree}

\begin{abstract}
We prove approximation lower bounds for treedepth, treewidth, and
total elimination depth on simple unweighted graphs of bounded
maximum degree. Treedepth is NP-hard to approximate within every
fixed factor below $18/17$, and treewidth within every fixed factor
below $12/11$. Under the ordinary Unique Games Conjecture, both
parameters are hard within every fixed factor below $4/3$. For total
elimination depth, the corresponding UGC threshold is $8/7$, and we
also prove NP-hardness within some absolute factor greater than one.
Under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis, every fixed constant factor
is hard for treedepth and total elimination depth. Our line-graph
construction also gives bounded-degree treewidth instances for the
known all-constant SSE hardness. In each result, the maximum degree
depends only on the excluded factor, and the reductions are
deterministic. The treedepth result resolves the conjecture that
computing unweighted treedepth remains NP-hard at bounded degree.

For NP-hardness of treedepth, we refine the tripartite witness and
anchor construction of Bonnet, Neuen, and Soko\l owski. Under UGC,
clique layers give the treedepth gap and a quadratic branching
estimate gives the total-depth gap. A replication argument transfers
tripartite vertex-cover gaps to treewidth. Under SSEH, a line graph
converts source cuts into balanced-separator lower bounds. To obtain
bounded degree, we prove separate sparsification theorems for the
three parameters. Expander replacements preserve the NO bounds after
deleting an arbitrarily small fixed fraction of the vertices, while
edge deletion preserves the YES upper bounds.

These bounds also imply hardness for trivially perfect completion by
number of added edges, vertex integrity, fracture number, starwidth,
and average and maximum customizable-label size. The linear treewidth gaps
give explicit exponential approximation lower bounds for the total
state space of binary junction trees. The paper also records which
of these consequences follow from existing reductions and which
require new arguments.
\end{abstract}
